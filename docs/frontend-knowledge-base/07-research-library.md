## F. How to find more free US academic research (search recipes)

1. **PubMed Central (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov):** search `"eye tracking" AND ("web" OR "website" OR "user interface")`, `"cognitive load" AND "interface"`, `"web accessibility" AND "screen reader"`, `"usability" AND "older adults" AND "website"`. Filter to "Free full text."
2. **PubMed:** same terms; use the "Free full text" filter; follow "Similar articles."
3. **ERIC (ies.ed.gov):** education-research database with many free articles on cognitive load and multimedia learning; US Department of Education.
4. **NIH, NEI, NIDCD, NIA sites:** vision, hearing, and aging resources that inform accessibility.
5. **CDC:** Disability and Health Data System.
6. **DTIC (Defense Technical Information Center), NASA NTRS (ntrs.nasa.gov):** human factors and workload research including NASA-TLX origins; free.
7. **Digital.gov, usability.gov (archived at HHS), 18F:** federal UX guidance and case studies.
8. **University repositories and preprints:** arXiv (Cornell, US) `cs.HC`; ACM Digital Library "Open Access" filter; many authors post free PDFs (e.g., University of Washington Makeability Lab).
9. **Google Scholar:** use "All versions" to find free copies; add `filetype:pdf site:.edu`.
10. **Unpaywall browser extension** surfaces legal free copies.
11. **Federal Register and eCFR** for regulations; **regulations.gov** for public comments, which often cite research and user data.

Suggested next reads (not yet ingested): ACM CHI/ASSETS open-access accessibility papers, W3C WAI "Understanding" documents per criterion, NN/g articles on mobile eye tracking, HHS guidelines chapters 3, 6, 7, 11, 13 and 17.

## G. Skeptical notes

- **Vendor research:** NN/g sells reports; I used free articles and flagged paid components. Sample sizes are modest for some studies and participants are mostly US adults.
- **Dated work:** several studies are 2006-2009 (F-pattern, inline validation, thumb targets, Way Edges). Behavior may persist (NN/g argues it does), but modern devices and conventions differ. Retest on your audience.
- **Small samples:** 16-32 participants for several peer-reviewed studies; effects may not generalize.
- **Conflicts noted:** progress bars vs skeletons; narrow lines and dyslexia; F-pattern universality; 7 vs 4 working memory.
- **Secondary sources:** litigation counts and overlay claims come from secondary summaries (grade C/D); verify before citing in legal or business documents.
- **Eye tracking** measures gaze, not understanding.
- **I did not verify** all links resolve to free full text beyond what search results showed.
