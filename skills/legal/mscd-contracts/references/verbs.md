# Verb structures: shall, will, may, must and the categories of contract language

Read this file when classifying sentences (pass 3), when choosing a verb in a redline or draft, and whenever a finding involves *shall*, *will*, *may*, *must*, *hereby*, *states*, *acknowledges* or a condition.

## Procedure for every sentence in the body

1. **Choose the category first, then the verb** (3.5). The categories: agreement, performance, obligation, discretion, prohibition, policy, declaration, intention, recommendation, and the three ways to express a condition.
2. **Run the *has a duty* test on every *shall*** (3.89-3.92): substitute *has a duty to*. If the sentence stops making sense, *shall* is wrong; pick the verb the true category calls for.
3. **Check the subject.** *shall* imposes a duty only on the subject, and only when the subject is a party. For a duty on anyone else, restructure first (active voice, or *shall cause* for a party's instrumentality); use *must* only if restructuring fails (3.154-3.166).
4. **Never pair *shall* with a negative subject** (house rule; 3.324). *Neither party may assign*; *Acme shall not assign*.
5. **Active voice** (house rule): every recast keeps the actor as the subject.

## Quick reference (MSCD5 Figure 1 and the MSCD5 Quick Reference, with riders applied)

| Category | Verb structure | Use it for | Example | MSCD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agreement | *agree* | The lead-in only | *The parties therefore agree as follows:* | 3.45-3.47 |
| Performance | *hereby* + simple present | An act done by signing | *Acme hereby sells the Units to Widgetco.* | 3.48-3.50 |
| Policy (no action on signing) | present continuous, negative | Saying a party isn't taking an action on signing; don't retool *hereby* performance into the negative | *In this agreement Widgetco is not assuming any Excluded Liabilities.* | 3.67 (language of policy, 3.332) |
| Obligation on the subject | *shall* | A duty on the subject, a party | *Acme shall deliver the Units to Widgetco.* | 3.89-3.92 |
| Obligation on the subject | *shall cause* | Subject is a party; the object isn't a legal person, or is the party's instrumentality | *Parent shall cause Sub to transfer the Assets.* | 3.160-3.165 |
| Obligation on someone else | *must* | Subject isn't a legal person, or is a nonparty (only if restructuring fails) | *The auditor must deliver its report to Widgetco no later than 30 days after the Closing.* | 3.149-3.157 |
| Discretion | *may* | A choice the contract grants | *Lee may transfer the Shares to Kim.* | 3.205-3.207 |
| Absence of obligation | *is not required to* | No duty | *Acme is not required to repair the Equipment.* | 3.292 |
| Absence of prohibition (rider) | *The parties acknowledge that nothing in this agreement prohibits* | Silence of the contract, stated as a shared fact | *The parties acknowledge that nothing in this agreement prohibits Acme from selling the Widgets.* | 3.304, superseded by the 19 Nov 2025 rider |
| Prohibition | *shall not* (positive subject); *may* with a negative subject | Forbidding conduct | *Customer shall not alter the Software.* *Neither party may assign this agreement.* | 3.305; 3.323-3.325 |
| Policy | simple present | Rules in effect on signing, or taking effect at a stated time | *Tennessee law governs this agreement.* | 3.334 |
| Policy | *will* | Uncertain future events | *Any purported transfer in breach of this agreement will be void.* | 3.334 |
| Condition, conditional clause | simple present in the *if* clause; *will* in the matrix clause where it would otherwise be present | An uncertain future event that triggers a consequence | *If Jones leaves Company, the Option will lapse.* | 3.352-3.362 |
| Condition, policy | *It is a condition to ... that* | One condition, or a list of *that* clauses | *It is a condition to Widgetco's obligation to close that Acme has delivered the Consent.* | 3.393-3.398 (prefer this to *is conditioned upon*) |
| Condition, obligation | *must* after *To* + infinitive or a *for* clause | Conditions a party must meet to get something | *To receive reimbursement, Acme must submit a claim to Widgetco ... .* | 3.386-3.391 |
| Declaration | *states* | Facts the declaring party knows | *Acme states that Schedule A lists all the Equipment.* | 3.457-3.458 |
| Declaration | *acknowledges* | Facts the other party offers | *Widgetco acknowledges that Acme is a consultant.* | 3.485-3.490 |
| Declaration (elaboration) | *states that it may* / *must* | A party's ability or requirement that exists apart from the contract | *Acme states that it may sell the Widgets.* | Nov 2025 blog; 3.208-3.211 |
| Intention | *The parties intend that ... will* | Status that depends on later circumstances | *The parties intend that Consultant will be an independent contractor.* | 3.491-3.500 (Adams's "state of mind" proposal noted only) |
| Recommendation | *recommends* (or *encourages*, *reminds*) + *that* | A stronger party pointing something out | *Company recommends that Participant consult a legal adviser.* | 3.502-3.505 |

Examples are adapted from MSCD5's quick reference and restated in house style (active voice, digits, no *within*). The lint confirms that none is passive.

## Riders on verbs (verified against the posts)

- **Absence of prohibition** becomes an acknowledgment (above).
- ***once* and *when* don't introduce a condition**: use *if*; or delete *once* where an adjective phrase remains (*a Redemption Notice once submitted*). Both words "suggest an expectation that the circumstance in question will come to pass."
- **A party's ability outside the contract** is a statement of fact: *Acme states that it may ...*.

### Chapter 3: Language of Obligation

- **R-3.92-has-a-duty-test** [sev 1] Apply the has a duty test to every shall: replace shall with has (or have) a duty to and check that the provision still makes sense and imposes a duty on a party that is the subject. Shall with an inanimate subject fails at once. (3.5, 3.92, 3.120...; PDF p. 150)
  - Book example: The rent shall be paid by the Tenant on the first of every month. [fails: abstract-noun subject] → The Tenant shall pay the rent on the first day of every month.
  - Book example: Jones shall be entitled to have her expenses reimbursed promptly. [fails] → Acme shall promptly reimburse Jones's expenses.
- **R-3.87-obligation-definition** [sev 3] Use language of obligation to state any duty the contract imposes on a party; it comes in two structures: obligation imposed on the subject of the sentence and obligation imposed on someone other than the subject. It is also one way to express a condition. (3.87, 3.88; PDF p. 149)
- **R-3.89-shall-duty** [sev 1] Use shall only to impose a duty on the subject of the sentence, when the subject is a party; don't use shall to express any other meaning. (3.89, 3.90, 3.91...; PDF p. 149)
  - Look for: shall + verb in any sentence
  - Test: Substitute 'has a duty to' for 'shall'. If the result doesn't make sense, shall is misused (3.92).
  - Fix: Use the verb the category calls for: must for a duty on someone other than the subject, may for discretion, the present tense or will for policy, the present tense in conditional and relative clauses, must with a to-clause for a condition.
  - Book example: (none) → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase the Shares from Hughes.
- **R-3.98-shall-future-time** [sev 3] Don't use shall to mark future time where standard English would use the simple present (This agreement shall terminate ...; shall be governed by ...). On the other side's draft, such benign overuse rarely confuses and can be let pass. (3.94, 3.95, 3.96...; PDF p. 151)
  - Test: Has a duty test (3.92).
- **R-3.100-discipline-not-eliminate** [sev 2] Address overuse of shall through disciplined use (shall only for a duty on the subject), not by eliminating shall; replacing shall with must or will doesn't fix the only routine dispute (obligation versus condition) and spreads the multiple-meaning problem. (3.100, 3.101, 3.102...; PDF p. 151)
- **R-3.107-no-must-for-subject** [sev 2] Don't use must for an obligation imposed on a party that is the subject; must is needed for obligations on others and for conditions, and muddying obligations and conditions is particularly unhelpful. (3.106, 3.107; PDF p. 151)
  - Exceptions: Use must with a to-infinitive or for clause to express a condition (3.386, 3.391); use must for obligations on someone other than the subject (3.154).
- **R-3.111-no-will-for-obligation** [sev 2] Don't use will to impose an obligation on the subject; will primarily expresses future time and would then do triple duty (obligation on the subject, obligation on others, future time). (3.108, 3.109, 3.110...; PDF p. 151)
- **R-3.122-aaa-clause** [sev 2] Don't use the standard AAA arbitration clause (... shall be settled by arbitration ...); it fails the has a duty test, and an obligation on the parties to arbitrate every dispute makes no sense. Use exclusive discretion: a party may demand arbitration as the exclusive means of initiating adversarial proceedings. (3.122, 3.123, 3.124...; PDF p. 160)
  - Book example: Any controversy or claim ... shall be settled by arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association. → As the exclusive means of initiating adversarial proceedings to resolve any dispute ..., a party may demand that the dispute be resolved by arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association ... .
- **R-3.125-no-agrees-to-obligation** [sev 2] Don't use agrees to to express obligations; it is clumsy, awkward for future obligations, and litigants have argued it is language of performance. (3.125, 3.126, 3.127; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1c] ××: Acme agrees to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase the Shares from Hughes.
- **R-3.128-no-promises-to** [sev 3] Don't use promises to, even in promissory notes; consistency favors shall pay. (3.128; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1d] ××: Acme promises to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase the Shares from Hughes.
- **R-3.129-no-undertakes-covenants** [sev 3] Don't use undertakes to, is bound to, hereby binds itself to, covenants to, pledges to, covenants and agrees to, commits to + gerund, or is committed to for obligations; use shall. (3.129; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1e] ××: Acme undertakes to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
  - Book example: [2-1f] ××: Acme is bound to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
- **R-3.130-no-is-obligated** [sev 2] Don't use is obligated to for obligations; it is long-winded and could suggest the obligation arises elsewhere. (3.130; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1l] ×: Acme is obligated to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
- **R-3.130-no-shall-be-obligated** [sev 3] Don't use shall be obligated to; it fails the has a duty test ('has a duty to be obligated'). (3.130; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1k] ××: Acme shall be obligated to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
- **R-3.131-no-has-a-duty-to** [sev 3] Don't use has a duty to in contract text, despite its role in the has a duty test. (3.131; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1j] ××: Acme has a duty to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
- **R-3.132-no-will-be-expected-to** [sev 2] Don't use will be expected to (common in employment agreements); it's oddly circumspect, and the consequences of an unmet expectation are unclear. (3.132; PDF p. 161)
  - Book example: [2-1n] ××: Acme will be expected to purchase ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
- **R-3.133-indicative-mood** [sev 3] Express obligations in the indicative mood, not the imperative (as the Construction Specifications Institute recommends for specifications); to avoid wordy passives, use The Contractor shall do the following: plus tabulated clauses. (3.133, 3.134, 3.135...; PDF p. 162)
  - Look for: imperative verbs stating obligations, typically in specifications
  - Exceptions: A rare legitimate contract imperative: Read before signing (5.41) (3.140).
- **R-3.141-no-simple-present-obligation** [sev 2] Don't use the simple present to express obligations (as The New Engineering Contract does); in standard English, the third-person present doesn't impose obligations. (3.141, 3.142, 3.143; PDF p. 162)
  - Look for: a party subject + simple present verb stating what it must do
  - Book example: the Contractor keeps accounts of his payments of actual cost → (see fix)
  - Book example: ... and the other party provides it. → (see fix)
- **R-3.144-no-is-responsible-for** [sev 2] Don't use is responsible for + present participle to express a duty owed to another party; it is long-winded and unclear whether it creates a duty or acknowledges one from another source. (3.144; PDF p. 162)
  - Book example: [2-1m] ××: Acme is responsible for purchasing ... . → [2-1] ✓✓: Acme shall purchase ... .
- **R-3.145-is-responsible-for-policy** [sev 3] Is responsible for can serve as language of policy to say that a party may handle as it wishes an obligation owed to a nonparty (such as taxes) but bears the resulting liabilities; an obligation to pay would have the same effect. (3.145, 3.146; PDF p. 162)
  - Book example: (none) → Acme is responsible for paying any taxes ... .
- **R-3.147-six-contexts** [sev 3] Recognize six contexts in which a sentence imposes an obligation on someone other than its subject: a passive verb (with or without a by-agent); a subject that isn't a legal person; a nonparty subject or by-agent; receive with a party subject; is entitled to with a noun or passive complement clause; and an obligation expressed as imposed by the other party or the contract. (3.147, 3.148, 3.149...; PDF p. 166)
- **R-3.148-passive-missing-actor** [sev 1] Don't express a party's obligation with a passive verb that omits the by-agent (The Consultant must be reimbursed); state who owes the duty, preferably in the active voice. (3.148, 3.158, 3.159; PDF p. 166)
  - Look for: passive obligation (must/shall/will be + past participle) with no by-agent
  - Book example: [3-2] ×: The Consultant must be reimbursed for all authorized expenses. → [3-2f] ✓✓: The Company shall reimburse the Consultant for all authorized expenses.
  - Book example: [3-2a] ××: The Consultant shall be reimbursed for all authorized expenses. → [3-2f] ✓✓: The Company shall reimburse the Consultant for all authorized expenses.
- **R-3.168-no-receive** [sev 1] Don't use receive to impose a duty on someone other than the subject (Jones shall receive a salary); like the passive, it doesn't say who pays. Use shall on the actor. (3.151, 3.168; PDF p. 172)
  - Book example: [3-7a] ××: Jones shall receive a salary of $100,000 a year. → [3-7d] ✓✓: Widgetco shall pay Jones a salary of $100,000 a year.
  - Book example: [3-7b] ××: Jones will receive a salary of $100,000 a year. → [3-7d] ✓✓: Widgetco shall pay Jones a salary of $100,000 a year.
- **R-3.169-no-is-entitled-to-obligation** [sev 2] Don't use is entitled to with a noun or a passive complement clause to express an obligation; focus on the party owing the duty. The obligor could otherwise claim it needn't perform until asked. (3.152, 3.169, 3.171; PDF p. 172)
  - Book example: [3-1c] ×: The Indemnifying Party is entitled to be notified by the Indemnified Party of any claim. → [3-1e] ✓✓: The Indemnified Party shall notify the Indemnifying Party of any claim.
  - Book example: [3-2d] ×: The Consultant is entitled to be reimbursed for all authorized expenses. → [3-2f] ✓✓: The Company shall reimburse the Consultant for all authorized expenses.
- **R-3.153-no-obligation-via-other-party** [sev 3] Don't express an obligation as imposed by the other party (hereby engages, hereby instructs, requires) or by the contract (This agreement requires); impose it directly with shall. (3.153; PDF p. 172)
  - Book example: [3-9] ××: Acme hereby engages Widgetco to deliver the following equipment to Acme ... . → [3-9d] ✓✓: Widgetco shall deliver the following equipment to Acme ... .
  - Book example: [3-9a] ××: Acme hereby instructs Widgetco to deliver the following equipment to Acme ... → [3-9d] ✓✓: Widgetco shall deliver the following equipment to Acme ... .
- **R-3.154-must-for-others** [sev 3] When a sentence must impose an obligation on someone other than its subject (passive verb, non-person subject, nonparty subject), use must, not shall or will. (3.154, 3.155, 3.156...; PDF p. 166)
  - Fix: Better still, restructure so a party is the subject (3.158).
  - Book example: [3-3a] ××: The Closing shall take place at Acme's offices. → [3-3] ✓: The Closing must take place at Acme's offices.
  - Book example: [3-3b] ×: The Closing will take place at Acme's offices. → [3-3] ✓: The Closing must take place at Acme's offices.
- **R-3.159-active-voice-obligation** [sev 3] Usually better than must: don't impose a duty on someone other than the subject; use the active voice with the obligated party as subject. (3.158, 3.159; PDF p. 166)
  - Look for: passive obligation with a party as by-agent
  - Book example: [3-1] ✓: Notice of any claim must be given by the Indemnified Party to the Indemnifying Party. → [3-1e] ✓✓: The Indemnified Party shall notify the Indemnifying Party of any claim.
- **R-3.160-shall-cause** [sev 2] State explicitly who owes the duty with shall cause, where the original subject isn't a legal person or is an instrumentality of a party (for example, a wholly owned subsidiary). (3.160, 3.161; PDF p. 170)
  - Book example: [3-4] ×: Sub must sell the Widget Assets no later than 30 days after Closing. → [3-4c] ✓✓: Parent shall cause Sub to sell the Widget Assets ... .
  - Book example: [3-4a] ××: Sub shall sell the Widget Assets ... . [Sub not a party] → [3-4c] ✓✓: Parent shall cause Sub to sell the Widget Assets ... .
- **R-3.162-no-procure-ensure-require** [sev 2] Don't use variants of shall cause: shall procure (Commonwealth, legalistic), shall ensure (implications unclear), shall require (could mean a duty to impose a duty), shall see to it that, or shall see to + abstract noun. (3.162; PDF p. 170)
  - Book example: [3-4d] ×: Parent shall procure Sub to sell the Widget Assets ... . → [3-4c] ✓✓: Parent shall cause Sub to sell the Widget Assets ... .
  - Book example: [3-4e] ×: Parent shall ensure that Sub sells the Widget Assets ... . → [3-4c] ✓✓: Parent shall cause Sub to sell the Widget Assets ... .
- **R-3.163-no-shall-cause-uncontrolled** [sev 2] Don't use shall cause (or a variant) when the original subject is a nonparty the party doesn't control, such as an employee, arbitrator, or unaffiliated contractor. (3.163, 3.164; PDF p. 170)
  - Book example: [3-6f] ×: The parties shall cause the arbitrator to issue the award no later than 20 days after the hearing. → [3-6] ✓: The arbitrator must issue the award no later than 20 days after the last day of the hearing.
- **R-3.165-restructure-nonparty** [sev 2] Where possible, restructure a provision about a nonparty's conduct to impose an obligation on a party, and state who bears the risk of the uncontrolled nonparty's conduct (for example, an obligation reflecting limited control plus a risk-allocation mechanism). (3.165, 3.173; PDF p. 170)
  - Book example: [3-5] ×: Each Acme employee must enter into a confidentiality agreement with Acme in the form of exhibit 2. → [3-5d] ✓✓: Acme shall enter into a confidentiality agreement in the form of exhibit 2 with each of its current employees ... .
  - Book example: [3-5a] ××: Each Acme employee shall enter into ... . → [3-5d] ✓✓ [as above]
- **R-3.166-must-for-nonparty** [sev 2] If you can't restructure, use must for a nonparty's obligation (for example, an arbitrator), in the active voice, and consider specifying what happens if the nonparty doesn't act, since remedies might not be evident. (3.166; PDF p. 170)
  - Book example: [3-6a] ××: The arbitrator shall issue the award no later than 20 days after the last day of the hearing. → [3-6] ✓: The arbitrator must issue the award no later than 20 days after the last day of the hearing.
  - Book example: [3-6b] ××: The arbitrator will issue the award ... . → [3-6] ✓: The arbitrator must issue the award ... .
- **R-3.167-judicious-verbs** [sev 3] Choose verbs that let a party be the subject, avoiding shall cause altogether. (3.167; PDF p. 170)
  - Book example: [3-3c] ✓: The parties shall cause the Closing to take place at Acme's offices. → [3-3d] ✓✓: The parties shall hold the Closing at Acme's offices.
- **R-3.170-no-shall-be-entitled** [sev 3] Don't write shall be entitled to; that shall fails the has a duty test (and also appears in language of policy). (3.170; PDF p. 172)
  - Book example: [3-1d] ××: The Indemnifying Party shall be entitled to be notified by the Indemnified Party of any claim. → [3-1e] ✓✓: The Indemnified Party shall notify the Indemnifying Party of any claim.
  - Book example: [3-2e] ×: The Consultant shall be entitled to be reimbursed for all authorized expenses. → [3-2f] ✓✓: The Company shall reimburse the Consultant for all authorized expenses.
- **R-3.172-feasible-obligation** [sev 2] Before imposing an obligation, decide whether it makes sense: does the party have enough control, and is compliance feasible? If not, use a reasonable efforts obligation, a condition, and a risk-allocation provision (for example, for landlord consent). (3.172, 3.173, 3.174...; PDF p. 173)
  - Look for: an obligation to obtain a nonparty's act or to achieve something outside the party's control
- **R-3.176-prevent-undesirable-events** [sev 2] To stop an undesirable event not fully within a party's control, use two provisions: a reasonable efforts obligation to take steps to prevent it, and a risk-allocation provision making the party liable for resulting losses if it happens anyway. Avoid shall prohibit (unclear how much is required) and shall prevent (liable regardless?). (3.176, 3.178, 3.179...; PDF p. 173)
- **R-3.177-shall-not-permit-ambiguous** [sev 1] Don't use shall not permit or shall not allow for events a party doesn't fully control; permit can mean merely allowing something to happen, so the extent of the duty is ambiguous. (3.177; PDF p. 173)
  - Fix: Use a reasonable efforts obligation plus risk allocation (3.176, 3.180).
- **R-3.181-only-in-obligation** [sev 3] Don't use only in an obligation that provides for the only possible means of performing; it's redundant. Keep it if other means are possible and you want to exclude them. (3.181, 3.182; PDF p. 173)
  - Fix: Delete a redundant only.
  - Book example: BioLab shall pay HealthLab only in United States dollars. [only one means] → (see fix)
  - Book example: BuildCo shall use untreated cedar timber in building the deck. [other materials possible] → BuildCo shall use only untreated cedar timber in building the deck.
- **R-3.183-no-obligation-to-comply** [sev 3] Don't impose an obligation to comply with an obligation stated elsewhere in the contract; say that the other provision applies, unless it would apply anyway. (3.183, 3.184; PDF p. 173)
  - Fix: Say that the other provision applies, or omit the sentence if it would apply anyway.
  - Book example: Upon termination ..., ABC shall comply with its obligations ... stated in article 12. → (see fix)
- **R-3.186-obligation-not-covenant** [sev 3] Use obligation, not covenant, including when grouping obligations (signing-to-closing, while debt is outstanding). (3.185, 3.186, 3.187...; PDF p. 175)
- **R-3.189-noncompetition-provision** [sev 3] Covenant not to compete is a term of art; noncompetition provision would be less fusty. (3.189; PDF p. 175)
- **R-3.190-no-affirmative-negative-covenant** [sev 3] Don't distinguish affirmative and negative covenants, and don't refer to obligations and prohibitions; obligation covers both doing and not doing. (3.190, 3.191; PDF p. 175)
- **R-3.193-obligation-or-duty** [sev 3] Choose obligation or duty and use it consistently; the choice has no implications for the transaction (the manual prefers obligation for contract duties). (3.192, 3.193; PDF p. 175)
- **R-3.194-no-adjectives-obligation** [sev 3] Don't modify obligations with irrevocable, absolute, or unconditional. (3.194; PDF p. 175)
- **R-3.195-comply-with-not-perform** [sev 3] Use comply with and compliance with for obligations, not perform, performance, discharge, or fulfill; an obligation can be a duty not to act. (3.195, 3.196; PDF p. 175)
- **R-3.199-breach-mass-noun** [sev 3] Prefer breach to violation, nonperformance, or noncompliance; use breach as a mass noun (no article) unless referring to multiple breaches. (3.197, 3.198, 3.199; PDF p. 175)
  - Book example: Any such disclosure will constitute a breach of the Recipient's obligations under this section 5.2. → Any such disclosure will constitute breach of the Recipient's obligations under this section 5.2.
- **R-3.200-breach-verb** [sev 3] Where you can, use the verb breach instead of the noun; it's more concise and keeps the actor (the mass noun can drop it). (3.200, 3.201, 3.202...; PDF p. 175)
  - Book example: will constitute breach of → will breach
  - Book example: In the event of breach of this section 6.7 ... → If Acme breaches this section 6.7 ...

### Chapter 3: General Considerations

- **R-3.10-third-person** [sev 2] In business contracts, use the third person (Acme shall purchase ...), not first or second person (we, us, our, you, your). (3.10, 3.11, 3.12; PDF p. 131)
  - Exceptions: First and second person are primarily used in consumer contracts (3.12).
  - Book example: (none) → Acme shall purchase the Shares from Ahmad.
- **R-3.27-throat-clearing** [sev 2] Eliminate throat-clearing: extraneous verb structures tacked onto the front of a sentence (Acme acknowledges that, agrees that, covenants that, represents that, It is agreed that ...). Reviewers should consider doing so too. (3.27, 3.28, 3.29...; PDF p. 134)
  - Test: Delete the introductory verb structure; if the sentence still says the same thing in its proper category, it was throat-clearing.
  - Book example: The Lead Manager hereby agrees that it undertakes that it shall: (i) submit ... → The Lead Manager shall: (i) submit ...
  - Book example: It is further agreed that the insurer undertakes that it shall not contend ... → The insurer shall not contend ...
- **R-3.31-wrong-principal-verb** [sev 3] Before deleting apparent throat-clearing, check whether it is a poorly chosen principal verb; if the sentence states a fact, use language of declaration (acknowledge) and might for possibility. (3.31, 3.32; PDF p. 134)
  - Book example: ABC and XYZ agree that each of them may obtain Confidential Information of the other. → ABC and XYZ each acknowledge that each of them might obtain Confidential Information of the other.
- **R-3.38-no-subjunctive-conditions** [sev 3] Don't use the subjunctive in ordinary conditions; it's archaic. Use the indicative. (3.34, 3.35, 3.36...; PDF p. 136)
  - Book example: If the Borrower be in default ... → If the Borrower is in default ...
  - Book example: It is a condition to closing that the Buyer have received an opinion ... → It is a condition to closing that the Buyer has received an opinion ...
- **R-3.39-keep-subjunctive-demands** [sev 3] Keep the subjunctive where the clause expresses a demand or suggestion, even inside a conditional clause (requires that Widgetco pay; recommends that the Employee retain). (3.39, 3.40; PDF p. 136)
  - Book example: If Acme requires that Widgetco pays the purchase price in Canadian dollars ... → If Acme requires that Widgetco pay the purchase price in Canadian dollars ...
  - Book example: The Company recommends that the Employee retains a lawyer ... → The Company recommends that the Employee retain a lawyer ...
- **R-3.43-consolidate-deal-points** [sev 3] Consider consolidating related deal points into one verb structure rather than a separate sentence for each. (3.43; PDF p. 138)
  - Book example: The Licensee shall make all regulatory filings. The Licensee shall decide in which countries to file for regulatory approval. → The Licensee shall make all regulatory filings in those countries in which it decides to seek Regulatory Approval for a Product.
  - Book example: Acme shall pay each invoice no later than 30 days after it receives that invoice. For Acme's payment of an invoice to be valid, Acme must pay that invoice by bank transfer. → Acme shall pay each invoice by bank transfer no later than 30 days after it receives that invoice.
- **R-3.44-different-not-stronger** [sev 3] Don't treat one category as stronger or weaker than another; avoid mere condition, mere covenant, mere obligation, and merely in such comparisons. The categories have different consequences. (3.44; PDF p. 138)

### Chapter 3: Language of Agreement

- **R-3.45-agreement-lead-in-only** [sev 2] Use language of agreement (agree) only once, in the lead-in (agree as follows); elsewhere agrees that is throat-clearing. (3.45, 3.47; PDF p. 139)
- **R-3.46-no-hereby-agreement** [sev 3] Don't use hereby in language of agreement; hereby belongs to language of performance. (3.46; PDF p. 139)

### Chapter 3: Language of Performance

- **R-3.48-performance-definition** [sev 3] Use language of performance for speech acts that accomplish an action involving a change in status by signing the contract (Acme hereby grants the License to Smith); use the present tense. (3.48, 3.49; PDF p. 140)
  - Book example: (none) → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.50-use-hereby** [sev 3] Use hereby in language of performance; it signals that the act is accomplished by the speech act and excludes a habitual reading. (3.50, 3.51; PDF p. 141)
  - Fix: Acme hereby purchases ...
  - Exceptions: You could omit hereby ([1-1a] is rated ✓); the habitual reading is unlikely to confuse, but it could distract (3.50-3.51).
  - Book example: [1-1a] ✓: Acme purchases the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.52-not-by-this-agreement** [sev 3] Don't replace hereby with by this agreement; hereby relates to the particular language of performance and is shorter. (3.52; PDF p. 141)
- **R-3.53-hereby-only-performance** [sev 3] Omit hereby wherever it is used other than in language of performance. (3.53; PDF p. 141)
  - Test: Is the clause a speech act that changes status on signing? If not, delete hereby.
- **R-3.55-no-now** [sev 3] Don't use now in language of performance: it's primarily oral, a little old-fashioned, and could be read as habitual. (3.54, 3.55; PDF p. 142)
  - Book example: [1-1b] ××: Acme now purchases the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
  - Book example: the Collaborator now assigns the Acme Foreground Intellectual Property to Acme → (see fix)
- **R-3.56-performance-active** [sev 1] Always use the active voice in language of performance, to make the actor clear. (3.56; PDF p. 143)
  - Look for: hereby + passive verb
  - Book example: [1-1c] ××: The Assets are hereby purchased from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.57-no-present-continuous** [sev 3] Don't use the present continuous in language of performance; performance is instantaneous, not an unfolding event. (3.57; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1d] ××: Acme is hereby purchasing the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.58-no-do-auxiliary** [sev 3] Don't use do as an auxiliary in language of performance (does hereby), and don't use doth or hath; they're archaisms. (3.58; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1e] ××: Acme does hereby purchase the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.59-no-agrees-to-performance** [sev 1] Don't use agrees to, or agrees to and does hereby, as language of performance; it invites a fight over whether the clause is performance or obligation. (3.59; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1f] ××: Acme agrees to purchase the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
  - Book example: [1-1g] ××: Acme agrees to and does hereby purchase the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.60-no-by-these-presents** [sev 3] Don't use by these presents, alone or preceded by does, in language of performance. (3.60; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1h] ××: Acme by these presents purchases the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.61-no-obligation-as-of-signing** [sev 1] Don't try to turn language of obligation into performance by adding (as) of the date of this agreement; it imposes an obligation to act sometime on that date rather than effecting a transfer on signing. (3.61; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1i] ××: Acme shall purchase the Assets from Jackson as of the date of this agreement. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.62-no-is-pleased-to** [sev 2] Don't use is pleased to in language of performance; it doesn't make clear that the speech act accomplishes the action. (3.62; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1j] ××: Acme is pleased to purchase the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
  - Book example: Acme Trust is pleased to retain Summit Consulting, LLC ... → (see fix)
- **R-3.63-no-irrevocably-performance** [sev 3] Don't use irrevocably, unconditionally, absolutely, or forever in language of performance; absent contrary language, an action taken can't be undone, so they're redundant. (3.63; PDF p. 143)
  - Book example: [1-1k] ××: Acme hereby irrevocably purchases the Assets from Jackson. → [1-1] ✓✓: Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson.
- **R-3.64-irrevocably-appoint** [sev 2] Use irrevocably with appoint if you don't want the appointing party to be able to remove the appointee, because the power to appoint might include the power to remove; or say so explicitly. (3.64; PDF p. 143)
- **R-3.65-irrevocable-ongoing-consent** [sev 2] If a party consents to ongoing conduct (not a one-time action), use irrevocably so the consenting party can't later change its mind. (3.65; PDF p. 143)
- **R-3.66-performance-status-only** [sev 2] Don't use language of performance for an action other than a change in status of a person or thing; you can't pay someone just by saying so. (3.66; PDF p. 144)
  - Book example: The Buyer hereby pays the Seller ... → (see fix)
- **R-3.67-absence-of-performance** [sev 2] To state that a party is not taking an action on signing, don't use language of performance in the negative; use language of policy with the negative present continuous. (3.67; PDF p. 144)
  - Book example: [1-2] ××: The Buyer does not hereby assume the Excluded Liabilities. → [1-2b] ✓✓: In this agreement the Buyer is not assuming any Excluded Liabilities.
  - Book example: [1-2a] ××: The Buyer hereby does not assume the Excluded Liabilities. → [1-2b] ✓✓: In this agreement the Buyer is not assuming any Excluded Liabilities.
- **R-3.68-future-performance** [sev 2] To state the consequences of entering into a contract or issuing a document in the future, don't use will ... thereby; use will be deemed. (3.68; PDF p. 144)
  - HOUSE: Active voice only. Recast passives: name the actor (any Person if unknown); legal fictions as 'This agreement treats X as' or a linking verb ('is effective'); amendments as 'The parties hereby amend the section titled ...'. See house-style.md section 1.
  - Book example: [1-3] ×: When the Company issues a Purchase Order, it will thereby hire the Contractor to perform the services stated in that Purchase Order. → [1-3a] ✓✓: When the Company issues a Purchase Order, it will be deemed to have hired the Contractor to perform the services stated in that Purchase Order.
- **R-3.69-one-perspective** [sev 3] For a purchase on signing, use one perspective (Acme hereby purchases ... or Jackson hereby sells ...); using both verbs is unnecessary. (3.69, 3.70, 3.71; PDF p. 145)
  - Exceptions: Drafters may want reciprocity shown even at the cost of redundancy; be prepared to be asked to use both (3.71).
  - Book example: (none) → Acme hereby purchases the Assets from Jackson. [or] Jackson hereby sells the Assets to Acme.
- **R-3.72-services-obligation** [sev 3] Use language of obligation as the default for performance of services; use language of performance (appoints, employs, retains) only where the services define the performer by a label such as agent or employee. (3.72, 3.73, 3.74; PDF p. 145)
  - Book example: (none) → The Fund hereby appoints the Agent as its Investor Servicing Agent ... [label: language of performance sounds natural]
- **R-3.76-granting-language-ip** [sev 3] Use granting language (hereby grants ... a license) in contracts dealing with rights to software and intellectual property, where it's entrenched and lets you use sublicense; resist it elsewhere, using language of discretion instead. (3.75, 3.76, 3.77...; PDF p. 147)
  - Book example: [1-4a] ✓: Acme hereby grants Widgetco the right to use the Marks in ... . → [1-4] ✓✓: Acme hereby grants Widgetco a license to use the Marks in ... .
  - Book example: [1-4b] ×× (in a license): Widgetco may use the Marks in ... . → [1-4] ✓✓: Acme hereby grants Widgetco a license to use the Marks in ... .
- **R-3.79-grant-a-license** [sev 3] Prefer grants ... a license to the verb license: the noun takes adjectives (nonexclusive, irrevocable, perpetual) more simply, and Acme hereby licenses alone could make Acme either licensee or licensor. (3.79, 3.80; PDF p. 147)
  - Book example: [1-4c] ×: Acme hereby licenses to Widgetco the right to use the Marks in ... . → [1-4] ✓✓: Acme hereby grants Widgetco a license to use the Marks in ... .
- **R-3.81-lease-verb** [sev 3] For leases, the verb lease remains conventional (Jones hereby leases to Smith); Jones hereby grants Smith a lease would be unorthodox. (3.81; PDF p. 147)
- **R-3.82-grant-not-grant-to** [sev 3] Omit to after grant: hereby grants Widgetco a license, not hereby grants to Widgetco a license. The same applies to other verbs, such as pay. (3.82, 3.83, 3.84...; PDF p. 147)
  - Book example: [1-4d] ×: Acme hereby grants to Widgetco a license to use the Marks in ... . → [1-4] ✓✓: Acme hereby grants Widgetco a license to use the Marks in ... .

### Chapter 3: Language of Discretion

- **R-3.205-discretion-definition** [sev 3] Use language of discretion to state that a party has discretion to take or not take a specified action. (3.205; PDF p. 180)
- **R-3.206-may-active-voice** [sev 3] Don't use may with a passive verb; make the party with discretion the subject. (3.206; PDF p. 180)
  - Book example: [4-2] ✓: The Option may be exercised by Smith before 1 January 2024. → [4-2b] ✓✓: Smith may exercise the Option before 1 January 2024.
  - Book example: [4-2a] ✓: The Option may be exercised before 1 January 2024. → [4-2b] ✓✓: Smith may exercise the Option before 1 January 2024.
- **R-3.206-use-may** [sev 3] Grant discretion with may in the active voice; don't use wordier or less precise alternatives (is authorized to, is entitled to, shall have the right to, reserves the right to, is permitted to, is allowed to, is free to, is hereby empowered to, is at liberty to). (3.206, 3.207; PDF p. 180)
  - Book example: [4-1a] ×: Acme is authorized to ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may appoint one or more subcontractors.
  - Book example: [4-1b] ×: Acme is entitled to ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may ... .
- **R-3.207-no-agreement-allows** [sev 3] Don't frame discretion as granted by the contract (This agreement allows Acme to ...). (3.207; PDF p. 180)
  - Book example: [4-1m] ××: This agreement allows Acme to ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may ... .
- **R-3.207-no-but-is-not-required** [sev 3] Don't add but is not required to (or but is not obligated to, or shall have the right but not the obligation to) after may; discretion inherently carries no obligation. (3.207, 3.528; PDF p. 180)
  - Book example: [4-1j] ××: Acme may but is not required to ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may ... .
  - Book example: ... the Company may (but is not required to) increase the Conversion Rate ... . → ... the Company may increase the Conversion Rate ... .
- **R-3.207-no-can** [sev 2] Don't use can to grant discretion; can expresses ability, not discretion. (3.207; PDF p. 180)
- **R-3.207-no-exercisable** [sev 3] Don't express discretion with a buried-actor policy such as The Option is exercisable; make the party the subject of may. (3.207; PDF p. 180)
  - Book example: [4-2c] ××: The Option is exercisable before 1 January 2024. → [4-2b] ✓✓: Smith may exercise the Option before 1 January 2024.
- **R-3.207-no-may-freely** [sev 3] Don't use freely with may; it's redundant. (3.207; PDF p. 180)
  - Book example: [4-1k] ××: Acme may freely ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may ... .
- **R-3.208-no-discretion-already-free** [sev 2] Don't use language of discretion to say what someone is free to do anyway; at best it's redundant, and at worst the other party might argue the discretion exists only because of the contract. To remind the parties, use language of declaration with acknowledges. (3.208, 3.209; PDF p. 181)
  - Look for: may + an action the party could take without the contract (inspect, rely, consult)
  - Fix: Delete it, or introduce the reminder with language of declaration using acknowledges (3.209).
  - Book example: The Buyer may inspect the Goods after they are received ... . → (see fix)
- **R-rider-2025-whether-world-at-large** [sev 2] When a party's ability (or requirement) to act arises outside the contract, from the world at large, and isn't granted or imposed by the contract, say so with a statement of fact: Acme states that it may sell the Widgets (or Acme states that it must sell the Widgets), adding the basis if helpful (Acme states that because of X, it may sell the Widgets in accordance with Y). (3.208, 3.209, 3.457; PDF p. 181; elaboration)
  - Look for: may or must describing a freedom or requirement that exists independently of the contract
  - Fix: [Party] states that [because of X,] it may [or must] [verb] ... .
- **R-3.210-dont-constrain-free-conduct** [sev 2] Don't constrain, in language of discretion, what a party is free to do anyway (inspect within a reasonable time); if timing matters only as a condition, put it in a conditional clause. (3.210, 3.211; PDF p. 181)
  - Book example: The Buyer may within a reasonable time inspect the Goods ... . → If on inspecting the Goods within a reasonable time after delivery the Buyer determines that any fail to meet the Specifications, the Buyer may return the Goods ... .
- **R-3.215-explicit-limited-discretion** [sev 1] Be explicit about whether discretion is limited. A specific grant of discretion (Acme may sell the Shares to Ferguson) suggests, through the expectation of relevance, that other options are excluded, and courts may invoke expressio unius. (3.212, 3.213, 3.214...; PDF p. 182)
  - Look for: may + a specific option where the party might have others
  - Book example: Acme may sell the Shares to Ferguson. → Unlimited: Acme may sell the Shares to any Person.  Limited: The only Person to whom Acme may sell the Shares is Ferguson. [or] Acme shall not sell the Shares to anyone other than Ferguson.
- **R-3.222-may-only-ambiguous** [sev 1] Don't use may ... only to express limited discretion; it's ambiguous between 'may elect to do only X' and 'the only thing it may do is X,' and repositioning only won't cure it. (3.222, 3.223, 3.224...; PDF p. 182)
  - Book example: Acme may close one or more Contract Stores for any reason, and in doing so it may consider only its own interests. → ... and in doing so it may elect to consider only its own interests.
  - Book example: Widgetco may sell only the 1965 Ford Mustang. → Widgetco shall sell no vehicle other than the 1965 Ford Mustang.
- **R-3.227-exclusive-option** [sev 2] If a party's only choices are to take a specified action or do nothing, use language of discretion and say that the choice offered is the only choice. (3.227, 3.228, 3.229...; PDF p. 182)
  - Book example: Any controversy or claim ... shall be settled by arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association. → As the exclusive means of initiating adversarial proceedings to resolve any dispute ..., a party may demand that the dispute be resolved by arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association ... .
- **R-3.231-might-for-possibility** [sev 1] Use may only to grant discretion; use might to express possibility. Using may for possibility creates ambiguity between authorization and possibility. (3.231, 3.232, 3.233...; PDF p. 187)
  - Test: Can you replace may with 'has discretion to'? If not, and you mean possibility, use might.
  - Fix: If you mean possibility, use might (and introduce it with language of declaration, 3.234); or recast as a condition: If the Investigator provides ... then ... (3.233).
  - Book example: The Investigator may provide the Sponsor with confidential information. → (see fix)
- **R-3.234-declaration-for-might** [sev 3] Because might states a fact, introduce it with language of declaration, such as The parties acknowledge that. (3.234; PDF p. 187)
- **R-3.236-no-might-expects-distinction** [sev 2] Don't rely on a distinction between might and expects to; use might, or state explicitly the basis for any expectation. (3.236; PDF p. 187)
- **R-3.237-keep-may** [sev 3] Don't abandon may for is permitted to out of fear that may will be read as might; the risk is not meaningful, and may remains the best way to express discretion. (3.237, 3.238, 3.239...; PDF p. 187)
- **R-3.241-may-require-obligation** [sev 2] Where may require frames one party's discretion over what is really the other party's obligation, use language of obligation and state the trigger: At X's request, Y shall ... . (3.241; PDF p. 188)
  - Book example: The Company may require a Participant to retain the shares ... → At the Company's written request, a Participant shall retain the shares ...
  - Book example: The Bank may require each Borrower to establish a lockbox ... → At the Bank's written request, each Borrower shall establish a lockbox ...
- **R-3.242-may-require-possibility** [sev 1] Where may require indicates possibility, use might. (3.242; PDF p. 188)
  - Book example: The issuer of these securities may require an opinion of counsel ... → The issuer of these securities might require an opinion of counsel ...
- **R-3.243-may-require-relative-clause** [sev 2] In a restrictive relative clause, may is superfluous; use the simple present (as the Bank requires). (3.243; PDF p. 188)
  - Book example: such other documentation as the Bank may require → such other documentation as the Bank requires
- **R-3.246-may-request-instruction** [sev 2] Where may request actually imposes an obligation on another party, say At the request of X, Y shall ... ; if a separate document (such as an Issuer Order) reflects the request, omit the may request language. (3.244, 3.245, 3.246...; PDF p. 188)
  - Fix: At the request of X, Y shall ... .
  - Exceptions: May request as a main verb might simply mean a party may ask, for example for assistance (3.245).
  - Book example: the Issuer may request and the Trustee, upon receiving an Issuer Order, shall authenticate ... → the Trustee, upon receiving an Issuer Order, shall authenticate ...
  - Book example: the Trustee may request that the Company deliver an officers' certificate ... → (see fix)
- **R-3.249-no-sole-discretion** [sev 2] Don't use at its sole discretion with may. If it's rhetorical emphasis, delete it; if it tries to address a real issue (such as the implied duty of good faith), address that issue directly. (3.249, 3.250, 3.251...; PDF p. 190)
  - Book example: [4-1n] ××: Acme may at its sole discretion ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may ... . [address any real concern directly]
- **R-3.253-no-sole-and-absolute** [sev 2] Don't use absolute instead of or with sole (sole and absolute), or uncontrolled; it's legalistic redundancy. (3.253; PDF p. 190)
- **R-3.254-no-good-faith-discretion** [sev 3] Don't refer to good-faith discretion; it's redundant. (3.254; PDF p. 190)
- **R-3.255-reasonable-discretion** [sev 2] Be aware that reasonable discretion might curtail discretion: a reasonableness standard could be read as stricter than good faith. (3.255; PDF p. 190)
- **R-3.267-no-good-faith-waiver** [sev 2] Eliminate any language that could be construed as waiving the implied duty of good faith (sole discretion defined as without regard to reasonableness, for any reason or no reason), even if it favors your client. (3.257, 3.258, 3.259...; PDF p. 190)
- **R-3.269-address-concern-directly** [sev 2] Instead of sole discretion, address the concern directly: make the discretion subject to stated conditions, or have the other party acknowledge that specified adverse consequences are irrelevant to the exercise of discretion. (3.269, 3.270, 3.271...; PDF p. 190)
  - Book example: Acme may at any time close any one or more Contract Stores. [adding at its sole discretion is reckless to rely on] → Acme may close one or more Contract Stores for any reason, and in doing so it may elect to consider only its own interests and will not be required to consider the effect ... on Widgetco ... .
- **R-3.275-fallback-omit** [sev 3] If you can't or won't craft explicit language, either use at its discretion and warn the client it might not ensure unfettered discretion, or (more prudent) omit it. (3.275; PDF p. 190)
- **R-3.276-eliminate-discretion** [sev 2] Where neither conditions nor acknowledgments can work (subjective judgments that cost the discretion holder nothing, as in Cussler), eliminate the problematic discretion, for example by making an approved item final or giving one party unilateral control. (3.276, 3.277; PDF p. 190)
- **R-3.278-sole-discretion-other-clauses** [sev 2] Treat at its sole discretion in conditional clauses and restrictive relative clauses the same way: address a real deal issue directly, or delete a legalistic habit. (3.278, 3.279, 3.280; PDF p. 190)
  - Book example: If Acme at its sole discretion buys more widgets ... → (see fix)
  - Book example: at a price acceptable to Widgetco in its sole discretion → (see fix)
- **R-3.281-no-grants-the-right-to** [sev 3] Instead of Party X hereby grants Party Y the right to [verb] (discretion disguised as performance), say Party Y may [verb]. (3.281, 3.282; PDF p. 196)
  - Exceptions: Granting a license (noun) remains appropriate for software and IP (3.76-3.77).
  - Book example: [4-1o] ××: The Customer hereby grants Acme the right to ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may ... .
  - Book example: The Borrower hereby grants to the Holder the right to set off against this note upon occurrence of an Event of Default. → The Holder may set off against this note upon occurrence of an Event of Default.
- **R-3.283-no-hereby-consents-to** [sev 3] Instead of Party X hereby consents to Party Y [verb]ing, say Party Y may [verb]. (3.283; PDF p. 196)
  - Book example: [4-1p] ××: The Customer hereby consents to Acme appointing ... . → [4-1] ✓✓: Acme may appoint ... .
- **R-3.285-discretion-requiring-cooperation** [sev 2] Where exercising discretion requires another party's cooperation, make the implicit obligation explicit with language of obligation; will be given the opportunity to only hints at it. (3.284, 3.285; PDF p. 196)
  - Book example: One or more Acme representatives may inspect Widgetco's financial records ... . → Widgetco shall permit one or more Acme representatives to inspect Widgetco's financial records during business hours at Widgetco's principal office.
- **R-3.286-no-waives-right-discretion** [sev 3] When hereby waives the right to really means the other party doesn't have to do something, say that (is not required to). (3.286, 3.287; PDF p. 196)
  - Book example: Acme hereby waives any right to inspect or approve of any finished photographs. → Photoco is not required to permit Acme to inspect or approve of any finished photographs.
  - Book example: The Employee hereby waives his right to receive any severance other than under this agreement. → The Company will not be required to pay the Employee any severance other than under this agreement.
- **R-3.288-is-entitled-to-discretion** [sev 2] Is entitled to with an active complement clause expresses discretion that depends on another party's performance, which may doesn't capture; but it's much clearer to ignore that distinction and state the other party's obligation. (3.288, 3.289, 3.290...; PDF p. 196)
  - Exceptions: In statutory drafting, expressing 'a person is entitled to vote' as an obligation would be awkward; the fix works in contracts because they have few actors (3.291).
  - Book example: [5-1] ✓: No later than five days after Rubin exercises the Option, Rubin will be entitled to purchase a number of Shares equal to ... . → [5-1c] ✓✓: No later than five days after Rubin exercises the Option, Acme shall sell to Rubin, at Rubin's election, a number of shares equal to ... .
  - Book example: [5-1a] ××: Exercise of the Option will entitle Rubin to purchase within five days a number of Shares equal to ... . → [5-1c] ✓✓ [as above]
- **R-3.292-is-not-required-to** [sev 3] Express absence of obligation with is not required to + verb; don't use nothing in this agreement requires, this agreement does not require, in no event is ... required to, is not obligated to, is not obliged to, or is not bound to. (3.292, 3.293, 3.294; PDF p. 199)
  - HOUSE: partly superseded: Blake's decision: is not required to is a fixed exception to the active-voice rule; use it as MSCD prescribes.
  - Book example: [6-1a] ××: Nothing in this agreement requires Acme to replace the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
  - Book example: [6-1b] ✓: Acme is not [obligated] [obliged] to replace the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
- **R-3.294-no-may-not-authorized-not** [sev 1] Don't use may not to mean 'is authorized not to'; may not has three possible meanings. (3.294; PDF p. 199)
- **R-3.295-active-after-not-required** [sev 3] Use the active voice after is not required to. (3.295; PDF p. 199)
  - Book example: [6-3] ×: Amounts collected from Participants are not required to be held in a segregated account. → [6-3a] ✓✓: Widgetco is not required to hold in a segregated account any amounts collected from Participants.
- **R-3.296-no-shall-not-be-required** [sev 3] Don't use shall not be required to; don't use will not be required to unless you are addressing the consequences of a contingent future event. (3.296; PDF p. 199)
  - Exceptions: Will not be required to is acceptable for consequences of a contingent future event (3.296; 3.334).
  - Book example: [6-1d] ××: Acme shall not be required to replace the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
  - Book example: [6-1e] ×: Acme will not be required to replace the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
- **R-3.297-not-imposing-on-other** [sev 1] Don't confuse not imposing an obligation on Party A with imposing that obligation on Party B. (3.297; PDF p. 199)
  - Book example: [6-1f] ××: Widgetco shall replace the Widget Equipment. [where the intent is only that Acme need not] → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
- **R-3.298-cap-not-limit** [sev 2] Don't impose limits on Party A when Party B's concern is only that it not be responsible beyond a limit; state what must be done, with a cap. (3.298; PDF p. 199)
  - Book example: [6-2a] ××: The Consultant's annual expenses must not exceed $10,000. → [6-2b] ✓✓: Acme shall reimburse the Consultant's expenses up to $10,000 per year.
  - Book example: [6-2] ✓: Acme is not required to reimburse the Consultant for annual expenses in excess of $10,000. → [6-2b] ✓✓: Acme shall reimburse the Consultant's expenses up to $10,000 per year.
- **R-3.299-no-is-not-entitled-absence** [sev 3] Instead of is not entitled to + a passive complement clause or + a noun, focus on the party not subject to the obligation. (3.299; PDF p. 199)
  - Book example: [6-1g] ✓: Widgetco is not entitled to have the Widget Equipment replaced. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
  - Book example: Acme is not entitled to advance notice. → Levine is not required to give Acme advance notice.
- **R-3.300-no-need-not** [sev 2] Don't use need not; need is used colloquially for both lack and obligation, and need not can also mean 'should not' or 'has no reason to.' (3.300; PDF p. 199)
  - Book example: [6-1h] ××: Acme need not replace the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
- **R-3.301-no-may-refuse** [sev 2] Don't use may refuse to, may decline to, or may abstain from to express absence of obligation, and don't say one party does not expect the other to act. (3.301; PDF p. 199)
  - Book example: [6-1i] ××: Acme may refuse to replace the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
  - Book example: [6-1j] ××: Acme may abstain from replacing the Widget Equipment. → [6-1] ✓✓: Acme is not required to replace the Widget Equipment.
- **R-3.302-address-real-issue** [sev 2] If it wouldn't make sense to impose an obligation on a party, don't negate it with is not required to or a variant; address the real issue, for example with language of policy stating the party will not be liable. (3.302, 3.303; PDF p. 199)
  - Fix: Say instead, in language of policy, that the owner and architect will not be liable if the contractor doesn't pay subcontractors (3.303).
  - Book example: Neither the Owner nor Architect shall have an obligation to pay or see to the payment of money to a Subcontractor or supplier except as may otherwise be required by law. → (see fix)
- **R-3.304-is-not-prohibited-from** [sev 3] MSCD5: express absence of prohibition with is not prohibited from; except to avoid confusion, don't use this agreement does not prohibit or nothing in this agreement prohibits. You could use may instead, but if Acme could act anyway absent the contract, don't suggest its ability comes from the contract. (3.304; PDF p. 203)
  - Book example: (none) → Acme is not prohibited from removing the Widget Equipment. [figure 1]
- **R-rider-2025-11-19-nothing-prohibits** [sev 2] To express absence of prohibition, use language of declaration rather than language of discretion: The parties acknowledge that nothing in this agreement prohibits Acme from [verb]ing. Don't use is not prohibited from. (3.304; PDF p. 203; rider)
  - Fix: The parties acknowledge that nothing in this agreement prohibits [Party] from [verb]ing ... .

### Chapter 3: Language of Prohibition

- **R-3.305-shall-not** [sev 1] Express prohibition with shall not (has a duty not to) imposed on a party that is the subject of the sentence. (3.305; PDF p. 204)
  - Test: Has a duty not to: if the subject isn't a party, shall not doesn't work (3.155).
  - Book example: (none) → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment without Acme's prior written consent.
- **R-3.306-no-must-not** [sev 2] Don't use must not instead of shall not for a prohibition on a party that is the subject. (3.306; PDF p. 204)
  - Book example: [7-1a] ×: The Customer must not modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.307-no-may-not** [sev 1] Don't use may not for prohibition; Acme may not transfer the Shares could mean Acme might not transfer, is authorized not to transfer, or isn't authorized to transfer. (3.307; PDF p. 204)
  - Exceptions: With a negative subject (no party, neither party), may is the better choice (3.324).
  - Book example: [7-1b] ×: The Customer may not modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.308-is-not-entitled-to-prohibition** [sev 3] Use is not entitled to + active complement clause only when the prohibition rests on another party's not being obligated (Mancini is not entitled to serve on Widgetco's board); otherwise don't use it for prohibition, and don't use has no right to. (3.308, 3.309, 3.310; PDF p. 204)
  - Book example: [7-1c] ×: The Customer is not entitled to modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
  - Book example: Acme shall not convert its Shares ... . → Acme is not entitled to convert the Shares except as provided in section 3.2.
- **R-3.311-no-shall-refrain** [sev 3] Don't use shall refrain from; refrain is polite deference suited to social contexts. Use shall not. (3.311, 3.312; PDF p. 206)
  - Book example: [7-1d] ××: The Customer shall refrain from modifying the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.313-no-waives-right-prohibition** [sev 3] Don't use hereby waives the right to (or any right to) to express prohibition; use shall not. (3.313, 3.314, 3.315...; PDF p. 206)
  - Book example: [7-1e] ××: The Customer hereby waives the right to modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
  - Book example: Acme hereby waives the right to sell the Shares. → Acme shall not sell the Shares.
- **R-3.317-jury-waiver** [sev 3] For jury-trial waivers, you could use prohibition (Each party shall not demand a trial by jury), but because waiver language is universal, you might find it expedient to keep it. (3.317; PDF p. 206)
- **R-3.319-no-shall-never** [sev 2] Don't use shall never; if an obligation should continue perpetually, even after termination, say so explicitly. (3.319; PDF p. 206)
  - Book example: [7-1f] ××: The Customer shall never modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.320-no-shall-in-no-way** [sev 3] Don't use shall in no way; it's rhetorical emphasis. (3.320; PDF p. 206)
  - Book example: [7-1g] ××: The Customer shall in no way modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.321-no-cannot-agrees-not** [sev 3] Don't use cannot or agrees not to in language of prohibition. (3.321; PDF p. 206)
  - Book example: [7-1h] ××: The Customer cannot modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
  - Book example: [7-1i] ××: The Customer agrees not to modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.322-no-nothing-gives-right** [sev 3] Don't say the contract doesn't authorize a party to do something; express it as a prohibition. (3.322; PDF p. 206)
  - Book example: [7-1j] ××: Nothing in this agreement gives the Customer the right to modify the Equipment ... . → [7-1] ✓✓: The Customer shall not modify the Equipment ... .
- **R-3.323-collective-noun-prohibition** [sev 3] With a collective-noun subject, express prohibition with a negative subject (neither for two, no for more) plus may, or with a singular subject and a negative verb (each party shall not); avoid neither party shall and the parties shall not ... their. (3.323, 3.324, 3.325; PDF p. 208)
  - HOUSE: House addition: Never use shall with a negative subject (Neither party shall, No Shareholder shall); use Neither party may / No Shareholder may (3.324). With a positive subject use shall not.
  - Book example: [7-2] ×: Neither party shall assign any of its rights ... . → [7-2a] ✓: Neither party may assign any of its rights ... .
  - Book example: [7-2b] ×: The parties shall not assign any of their rights ... . → [7-2c] ✓: Each party shall not assign any of its rights ... .
- **R-3.326-no-nor-shall** [sev 3] Don't use nor shall to continue a prohibition; start a new sentence with language of prohibition. (3.326, 3.327, 3.328; PDF p. 208)
  - Book example: The Receiving Party shall not use Confidential Information other than as authorized ... under the SOW, nor shall the Receiving Party disclose any Confidential Information ... . → The Receiving Party shall not use Confidential Information other than as authorized ... under the SOW. The Receiving Party shall not disclose any Confidential Information ... .
- **R-3.329-exception-to-discretion** [sev 2] To express a prohibition as an exception to language of discretion, state it explicitly rather than relying on the expectation of relevance. (3.329, 3.330; PDF p. 208)
  - Book example: Widgetco may sell one or more of the Vehicles except the 1965 Ford Mustang. → Widgetco may sell one or more of the Vehicles but not the 1965 Ford Mustang. [or: ..., except that it shall not sell the 1965 Ford Mustang]
- **R-3.331-exception-to-obligation** [sev 1] To express a prohibition as an exception to language of obligation, be explicit; merely carving the item out of the obligation says nothing about whether the party may do it. (3.331; PDF p. 208)
  - Book example: Widgetco shall sell all Vehicles except the 1965 Ford Mustang. → Widgetco shall sell one or more of the Vehicles, except that it shall not sell the 1965 Ford Mustang.

### Chapter 3: Language of Policy

- **R-3.332-policy-definition** [sev 3] Use language of policy for rules the parties must observe that don't, at least expressly, require or permit action or inaction: rules governing a thing, event, or circumstance, and rules on the scope, meaning, or duration of the contract. (3.332, 3.333; PDF p. 211)
- **R-3.334-policy-present-tense** [sev 3] Use the present tense for policies that apply on effectiveness of the contract (governing law, entire agreement, definitions) and for policies that state a fixed time of effectiveness or lapse. (3.334; PDF p. 211)
  - Look for: governing law, entire-agreement, definition, or fixed-date termination in will or shall
  - Book example: [8-2a] ×: The laws of the state of New York will govern ... . → [8-2] ✓✓: The laws of the state of New York govern all matters arising out of this agreement.
- **R-3.334-policy-will-contingent** [sev 2] Use will for policies about contingent future events, meaning events that might not happen or whose timing is uncertain (will terminate upon the closing of a Qualified IPO; will be void). (3.334; PDF p. 211)
  - Look for: a policy tied to an uncertain future event stated in the present tense or with shall
  - Book example: [8-6a] ×: This agreement terminates upon the closing of a Qualified IPO. → [8-6] ✓✓: This agreement will terminate upon the closing of a Qualified IPO.
  - Book example: [8-1] ×: Any attempted transfer of Shares in violation of this agreement is void. → [8-1a] ✓✓: Any attempted transfer of Shares in violation of this agreement will be void.
- **R-3.335-no-shall-in-policy** [sev 3] Don't use shall in language of policy; policy imposes no obligation. (3.335; PDF p. 211)
  - Book example: [8-1b] ××: Any attempted transfer of Shares in violation of this agreement shall be void. → [8-1a] ✓✓: ... will be void.
  - Book example: [8-2b] ××: The laws of the state of New York shall govern ... . → [8-2] ✓✓: The laws of the state of New York govern ... .
- **R-3.336-buried-actor-policies** [sev 1] Rephrase buried-actor policies (exercisable, payable, recoverable) as language of obligation, language of discretion, or a conditional clause; they often omit the responsible party, and The fee is payable by 20 April could mean an obligation, discretion, or a condition. (3.336; PDF p. 213)
  - Book example: [8-7] ✓: Interest is payable at a rate of 8% per year. → [8-7b] ✓✓: The Borrower shall pay interest ... .
  - Book example: [8-7a] ××: Interest shall be payable ... . → [8-7b] ✓✓: The Borrower shall pay interest ... .
- **R-3.337-no-is-due** [sev 2] Rephrase is due as language of obligation. (3.337; PDF p. 213)
  - Book example: The First Installment is due by 1 March 2024. → The Purchaser shall pay the First Installment by 1 March 2024.
- **R-3.338-no-subject-to-change-eligible** [sev 2] Instead of is subject to + abstract noun with an abstract-noun subject, use may + verb with a party as subject; instead of is eligible for, state the obligation. (3.338; PDF p. 213)
  - Book example: The Employee's position and assignments are subject to change. → The Company may change the Employee's position and assignments.
  - Book example: The Employee will be eligible for a bonus if ... → The Company shall pay the Employee a bonus if ...
- **R-3.339-passive-in-policy** [sev 3] The passive voice in language of policy is unremarkable; where the missing by-agent is the world at large, prevent any implication that someone must act by adding automatically or by switching to an active verb with the same subject. (3.339, 3.340; PDF p. 213)
  - HOUSE: Active voice only. Recast passives: name the actor (any Person if unknown); legal fictions as 'This agreement treats X as' or a linking verb ('is effective'); amendments as 'The parties hereby amend the section titled ...'. See house-style.md section 1.
  - Exceptions: Don't switch will be deemed to the active voice (3.340; 13.209-.210).
  - Book example: the Option Price will be increased → the Option Price will increase
- **R-3.341-no-policy-obfuscation** [sev 2] Don't turn provisions about party actions into opaque language of policy through jargon (lead-time, net 30); state the action and the actor, even at the cost of extra words. (3.341, 3.342, 3.343...; PDF p. 213)
  - Book example: Acme will have a lead-time of up to 45 days from accepting a Purchase Order for scheduling Services. → No later than 45 days after it receives a Purchase Order, Acme shall notify the Customer of the schedule for performing the Services requested in that Purchase Order.
  - Book example: Payment terms are net 30 from the date of the invoice. → The Customer shall pay each invoice no later than 30 days from the date of the invoice.

### Chapter 3: Expressing Conditions (general)

- **R-3.348-condition-definition** [sev 3] Treat a condition as a future and uncertain event or circumstance on which a legal relation depends; express it by a conditional clause, language of obligation (must + to-clause), or language of policy (It is a condition to ... that), choosing by context. (3.348, 3.349, 3.351; PDF p. 215)
- **R-3.350-condition-terminology** [sev 2] Say condition, not condition precedent; never use condition subsequent (state that if X happens, Y will cease); and say a condition is satisfied or not satisfied, not breached or complied with. (3.350; PDF p. 215)

### Chapter 3: Expressing Conditions

- **R-3.352-conditional-clause-structure** [sev 3] A conditional clause (introduced by if, unless, where, when, as long as, so long as, or on condition that) modifies a matrix clause in language of obligation, discretion, prohibition, or policy; it isn't itself a category. (3.352, 3.353, 3.354; PDF p. 216)
  - Book example: (none) → If Acme has not exercised the Option by 31 December 2022, Smith may transfer the Shares to another Person.
- **R-3.356-if-then** [sev 3] Use then at the start of the matrix clause only when a complex conditional clause comes first and then would prevent a reader miscue; omit it after a short conditional clause. (3.356, 3.357; PDF p. 216)
- **R-3.358-conditional-present-tense** [sev 3] Use the present tense in conditional clauses and other subordinate clauses (notwithstanding, even if); don't use shall, should, or the subjunctive there. (3.358, 3.359, 3.360; PDF p. 216)
  - Book example: [9-1b] ××: If Investco shall receive a Violation Notice, ... . → [9-1] ✓✓: If Investco receives a Violation Notice, it shall promptly notify Widgetco.
  - Book example: [9-1c] ××: If Investco shall have received a Violation Notice, ... . → [9-1] ✓✓: If Investco receives a Violation Notice, ... .
- **R-3.358-present-perfect-option** [sev 3] The present perfect in a conditional clause is acceptable if satisfying the condition is part of the transaction rather than a response to a contingency, but use the present tense for consistency. (3.358; PDF p. 216)
  - Book example: [9-1a] ×: If Investco has received a Violation Notice, ... . → [9-1] ✓✓: If Investco receives a Violation Notice, ... .
  - Book example: [9-2a] ✓: If Acme has received a Notice of Consent, ... . → [9-2] ✓✓: If Acme receives a Notice of Consent, it may transfer the Shares.
- **R-3.359-stative-verb-condition** [sev 2] With a stative verb in a conditional clause, address any fear that the condition must be met at signing by adding at any time, or better, switch to a dynamic verb. (3.359; PDF p. 216)
  - Look for: if + is/has (stative) describing a state that could arise later
  - Book example: [9-3] ✓: If the Borrower is in default, the Lender may accelerate the Loan. → [9-3d] ✓✓: If the Borrower defaults, ... .
  - Book example: [9-3] ✓: If the Borrower is in default, ... . → [9-3c] ✓: If the Borrower is at any time in default, ... .
- **R-3.361-matrix-clause-will** [sev 3] In a matrix clause following a conditional clause, use will where the verb would otherwise be in the present tense; don't use the present tense or shall. Shall, may, and must are unaffected by a conditional clause. (3.361, 3.362; PDF p. 216)
  - Look for: matrix clause after if/unless with a present-tense or shall policy verb
  - Book example: [10-1a] ××: If Jones ceases to be employed by the Company, the Option shall terminate. → [10-1] ✓✓: If Jones ceases to be employed by the Company, the Option will terminate.
  - Book example: [10-1b] ×: If ... , the Option terminates. → [10-1] ✓✓: If ... , the Option will terminate.
- **R-3.363-positive-or-negative** [sev 2] Choose between may ... only if and shall not ... unless based on what the client expects or prefers; to avoid relying on the expectation of relevance, say: Acme shall not sell the Shares, except that it may sell the Shares if Widgetco consents. (3.363, 3.364, 3.365...; PDF p. 219)
  - Book example: Acme may sell the Shares only if Widgetco consents. / Acme shall not sell the Shares unless Widgetco consents. → Acme shall not sell the Shares, except that it may sell the Shares if Widgetco consents.
- **R-3.369-unless-necessary-condition** [sev 2] In language of policy, use the negative unless form for a condition that is necessary but not sufficient (amendments): will not be effective unless ..., not will be effective if ... . (3.369, 3.370, 3.371; PDF p. 219)
  - Book example: An amendment to this agreement will be effective if it is in writing and signed by both parties. → An amendment to this agreement will not be effective unless it is in writing and signed by both parties.
- **R-3.372-unless-with-activity-obligation** [sev 1] When an obligation using a verb of activity (keep confidential, maintain) is coupled with an unless clause, replace the unless clause with except that + is not required to (or will no longer be required to) + an if clause. (3.372, 3.373, 3.374...; PDF p. 219)
  - Look for: shall + activity verb (keep, maintain, continue) + unless
  - Book example: A shall keep the information confidential unless by 1 July 2012 B has not paid A $10,000. → A shall keep the information confidential, except that if by 1 July 2012 B has not paid A $10,000, A will no longer be required to keep the information confidential.
- **R-3.386-condition-via-must** [sev 1] To express a condition through language of obligation, use must plus a to-infinitive clause or a for clause that makes the condition explicit; must alone or shall alone will likely be read as an obligation, and need only is too colloquial. (3.386, 3.387, 3.388...; PDF p. 224)
  - Book example: [11-1a] ××: Acme must submit to Widgetco ... [each invoice within 90 days]. → [11-1] ✓✓: To be reimbursed, Acme must submit to Widgetco no later than 90 days after Acme receives it each invoice for expenses that Acme incurs.
  - Book example: [11-1b] ××: Acme shall submit to Widgetco ... . → [11-1] ✓✓ [as above]
- **R-3.394-it-is-a-condition** [sev 3] To express a single condition in language of policy most concisely, begin with It is a condition to ... that; subject to satisfaction of the condition later in the sentence is wordy. (3.393, 3.394; PDF p. 226)
  - Book example: [12-1e] ××: Tanaka's employment as the Company's chief financial officer is subject to satisfaction of the condition that Tanaka has settled the Litigation. → [12-1] ✓✓: It is a condition to Tanaka's employment as the Company's chief financial officer that Tanaka has settled the Litigation.
- **R-3.395-closing-conditions-that-clauses** [sev 1] Introduce a group of closing conditions with ... are subject to satisfaction of the following conditions: and state each as a tabulated that-clause; for one condition, say ... are subject to the condition that ... . Without that, a condition can read as a statement of fact or, with shall, an obligation. (3.395, 3.396; PDF p. 226)
  - Book example: [12-2] ××: Acme's statements of fact are accurate at the Closing as though made at the Closing; ... . → [12-2a] ✓✓: that Acme's statements of fact are accurate ... .
  - Book example: [12-3] ××: the Buyer has received an opinion of Acme's counsel in the form of exhibit A ... . → [12-3a] ✓✓: that the Buyer has received an opinion of Acme's counsel ... .
- **R-3.397-condition-verb-tense** [sev 3] In conditions stated in language of policy, use the present tense or the present perfect; don't use shall (have), must (have), will (have), or the subjunctive. (3.397, 3.398; PDF p. 226)
  - Book example: [12-1a] ××: ... that Tanaka shall have settled the Litigation. → [12-1] ✓✓: ... that Tanaka has settled the Litigation.
  - Book example: [12-1b] ××: ... that Tanaka must have settled the Litigation. → [12-1] ✓✓ [as above]
- **R-3.399-compare-condition-methods** [sev 3] Pick the way of expressing a condition that context allows: for termination on cessation of employment, only a conditional clause is plausible; elsewhere (amendments, dispute notices) all three work and the difference is concision. (3.399, 3.400, 3.401; PDF p. 229)
  - Book example: *For the Option to terminate, Becker must cease to be employed by the Company. / *It is a condition to the Option terminating that Becker ceases to be employed by the Company. → If Becker ceases to be employed by the Company, the Option will terminate.
- **R-3.402-condition-cannot-be-ignored** [sev 1] If a party could ignore a condition before taking the action it governs, use an obligation instead; use a condition only if satisfying it depends on something outside the control of the party that would act. (3.402, 3.403, 3.404...; PDF p. 229)
  - Book example: It is a condition to Acme's conducting a public sale of widgets that Acme notify Widgetco in advance ... . → Before conducting a public sale of widgets, Acme shall notify Widgetco in advance of the date and location of that sale.
- **R-3.408-not-only-condition** [sev 2] Recognize that a stated condition might not be the only condition; a court might read others into the contract (an invoice 'valid only if' timely still wouldn't be valid at triple the agreed price). (3.408, 3.409; PDF p. 229)

### Chapter 3: Language of Declaration

- **R-3.410-declaration-definition** [sev 3] Use language of declaration (a verb of speaking) to declare facts so the declaring party is seen to assert them: states for a fact the declaring party knows and the other party wants asserted as accurate; acknowledges for a fact the other party knows and wants the declaring party to accept. (3.410, 3.411; PDF p. 231)
  - Book example: (none) → [13-1] ✓✓: Acme states that the Equipment is listed in schedule A.
  - Book example: (none) → [13-2] ✓✓: The Investor acknowledges that it has received a copy of each SEC Document.
- **R-3.412-no-hereby-declaration** [sev 3] Don't use hereby with language of declaration. (3.412; PDF p. 231)
  - Book example: [13-1a] ×: Acme hereby states ... . → [13-1] ✓✓: Acme states that the Equipment is listed in schedule A.
  - Book example: [13-2a] ×: The Investor hereby acknowledges ... . → [13-2] ✓✓: The Investor acknowledges that it has received a copy of each SEC Document.
- **R-3.414-no-piled-declaration-verbs** [sev 2] Don't pile other verbs onto represents or warrants (represents and acknowledges; represents, warrants, covenants, and agrees); it makes no sense. (3.413, 3.414; PDF p. 232)
- **R-3.415-sale-of-goods-warranty** [sev 3] Treat warrant and warranty for goods in a sale-of-goods contract as a separate function; using represents and warrants there probably won't cause a fight but conflates two confusing concepts. (3.415, 3.453; PDF p. 232)
- **R-3.436-verb-not-remedy** [sev 2] Don't assume that using represents, warrants, or both determines whether an inaccurate statement supports a misrepresentation claim, a breach-of-warranty claim, or both (the 'remedies rationale'); in the U.S. it is of no practical relevance. (3.416, 3.417, 3.418...; PDF p. 232)
- **R-3.443-no-timeframe-rationale** [sev 2] Don't match the verb to the timeframe (represents for past or present facts, warrants for future facts); it is inconsistent with warranty law and makes no semantic sense. (3.437, 3.438, 3.439...; PDF p. 232)
- **R-3.450-english-warrants-only** [sev 2] In contracts governed by English law, don't rely on warrants alone (the standard English practice) to exclude misrepresentation claims and rescission; courts give the restrictive remedies rationale 'significant but not overwhelming support.' Address remedies expressly instead. (3.444, 3.445, 3.446...; PDF p. 232)
- **R-3.451-rw-disorder** [sev 2] Whether it's your draft or the other side's, rearrange provisions so that only statements of fact follow represents and warrants (or states); move obligations and future facts to where they belong. (3.451, 3.452; PDF p. 232)
  - Look for: obligations (will, shall) or predictions inside a list of representations
  - Book example: [Consultant represents and warrants that:] it will use industry best practices to prevent the introduction of any viruses ... . → [book's classification] Obligation; see 3.89.
  - Book example: [Consultant represents and warrants that:] the Services will be performed in a professional manner ... . → [book's classification] Passive-voice obligation; see 3.148.
- **R-3.457-use-states** [sev 3] Don't use represents, warrants, or represents and warrants to introduce statements of fact; use states. Use the noun statement of fact, not representation and warranty, and don't use asserts or confirms. (3.454, 3.455, 3.456...; PDF p. 242)
  - Book example: [13-1b] ×: Acme represents ... . → [13-1] ✓✓: Acme states that the Equipment is listed in schedule A.
  - Book example: [13-1c] ××: Acme represents and warrants ... . → [13-1] ✓✓: Acme states that the Equipment is listed in schedule A.
- **R-3.458-states-series** [sev 3] To introduce a series of statements of fact, say [Party name] states that the following facts are accurate: so a full independent clause precedes the colon. No signal is needed that an inaccurate statement supports a remedy. (3.458; PDF p. 242)
- **R-3.459-verb-remedy-sentence** [sev 3] To allay fears about states, add: The verb used to introduce a statement of fact in this agreement does not affect the remedies available for inaccuracy of that statement of fact. (3.459, 3.460, 3.463; PDF p. 242)
  - HOUSE: partly superseded: House wording, in the active: The verb that this agreement uses to introduce a statement of fact does not affect the remedies available for inaccuracy of that statement of fact.
  - Book example: (none) → The verb used to introduce a statement of fact in this agreement does not affect the remedies available for inaccuracy of that statement of fact.
- **R-3.461-states-pushback** [sev 2] In slow-changing practice areas (one-off M&A deals) or when marking up the other side's draft in higher-volume deals, states may cause more consternation than it's worth; if stuck with represents and warrants in a U.S. contract, rely on a no-reliance provision and add the 3.459 sentence. (3.461, 3.462; PDF p. 242)
- **R-3.464-address-remedies-directly** [sev 2] If remedies for inaccurate statements of fact are an issue, address them explicitly (for example, state that a party may claim misrepresentation, breach of warranty, or both, or that misrepresentation claims are precluded); relying on any combination of represents and warrants is irresponsible. (3.464, 3.465, 3.466...; PDF p. 242)
  - Exceptions: Utility is uncertain: enforceability is mixed; a bare preclusion leaves room for extracontractual claims (use a no-reliance provision); rote limits may not suit the deal; the analysis is speculative; indemnification, liquidated damages, or liability limits may be simpler (3.467-3.471).
- **R-3.474-no-future-facts** [sev 2] Don't present predictions ('future facts') as statements of fact. If the stating party controls the matter, impose an obligation; you can also or instead state a remedy; if it lacks control, allocate the risk (indemnification, liquidated damages, breakup fee). (3.474, 3.475, 3.476...; PDF p. 246)
  - Look for: statement of fact using will about the future
  - Book example: The Units will comply with the Specifications. → The Seller shall sell to the Buyer Units that comply with the Specifications.
  - Book example: The Units will comply with the Specifications. → If a Unit fails to comply with the Specifications, then ... .
- **R-3.481-not-breached** [sev 2] Say a statement of fact is accurate or inaccurate, not breached (you breach an obligation), and don't say a statement can become inaccurate. (3.481, 3.483, 3.484; PDF p. 246)
- **R-3.482-no-cure-of-reps** [sev 2] Don't lump statements of fact with obligations, for example by providing for cure of 'breached' representations; an inaccurate statement can't later be made accurate. (3.482; PDF p. 246)
- **R-3.485-acknowledges** [sev 3] Use acknowledge when the declaring party accepts as accurate a fact offered by another party; it precludes later challenge. Don't use understand, accept, or concede instead. (3.485, 3.486; PDF p. 248)
  - Book example: (none) → Acme acknowledges that the Consultant is in the business of providing services and consulting advice to others.
  - Book example: (none) → Each Shareholder acknowledges that the Merger Shares have not been registered under the Securities Act and are instead being issued under an exemption from registration.
- **R-3.487-recitals-or-acknowledgment** [sev 3] Put background facts in the recitals, which need no language of declaration; if a fact is important, have the declaring party acknowledge it in the body. (3.487; PDF p. 248)
- **R-3.488-acknowledge-not-throat-clearing** [sev 2] Use acknowledge only to introduce a fact asserted by another party, not language that falls in another category; and never combine acknowledge with another verb (acknowledges and agrees). (3.488, 3.490; PDF p. 248)
  - Book example: Each Lender acknowledges that it shall conduct its own independent investigation of the financial condition and affairs of each Borrower. → Each Lender shall conduct its own independent investigation of the financial condition and affairs of each Borrower.
  - Book example: The parties acknowledge that this agreement does not supersede, modify, or otherwise affect the terms of any stock options ... . → This agreement does not supersede, modify, or otherwise affect the terms of any stock options ... .
- **R-3.489-no-unconditionally-acknowledge** [sev 3] Don't use unconditionally acknowledge or expressly acknowledge; it's rhetorical emphasis. (3.489; PDF p. 248)

### Chapter 3: Language of Intention

- **R-3.491-intends-that** [sev 2] When a status can't be addressed definitively because it depends on circumstances after signing (independent-contractor status), state what the parties intend: The parties intend that ... will ... . Don't use obligation, policy, acknowledgment, or shall be construed. (3.491, 3.492, 3.493...; PDF p. 249)
  - Book example: [14-1b] ×: The Consultant will be an independent contractor. → [14-1] ✓✓ [as above]
- **R-3.500-policy-for-governing-law** [sev 3] Use language of policy, not intention, where the contract's choice applies unless a court later finds it fails minimum legal requirements, as with governing law. (3.500, 3.501; PDF p. 249)
  - Book example: The parties intend that New York governs ... . [awkward] → New York law governs this agreement. [figure 1 policy example]

### Chapter 3: Language of Recommendation

- **R-3.502-recommends** [sev 3] A party with greater bargaining power may include a recommendation to the other party using recommends (not advises, which suggests a relationship of trust), without hereby. (3.502, 3.503, 3.504...; PDF p. 252)
  - Exceptions: Encourages and reminds are other possibilities; alternatively, have the other party acknowledge the recommendation or release liability for failing to follow it (3.504-3.505).
  - Book example: [15-1a] ×: The Company advises the Participant to consult with his or her personal legal advisor ... . → [15-1] ✓✓: The Company recommends that the Participant consult with his or her personal legal advisor if the Participant is uncertain whether the insider rules apply.

### Chapter 3: Shall and May in Restrictive Relative Clauses

- **R-3.507-no-shall-relative-clause** [sev 3] Don't use shall in a restrictive relative clause; use the present tense. (3.506, 3.507; PDF p. 253)
  - Book example: [16-1a] ××: ... to any account that Acme shall specify in writing. → [16-1] ✓✓: Jones shall pay the Purchase Price by wire transfer to any account that Acme specifies in writing.
- **R-3.508-after-acquired** [sev 1] With a stative verb in a relative clause (Shares it owns), say explicitly whether after-acquired items are covered, rather than using shall. (3.508; PDF p. 253)
  - Look for: relative clause with own/hold/possess where after-acquired property matters
  - Book example: [16-2a] ××: ... any Shares it shall own. → [16-2b] ✓✓: ... any Shares it currently owns or any additional Shares it acquires.
  - Book example: [16-2] ✓: Other than under the terms of this agreement, no Stockholder may transfer any Shares it owns. → [16-2b] ✓✓ [as above]
- **R-3.509-no-may-relative-clause** [sev 1] Don't use may in a restrictive relative clause; if it means might it is redundant, and it can be read to cover any plausible candidate whether or not the event occurs. Usually just omit may. (3.509, 3.510, 3.511...; PDF p. 253)
  - Book example: [16-3a] ××: If Acme sells Assets to one or more buyers that Singh may introduce to Acme ... . → [16-3] ✓✓: If Acme sells Assets to one or more buyers that Singh introduces to Acme ... .

### Chapter 3: Dual Verb Structures

- **R-3.513-no-dual-verb-structures** [sev 2] Don't use two verb structures with the same verb in one clause (be and hereby is; shall grant and hereby grants; has granted and hereby grants; may but is not required to; has no authority to and shall not; shall and continue to); none the author has seen makes sense. (3.513; PDF p. 255)
  - Look for: two verb structures sharing one main verb
- **R-3.514-be-and-hereby-is** [sev 3] In resolutions, use only performative resolutions (resolved, that ... hereby is authorized), not be and hereby is; in contracts, use only language of performance with hereby (and you can't both amend a contract and express an intention to amend it). (3.514, 3.515, 3.516...; PDF p. 255)
  - Fix: Use only language of performance with hereby (3.519).
  - Book example: Section 1(c) of the Employment Agreement be and hereby is deleted in its entirety and replaced in full by the following: → (see fix)
- **R-3.520-shall-grant-hereby-grants** [sev 2] Don't combine an obligation to grant with a present grant (shall grant, and hereby grants). Put the language of performance in one sentence, and in a separate sentence require the assigning party, at the other party's request, to sign whatever documents are needed if the present assignment proves ineffective. (3.520, 3.521, 3.522...; PDF p. 255)
  - Exceptions: Where no fallback rationale applies (for example, an access grant to real property), the structure is 'just silly' (3.523).
  - Book example: Upon termination, Acme shall grant, and hereby grants, to PharmaCo a nonexclusive, worldwide license ... . → (see fix)
- **R-3.525-has-granted-hereby-grants** [sev 3] Don't say a party has granted and hereby grants; if a belt-and-suspenders fallback is intended, say so in separate sentences. (3.525, 3.526, 3.527; PDF p. 255)
  - Book example: Trustor by executing and delivering this Deed of Trust has granted and hereby grants to Beneficiary, as security for the Debt, a security interest in the Trust Property ... . → (see fix)
- **R-3.529-no-authority-and-shall-not** [sev 3] Don't say a person has no authority to, and shall not, act as agent; use shall not alone (it makes clearer that you have a remedy), and consider stating that the person isn't an agent of the company. (3.529, 3.530; PDF p. 255)
  - Fix: Use shall not alone; a sensible starting point is to state that the person isn't an agent of the company (3.530).
  - Book example: The Contractor has no authority to, and shall not, act as agent for or on behalf of Client ... . → (see fix)
- **R-3.531-no-and-continue-to** [sev 3] Don't add and continue to (or keep, as in insure and keep insured, indemnify and keep indemnified) to an obligation; shall already imposes an ongoing obligation. If timing or duration is in question, address it explicitly. (3.531, 3.532, 3.533...; PDF p. 255)
  - Book example: Acme agrees to pay and continue to pay all fees ... . → (see fix)
  - Book example: the Employee shall and continue to participate ... → (see fix)

### Chapter 3: Not a Category: Standard Terms; Assumptions

- **R-3.536-standard-terms-habitual-present** [sev 2] In unsigned standard terms, don't use will for the company's obligations and must for the customer's ('bizarre'); the habitual present (Acme initiates reimbursement ...) is expedient but ducks the category question and could cause confusion if a provision is disputed. (3.536, 3.537, 3.538...; PDF p. 260)
  - Look for: standard terms describing company duties in the simple present or with will
  - Book example: Acme initiates reimbursement five Business Days after the day it receives the Data File. → (see fix)
  - Book example: Acme calculates payment terms based on the date invoices are delivered. → (see fix)
- **R-3.541-recast-assumptions** [sev 2] Recast each statement-of-work assumption using the appropriate category of contract language (obligation, condition, policy); assumptions leave unclear what happens if they fail. (3.541, 3.542, 3.543; PDF p. 260)

### Chapter 3: Selecting Which Category of Contract Language to Use

- **R-3.545-transfer-on-signing** [sev 1] To transfer rights or assets on signing, use language of performance (Acme hereby assigns ...). Don't rely on language of obligation (shall assign, agrees to assign) or language of policy (will be the property of, will vest in). (3.545; PDF p. 262)
  - Book example: Acme shall assign the Intellectual Property ... . / Acme agrees to assign the Intellectual Property ... . → Acme hereby assigns the Intellectual Property ... .
  - Book example: The Intellectual Property will be the property of Acme ... . → Acme hereby assigns the Intellectual Property ... .
- **R-3.546-amendment-as-condition** [sev 2] Express what is required to amend the contract as a condition; language of obligation, prohibition, or limited discretion presupposes breach and a remedy, which make no sense here. The obligation-based condition is most concise. (3.546; PDF p. 262)
  - Book example: The parties may amend this agreement only by a writing signed by both parties. → To be effective, an amendment to this agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties. [17 words]
  - Book example: The parties shall not amend this agreement, except by written agreement of the parties. → An amendment to this agreement will not be effective unless it is in writing and signed by both parties. [19 words]
- **R-3.547-reliance-on-information** [sev 2] Don't say a party may rely on information the other furnishes; it's free to rely anyway, and discretion doesn't address what happens if the information is inaccurate. Use language of policy allocating liability. (3.547; PDF p. 262)
  - Book example: The Contractor may rely on the accuracy of information furnished by the Owner. → The Owner will be liable for any damages the Contractor incurs arising from inaccurate information the Owner provides to the Contractor.
- **R-3.548-dispute-notice-condition** [sev 1] Express a deadline for disputing an invoice (or similar notice) as a condition, not an obligation; the only sensible consequence of lateness is losing the right to dispute. (3.548; PDF p. 262)
  - Look for: shall submit/give notice within X days, where lateness should forfeit a right
  - Book example: Patterson shall submit any Dispute Notice no later than five Business Days after she receives the related invoice. → Patterson may dispute an invoice if she submits a Dispute Notice no later than five business days after she receives that invoice.
  - Book example: Patterson shall submit any Dispute Notice ... . → To dispute an invoice, Patterson must submit a Dispute Notice no later than five Business Days after she receives the related invoice.
- **R-3.549-unreasonably-withheld-consent** [sev 2] Decide whether a tenant (or other party) should get a remedy for unreasonably withheld consent or permission to proceed; if permission, the clearest form is prohibition plus an exception in language of discretion. (3.549; PDF p. 262)
  - Book example: The Tenant shall not assign this lease without the Landlord's consent, which the Landlord shall not unreasonably withhold. [remedy for breach] → The Tenant shall not assign this lease without the Landlord's consent, except that the Tenant may assign this lease if the Landlord unreasonably withholds consent. [permission]

### Chapter 13: Selected Usages

- **R-13.208-deem** [sev 3] Use deem only to create a legal fiction: to treat a thing as what it is not or might not be, or as having qualities it does not or might not have. Don't use deem where no legal fiction is established. (13.208, 13.211; PDF p. 624)
  - Test: Is the provision treating something as what it is not, or might not be? If not, delete deemed (to be).
  - Fix: Delete deemed to be; state the consequence directly.
  - Book example: Any violation ... will be deemed to be a breach by Acme of this section 4.2. → Any violation ... will be a breach by Acme of this section 4.2.
  - Book example: The date of exercise will be deemed to be the date on which the Company receives notice of exercise. → The date of exercise will be the date on which the Company receives notice of exercise.
- **R-13.209-deem-will-passive** [sev 3] Use deem with will, not shall (Any notice will be deemed to have been received on delivery), and in the passive, not the active voice (not The parties will deem). (13.209, 13.210; PDF p. 624)
  - HOUSE: Active voice only. Recast passives: name the actor (any Person if unknown); legal fictions as 'This agreement treats X as' or a linking verb ('is effective'); amendments as 'The parties hereby amend the section titled ...'. See house-style.md section 1.
  - Test: Has-a-duty test: substitute 'has a duty to'; shall be deemed fails.
  - Fix: will be deemed
- **R-13.212-deem-release** [sev 2] To make a release automatic, use deem instead of language of obligation: instead of If X, Acme shall release Widgetco, say If X, Acme will be deemed to have released Widgetco. (13.212, 13.213, 13.214; PDF p. 625)
  - Fix: Acme will be deemed to have released Widgetco
- **R-13.438-shall-indemnify** [sev 3] Use shall indemnify (language of obligation), not hereby indemnifies (language of performance); don't combine the two. (13.438, 13.439; PDF p. 676)
  - Fix: shall indemnify
- **R-13.460-will-be-liable-for** [sev 3] Consider not using indemnify in the operative provisions: say what is covered, for example will be liable to those Acme Persons for, rather than shall indemnify those Acme Indemnitees against. An interim step is to use indemnification only as a heading. This wording also attracts less attention from counterparties skittish about indemnification obligations; but pick your battles. (13.460, 13.461, 13.462...; PDF p. 681)
  - Fix: will be liable to [persons] for [losses]
- **R-13.937-warrants-statement-of-fact** [sev 2] Use states instead of warrants to introduce a statement of fact (consistent with states instead of represents); represents and warrants is pointless and confusing. A statement needn't be called a warranty to be one. (13.937, 13.938, 13.939...; PDF p. 792)
  - Fix: states
- **R-13.945-warranty-remedy-conditional** [sev 3] In sale-of-goods contracts, you can instead combine the warranty and its remedy in one sentence: make the actual or future fact a conditional clause and the remedy the matrix clause. (13.945, 13.946, 13.947; PDF p. 794)
  - Look for: product warranty followed by a separate remedy provision
  - Fix: If at any time during the six months after the date of this agreement the Equipment does not conform to the Specifications, the Vendor shall modify or replace the Equipment.
- **R-13.948-warrants-obligation** [sev 2] Don't use warrants to introduce an obligation (Acme warrants that it shall perform routine maintenance); it is throat-clearing. (13.948; PDF p. 795)
  - Fix: Acme shall perform routine maintenance ...
- **R-13.949-warranty-noun** [sev 3] The noun warranty may refer to provisions in a contract or absent from it (Acme makes no warranties, express or implied), and it is appropriate to head a section Warranty or Warranties if that is what it consists of. But whether a provision is a warranty depends on content, not label. (13.949, 13.950, 13.951; PDF p. 795)
  - Fix: Keep the noun and heading; don't rely on the label.
