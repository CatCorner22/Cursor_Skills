# MSCD contracts

This skill makes contract language say 1 thing, clearly, the way Ken Adams's *A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting* (MSCD5, 2023) prescribes, as updated by his blog through October 2026 and adjusted by Blake's house style. It works in 4 modes: **Review** (a findings report), **Redline** (tracked changes in the .docx), **Comment** (Word comments), and **Draft** (a new contract). Every mode runs the same analysis and cites the MSCD paragraph or blog post behind every change.

2 principles drive everything:

- **Ambiguity is never acceptable; vagueness stays only on purpose.** Ambiguity is text that can express 2 or more inconsistent meanings (MSCD 7.5). It causes disputes, so the skill removes every instance it finds. MSCD concedes that a drafter can't realistically eliminate every ambiguity of the part versus the whole (7.5); where you can't remove one, flag it. Vagueness (a standard that depends on circumstances, such as *reasonable*) is sometimes the right tool, so the skill flags it and keeps it only for a stated reason.
- **Annotate first; never change meaning silently.** Adams doubts that technology can turn a traditional contract into an MSCD-compliant draft unaided, and suggests "explanatory annotations," leaving fixes to the user ("Could You Use Artificial Intelligence to Check Whether a Contract Complies with MSCD?", mid-2023). So every change comes with its reason. A redline changes only wording whose meaning is certain; anything that needs a choice of meaning or a business term becomes a comment for Blake's decision.

Blake wants to understand the mechanics of every fix, so every internal finding explains the grammar or reasoning behind it, not just the replacement wording. A finding counts only if its fix is right: the fix solves the problem, follows MSCD and the house rules, keeps the deal's meaning, and creates no new problem.

## Precedence: which source wins

When sources conflict, follow the 1st one that addresses the point, and say in the finding which one you followed.

1. **Blake's instruction for the matter.**
2. **Law that requires specific wording**: when Blake identifies it, or when it is a common case listed under Hard cases (a UCC disclaimer naming MERCHANTABILITY in capitals; a negotiable note's *promises to pay to the order of*; *work made for hire*). Wording a statute or rule requires isn't a drafting choice, so keep it even where a house rule would change it, and flag it. Don't research or decide law.
3. **Blake's house style** (`references/house-style.md`). Blake's rule: his way always prevails over MSCD.
4. **Adopted riders** (`references/riders.md`): blog posts in which Adams changes MSCD5.
5. **MSCD5** (`data/rules.jsonl` and the reference files).
6. **Blog elaborations** that extend MSCD5 without changing it.
7. **Proposals** Adams only floats (language of inaction, language of state of mind, *human* for *individual*, *afterwards*, descriptive headings instead of *Miscellaneous*): note them; don't apply them.
8. **Standard English** per *The Chicago Manual of Style*, where everything above is silent.

## Stage 2: Analysis

Run these passes in order; later passes use what earlier ones learn. A sentence can hold several problems: after you record a finding, run the remaining passes on the same sentence and on your fix's wording (*5% of the overdue installment* keeps the money-formula problem; *located in the Territory* keeps the reach problem; a new cap that is Blake's business call still gets the each-or-aggregate check). When 2 findings change the same words, write 1 combined wording that fixes both and put it in the finding with the higher severity (if equal, the earlier pass). The other finding keeps its own Why and Source; its Fix reads *wording: see finding N*. Both stay in `findings.json`.

**Before you flag anything under an MSCD rule,** read its record (`rules_query.py --id`) and confirm the text matches the rule's own pattern and example: *may ... only if* is MSCD's approved form (3.363-3.364), though 3.222 warns about *may ... only*; MSCD 16.34 targets a party's acknowledgment, not every sentence near a disclaimer. If both readings give the same result, there is no ambiguity, so don't flag it.

0. **Lint.**
   - *Run it.* Run `python3 <skill folder>/scripts/mscd_lint.py <file> --inventory --whose <blake|other-side> --json lint.json`. It reads .docx, .txt, and .md: save pasted text to a .txt file first. For a PDF with a text layer, extract its text with `pdftotext -layout` to a .txt; extraction can collapse 2 spaces into 1 and loses fonts and alignment, so don't report spacing or typography from extracted text: check it on the rendered page, or say it wasn't checked. For a single clause or a fragment, leave out `--inventory` (a fragment has no definitions), review only what Blake asked about, and say that the rest of the contract wasn't reviewed.
   - *What the report lists.* Read the report's notes at the top: they say what the lint couldn't read (an encoding, Word's numbering, text it decoded badly). The report lists *candidates* in 2 groups: direct findings (house rules, MSCD's signature phrases such as *and/or*, *best efforts*, *within 30 days of*, the outline, and the defined-term inventory), which are usually right and carry their fix, and rule triggers, which point you at sentences to check in passes 3 to 9 and are often not problems in a clean draft (the attachments note, 1 per document, sits with them: it asks you to run the schedule-versus-exhibit procedure; it names no problem). Broad triggers (every *shall*, every *may*) appear once as counts. The lint reads a .docx with tracked changes as if the reader had accepted them; the report says so and lists each change with its author and text (deleted text isn't in the paragraphs, so review it from that list).
   - *Judge each candidate.* Confirm or reject each candidate in context; the script can't read meaning. For a candidate that may be correct text that only looks wrong, search `references/look-alikes.md`. On a traditional template expect hundreds of candidates, most of them real.
   - *Read the text from lint.json.* Read the contract's text from the lint's paragraphs (`paragraphs` in lint.json), not from a python-docx dump: the lint reads tracked insertions as accepted, and a dump drops them. The paragraph numbers count every paragraph in order: body, table cells, text boxes, then headers, footers, and notes. The lint skips a harness note at the top of a test file (*Draft of ...; Blake's client: ...*), so its paragraph reads as blank. `coverage.py` counts each place a direct finding fires (a finding repeated in 12 paragraphs is 12 places); it doesn't count rule triggers.
1. **Structure.** Front of the contract, body, back, attachments; enumeration in the house scheme; headings; cross-references (house rule: avoid them, and refer by heading). `structure.md`, `attachments.md`. An operative provision in a schedule moves into the body (`attachments.md`, step 6 says where).
2. **Defined terms.** From the inventory: terms defined twice, unused, undefined (role labels such as *the Receiving Party* included), used in 2 forms, circular, stuffed with obligations, or defined with *shall mean*; *this Agreement* defined as a term (2.123); party names take no article (house rule 11). Check every defined term against the terms of art of contract law (`data/terms-of-art.json`; house rule 13): a term of art keeps its legal meaning, so an *Assignment* that means a work order, a software *Release*, or an *Uptime Guarantee* needs a new name (severity 1 when the contract also uses the word in its legal sense). `defined-terms.md`.
3. **Categories, sentence by sentence.** Assign every sentence in the body a category of contract language and check its verb (`verbs.md`): the *has a duty* test on every *shall*; *must* only for a duty on someone other than the subject; *may* only for discretion; never *shall* with a negative subject; active voice in every clause. Pick the tense of every policy you write or keep: in force on signing or at a fixed date, the present tense; tied to an event that may not happen or whose timing is uncertain, or in a matrix clause after *if*, *when*, or *unless*, *will* (MSCD 3.334; 3.361); that includes a notice's effectiveness (*A notice will be effective when the recipient receives it*, MSCD's own form; 13.209), an amendment's or waiver's (*will not be effective unless*, 3.369), and the agreement's own on signing (*will become effective when all parties have signed it*, 5.25), while a standing allocation keeps the present (*No Member is liable for the Company's debts*). Recitals take no category: *will* there states the future, not a duty, and isn't a finding (MSCD 2.130-2.132); a purpose recital's verb is *wants*, never *intends to* (2.146-2.147). A correct category stays even when another would serve the client better: *is not required to* (3.292) isn't a prohibition in disguise, and wanting a prohibition is a deal point for Blake.
4. **Ambiguity.** Check every sentence against every cause in `ambiguity-catalog.md`, including the riders (right-node-raising pivot ambiguity; *each* or *in the aggregate*; the specific versus the general). For each ambiguity, list every candidate before you propose a fix: for a pronoun, every singular noun in its sentence and the sentence before; for a modifier, each noun it can reach; for *within N days of*, before and after. N meanings listed means N wordings offered. A meaning drops out only when you name the provision that excludes it, never because it reads less naturally or the context favors the other. Then the fix must either give wording for each meaning and ask Blake which he intends, or name the provision that rules a meaning out. A fix that quietly adopts 1 meaning is wrong, even when that meaning is the likelier one ("each laptop and desktop that Provider leases" silently settles whether *leased* reaches *desktops*). A period that runs from a notice or demand must say whether it runs from giving it or from receiving it.
5. **Vagueness.** Flag each vague standard (*reasonable*, *prompt*, *adequate*, *material*, *substantial*, *satisfactory*) and classify it: keep on purpose, replace, or ask (`vagueness.md`). Keeping it is Blake's call, not yours: offer the measure the contract already supplies (a period it states elsewhere, a manual's levels, the Documentation, the sections of a deliverable) or a bracketed number, and ask. Rule *keep* only when no measure exists (*reasonable efforts*, *substantially all*), and say why none does; a reason that a measure would hurt a party is a business judgment and isn't yours to make.
6. **Words to avoid.** Every hit in `words-to-avoid.md`, judged in context. Never *should*, in any sense (house rule 12): give the replacement for its sense (*shall*, *may*, the present tense in a condition, a duty or fact for an expectation, a defined Knowledge term for *should have known*). Every term of art used as an ordinary word in another sense (*work assignment*, *marketing collateral*, *new release*): replace it with the plain word (house rule 13). *Covenant* for an obligation becomes *obligation*, grouped obligations included (*Financial Covenants*; MSCD 3.186-3.188), but *covenant not to sue* and *restrictive covenant* are terms of art and stay, and a reference that quotes a heading (*the section titled "Financial Covenants"*) follows the heading: fix the heading.
7. **Time, numbers, money.** `time-and-numbers.md`; house rules: digits for every number, January 1, 2027. Check the arithmetic: installments against the total, percentages against amounts, each table row against the body, every date against its weekday and against the date of the agreement (a deadline that falls before signing is impossible, not a count question). A money formula takes *an amount equal to* and *minus* (14.22-14.24). An amount stated twice (a body fee and a schedule total; *$84,000 ($7,000 per month)*) is a finding even when the copies agree: each option keeps 1 copy (MSCD 1.56), and when the copies disagree each option both reconciles and deletes 1 copy.
8. **Consistency.** 1 word for 1 meaning across the whole document, attachments included (1.57-1.60). Compare each provision with every other provision on the same subject, however far apart: a payment term in the body against a schedule, a definition against its use in an exhibit, an order-of-precedence clause against a specific provision.
9. **Standard English and typography.** `standard-english.md`; the serial comma (12.59); house rules: 2 spaces between sentences, 1 after a colon, 12-point Aptos, ragged right, clean visual lines. The front: title centered in capitals (2.2), party names in capitals in the introductory clause (2.51), the lead-in (2.159), defined terms in bold where defined (16.20). A .txt file carries no font, alignment, or bold, and a .md file carries only bold (`**...**`): say once in the report which typography wasn't checked, and don't guess. Leave *date* and *day* alone unless 1 causes confusion (10.16). MSCD lets you omit the concluding clause (5.5), so a contract without one has no problem to report. A letter agreement has no introductory clause or lead-in: apply MSCD chapter 19 to its front and back instead (`rules_query.py --para 19.3`).

**Before you write any fix, read `references/fix-patterns.md`.** In blind tests, most failed fixes changed the deal, rewrote correct text, answered a different problem, or dropped a reading; the file shows each mistake with a fix that passes, and ends with 8 questions to ask of every fix along with the 6 tests in `fix-check.md`.

Record each finding in this form (it feeds every mode):

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Location | The section's heading (and number, if the document has one) and the quoted words |
| Kind and severity | 1 must fix (ambiguity, or a category or usage error that can change meaning); 2 fix (vagueness, dispute risk); 3 style. See the severity policy below |
| Meanings | For an ambiguity, each possible meaning in plain words |
| Fix | The replacement wording, in house style |
| Why | The mechanism (grammar, category, or risk) in 1 or 2 sentences |
| Source | Rule id and MSCD paragraph, rider post, or house rule (noting where MSCD differs) |
| Decision needed | Yes, with options, when the fix needs a business term or a choice of meaning |

**Severity policy.** Apply the policy in `house-style.md` under 'Severity of a house-rule finding' (the lint applies the same policy: for a lint finding use the lint's severity unless the context changes it, and say why). `rules_query.py --id`, which gives a rule record's severity, also accepts a paragraph number, such as `--id 3.358`.
- Context: if the context removes the problem (the 2nd reading can't arise here), the candidate isn't a finding; reject it with the reason. Lower a severity only when the lint's or the rule's severity assumes a fact that is false here (2 possible actors, when only 1 party could act; 2 copies that disagree, when they agree), and name that fact in the finding. A finding with 2 surviving readings stays 1; a house-rule breach keeps its rule's severity however minor it looks.
- A defined term used in lowercase is a 2nd form (severity 1) only when the drafter intends the defined meaning. A common word in its ordinary sense (*business hours* beside a defined *Business*) isn't a finding (MSCD 6.11), and neither is a party's common-noun term used in its ordinary sense (*a company that acquires Company* beside the defined *Company*; rider R-rider-lowercase-party-noun).

## Stage 2b: Check the fixes

**Account for every lint finding before you deliver.** Keep your findings in `findings.json` as you work (a list of `{"quote": exact contract words, "type", "severity", "fix", "source"}`; a finding that repeats with 1 fix everywhere can add `"check": "<lint check>", "all": true` instead of 1 object per place), and put each lint direct finding whose text you checked and found correct in `rejected.json` (`{"para", "match", "check", "reason"}`). The `fix` field holds the proposed wording first, then the explanation and any question to Blake. The checker rules on all of it, so the explanation follows the house writing rules too. `rejected.json` holds lint direct findings only. A rule trigger you reject needs no entry: `coverage.py` ignores triggers and says so in its summary line. Mention a rejected trigger only in the report's list of checks that found nothing, with the reason when the call was close. Then run `python3 <skill folder>/scripts/coverage.py lint.json findings.json --rejected rejected.json`. It lists every direct finding that no finding or rejection accounts for. Long reviews drop real lint findings while the reader works on harder problems, so resolve each one listed and run it again until it reports 0 missing. When 1 decision covers every place a check fires (a serial comma added in every list), 1 entry with `"check": "<lint check>", "all": true` and its fix or reason accounts for all of them; list the places in the report. For a 1-clause question, skip this step.

**Check every fix before you deliver it.** Run every proposed wording (each fix, option, redline insertion, and suggested sentence) through the 6 tests in `references/fix-check.md`, 'The 6 tests': Rules, Whole problem, The deal, No new problem, Options, In place. Lint each wording first (a .txt, 1 paragraph per wording, no `--inventory`).

**Then get the fixes checked independently.** For a review, redline, or comment set (not a 1-clause question), if you can start a subagent, give it `references/fix-check.md` and the paths it names (the contract, `findings.json`, `references/house-style.md`, `scripts/rules_query.py`) and this skill's folder path, so it can open `look-alikes.md`, `terms-of-art.json`, and the lint. It rules each finding `pass`, `fail` (with the test), or `keep` (the finding proposes changing text that is correct). Fix every `fail`; drop every `keep` you agree with (move it to `rejected.json` with the reason; for a finding the lint never fired on, set `"check"` to `"pass-N"`, which `coverage.py` ignores and the report keeps); then, on each changed fix, lint the new wording again (a .txt, 1 paragraph per wording, no `--inventory`) before the other 5 tests, because a reworded fix that no lint has read is an unchecked fix; then run `coverage.py` again until it reports 0 missing. A flag whose fix is already `keep` (a legal question for Blake, wording a statute requires) stays in the findings: it passes when the flag is accurate. Where you disagree with a ruling, say why in the finding. Without subagents, do the same check yourself, cold, after you draft the findings: read `fix-check.md` and rule on each finding in turn.

## Stage 3: Output by mode

### Review
Write the report in the form in `comment-templates.md`: a short summary, then findings sorted by severity and location, with wording cleanup, ambiguity fixes, and substantive changes kept apart. Group a repeated finding into 1 row with all its locations.

### Redline
Use the docx skill's workflow for editing an existing document (read its SKILL.md: "Editing existing documents", "Tracked changes" and "Comments"; find its folder from the skill listing; on claude.ai it is usually `/mnt/skills/public/docx`).
1. Work on a copy; never modify Blake's original. Unzip it, strip symlinks, and run the docx skill's `merge_runs.py`, as that workflow says.
2. Make only changes whose meaning is certain. Where a fix needs a choice of meaning or a business term, leave the text, insert a bracketed blank if needed, and put the options in a comment.
3. Track every change as Blake's AI Agent and change only the words that differ; the markup rules are in `references/docx-mechanics.md`.
4. Leave the other side's tracked changes alone: don't edit inside their `<w:ins>` or `<w:del>`. Put your change beside theirs, and say so in a comment, or comment instead of editing.
5. Carry each change through: a renamed defined term changes at every use; a moved provision updates every heading reference to it. Keep an old-to-new numbering map when converting to the I., A., 1., (a), (i) scheme, and deliver it with the redline. When typed numbers must become the house scheme, don't track every renumbered paragraph; say so in 1 comment, deliver the map, and offer to convert to Word's native numbering in the clean copy. A font set in a style doesn't show as a tracked change: flag it in 1 comment.
6. Zip the folder and validate it as a redline, then make the clean copy from it and validate that too; the commands and the fallback when validation fails are in `references/docx-mechanics.md`. The clean copy accepts every tracked change, the other side's too; comments already in the file stay in it.
7. Add your comments to `tracked.docx` to make the redline (see Comment, below), and validate it again as a redline (`references/docx-mechanics.md`).
8. Read both files back: run the lint on the clean copy and confirm that each edited sentence reads as you intended, with no new direct finding in an edited paragraph. Render both to images and look at them: the validator checks text, not numbering or formatting.
9. Deliver the redline, the clean copy, and the findings report. If the file arrived with the other side's tracked changes, say that the clean copy accepts theirs and yours alike, and list their changes in the report with a recommendation on each.

### Comment
Anchor 1 comment to the exact words of each finding with the docx skill's `scripts/comment.py`, as Blake's AI Agent with initials BAR-AI (never BAA); the command's flags, the markers, and line breaks inside a comment are in `references/docx-mechanics.md`. Start each comment as `comment-templates.md` shows for the reader: use the internal form for Blake and the external form for the other side, and never mix the 2 forms in 1 file; when a file with internal comments is going to the other side, deliver a 2nd copy with external comments instead. Comments can accompany a redline: put the reason for each tracked change in its comment. Validate the file afterward.

### Draft
1. Take in the term sheet, notes, or old form. List every deal point you can't find as a question for Blake; never invent a deal term, number, date, or party detail.
2. Outline to `structure.md`: title; introductory clause; recitals only if there's a back story; the lead-in *The parties agree as follows:* (with recitals, *The parties therefore agree as follows:*, 2.159); the body in the house enumeration with a bold heading for every section; definitions where chapter 6 places them; concluding clause; signature blocks; Schedules, then Exhibits.
3. Write each provision by choosing its category first, then its verb (`verbs.md`), in the active voice, with digits. Never write *should* (house rule 12), and never use a term of art for anything but its legal meaning, as a defined term or otherwise (house rule 13; `data/terms-of-art.json`).
4. Run all 10 passes (0 to 9) on your own draft and fix every finding; list any severity 2 or 3 candidate you reject, with the reason, under the open questions.
5. **Get an independent review.** If you can start a subagent, give it the draft, `references/house-style.md`, and this file's Stage 2, and ask it to check every sentence and return findings; it hasn't seen your drafting, so it won't share your blind spots. Without subagents, reread the whole draft cold, pass by pass. Fix what the review finds, then rerun the lint.
6. Build the .docx with the docx skill (12-point Aptos, native multilevel numbering, ragged right), render it, look at it, and deliver it with the open questions.

## Hard cases

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| *Must* states what a nonparty or a thing must do or be (*The arbitrator must issue the award ...*; *The Escrow Agent must release ...* when the escrow agent doesn't sign) | **Correct *must*.** Correct when no party can do the act itself (MSCD 3.154; 3.166): keep it. If the party relying on the nonparty has no remedy when it doesn't act, say so with fix `keep`, and offer, for Blake to choose, what happens then (MSCD 3.166).<br>**A party could act.** If a party could do the act itself (*Each Acme employee must sign a confidentiality agreement*), the fix is the restructure: *Acme shall enter into a confidentiality agreement with each of its employees* (MSCD 3.165).<br>**The banned verbs.** Never *shall cause*, *shall ensure*, *shall require*, *shall procure*, *shall see to it*, *shall have [the nonparty] [act]*, *shall not permit*, *shall not allow*, or *shall prevent* for a nonparty the party doesn't control (MSCD 3.162-3.163; 3.176-3.177); control means an instrumentality such as a subsidiary, and a counterparty the party picks (a contractor, an insurer, a subcontractor) is still a nonparty. A body the party acts through is not a nonparty: *Company shall cause the Board to approve* is correct (MSCD 3.160-3.161; question 20 for Blake). Leave it, or offer *Company, acting through the Board, shall approve* (the *acting through* form of MSCD 2.80) as a style option.<br>**_Shall obtain_.** *Shall obtain* or *shall secure* [a nonparty's consent, approval, or document] is different: MSCD 8.5 treats it as a valid unqualified duty of result whose risk sits on the obligated party. Don't call it a category error; flag it (severity 3) when Blake's client bears it, and offer the efforts standard (*shall use reasonable efforts to obtain*) or a condition as the alternative for Blake to choose.<br>**Lead with the minimal fix** (*must* on the nonparty, 3.166, or a limited-control duty such as *shall request* or *shall include the deadline in the engagement letter*, 3.165), and offer a risk-allocation term only as an added bracketed option. A nonparty's prohibition (*No subcontractor shall ...*) gets the same treatment: offer the nonparty form with *may* (*No subcontractor may ...*, house rule 8) and, as a bracketed alternative for Blake, the party's duty with a standard (*[shall use reasonable efforts to cause] [is liable to Buyer for]*); never weaken it silently to *shall instruct* or a narrower duty.<br>**Never delete the provision**: the requirement survives, on the party (*the Receiving Party shall ensure that each Permitted Recipient ...* is also wrong; write *shall not disclose ... to any Permitted Recipient unless that recipient has agreed in writing to ...*) |

## Refreshing the knowledge

The knowledge is current through Adams's blog post of October 22, 2026. When Blake asks to refresh, or a question turns on a recent change: read every post published since that date in the blog's Categories of Contract Language and Ambiguity archives (not only the newest), classify each as rider, proposal, or elaboration, add riders to `data/rules.jsonl` and `references/riders.md` (steps in `references/rule-database.md`), and run `scripts/build_references.py`. Never apply a proposal as a rule. Make the change in the skill's master copy (the GitHub repository CatCorner22/Cursor_Skills, `skills/legal/mscd-contracts`), not in an installed copy, then repackage.

## Limits to state honestly

- The lint script finds candidates; it can't read meaning. On MSCD's Appendix A redraft, the script alone flags 77% of the changes Adams made (146 of 190 locatable footnotes); the rest come from the analysis passes.
- The rule database holds 597 book examples: 560 match the book's text, 34 failed the match, and 3 have no check. Quote only verified examples as the book's wording.
- The skill doesn't verify law or caselaw. Never cite a case that nobody has verified, even 1 that Blake reports.
