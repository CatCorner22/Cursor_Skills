## Summary

A focused evidence review supporting the "One Way Optimal Language" style manual. It covers how patients remember and misread health information, how clinicians read obligation words in guidelines, how to present numbers and risk, pharmacy-label standards, plain-language and accessibility standards, neurodivergent and other vulnerable readers, and the U.S. legal floor for accessibility and language access as of November 2026.

## Key concepts

- **Memory:** patients forget 40–80% of medical information almost immediately and misremember about half of what they keep (Kessels 2003). Primacy and perceived importance raise recall; specific, concrete advice beats general advice; explicit announced categories improve recall while mere logical order does not (Ley 1973, 1979; Kessels 2003).
- **Gist:** patients rely on bottom-line meaning within minutes (Reyna, fuzzy-trace theory).
- **Literacy (PIAAC 2023):** 28% of U.S. adults at or below literacy Level 1 (19% in 2017); 34% at or below numeracy Level 1.
- **Label misreading (Davis 2006, n=395):** 46% misunderstood ≥1 of 5 labels; low-literacy patients: 70.7% stated "Take two tablets by mouth twice daily" correctly but 34.7% demonstrated the right daily count.
- **USP <17>:** separate dose from timing, numerals, explicit times (e.g., "Take 2 tablets in the morning and 2 tablets in the evening"), purpose for use, ≥12-pt Times Roman or 11-pt Arial for critical info, avoid "take as directed" without supplemental information. Universal Medication Schedule = morning, noon, evening, bedtime.
- **Deontic terms (Lomotan 2010, n=445):** *must* median 100 (IQR 5); *may* 37; *may consider* 33; *should*, *is recommended*, *is indicated*, *should consider* intermediate with wide overlapping IQRs. Authors: use three levels (must/should/may) that match recommendation strength; avoid *consider*; reserve *must* for legal standards or imminent harm.
- **Ambiguity vs. vagueness in guidelines (Codish & Shiffman 2005):** both reduce adherence and create inappropriate variation; tri-axial model.
- **GRADE (Andrews 2013):** strong = "we recommend"; weak/conditional = "we suggest."
- **Federal plain language / OFR:** *must* (obligation), *must not* (prohibition), *may* (discretion), *should* (recommendation); don't use *shall*.
- **Risk formats:** readers understand natural frequencies better than probabilities (Cochrane, Akl 2011, SMD 0.69); readers perceive RRR as larger and more persuasive than ARR without understanding it better; NNT reduces understanding; icon arrays/bar graphs help (Zipkin 2014).
- **Verbal frequency labels:** EU terms (*very common*, *common*, *rare*) produce large overestimates (Berry, Knapp, Raynor 2002–2004); e.g., *common* → 34.2% estimate vs. 2.5% actual.
- **Jargon (Gotlieb 2022, n=215):** most readers understood "negative cancer screening"; only ~21% understood that an "impressive" X-ray means bad news.
- **Passive voice:** offline misinterpretation of passives (Ferreira 2003; Christianson 2010); lower-ability readers lean on heuristics (Stella & Engelhardt 2022); some online studies show no reading-time cost for fluent adults.
- **Pictures (Houts 2006):** pictures that go with the text raise attention, comprehension (for relationships/spatial info), recall, and adherence; low-literacy readers benefit most; emotional pictures can backfire.
- **Teach-back (Talevski 2020):** effective in 19 of 20 studies.
- **Assessment tools:** CDC Clear Communication Index (20 items; item 6 = active voice in main message and call to action); AHRQ PEMAT (≥70% conventional threshold); ISO 24495-1:2023 defines plain language by outcomes, not formulas; many sources cite the AMA/NIH 6th–8th grade targets; formulas disagree (SMOG vs. FK).
- **Notes and bias:** stigmatizing chart language worsened attitudes and pain management in a vignette RCT (Goddu 2018); 10.5% of 22,959 note readers reported that a note seemed to judge or offend them (Fernández 2021); Cures Act 2021 rule made notes immediately accessible.
- **Consent:** revised Common Rule requires a "concise and focused presentation of the key information" first (45 CFR 46.116(a)(5)(i)).
- **Neurodivergence:** autistic adults report communication as a top barrier (Doherty 2022: 80% vs. 37% reported difficulty visiting a GP); request clearer instructions, written supplements, slower pace, more detail (Autism in Adulthood 2022); AASPIRE: be specific and precise, avoid idioms and ambiguity. Dyslexia: spacing > typeface; no single dyslexia font (BDA; Dyslexia Scotland). COGA: common words, simple tense, active voice, literal language, no double negatives or nested clauses, <25-word sentences. Easy Read: readers value it, but the evidence of effectiveness is weak (Chinn & Homeyard 2017; Wilson 2026). This research found no ADHD-specific evidence for written health materials.
- **Older adults:** NIA/NLM 12–14 pt body text; few recommendations.
- **Legal floor (Nov 2026):** HHS moved the §504 WCAG 2.1 AA dates to 11 May 2027 (≥15 employees) / 10 May 2028 (FR Doc. 2026-09266); DOJ moved the ADA Title II dates to 26 April 2027 / 26 April 2028 (DOJ IFR eff. 20 April 2026); §1557 Notice of Availability in top 15 state LEP languages, qualified translators, human review of machine translation for critical/accurate/complex text (45 CFR 92.11, 92.201(c)(3)); language-access provisions survived the 2025–2026 partial vacatur.
- **Joint Commission "Do Not Use" list:** U, IU, QD, QOD, trailing zero, missing leading zero, MS, MSO4, MgSO4.

## Relationships

- **Agrees with:** mscd-5th-edition on consistency, active voice, *might* for possibility, explicit time, avoiding verbal probability words, no emphasis by capitals, restructuring over patching.
- **Contradicts:** mscd-5th-edition on *shall* (vs. *must*), words for 1–10 (vs. numerals), third person (vs. *you/we*), imperative mood, and permitted passives. Internal contradictions: serif vs. sans serif (BDA vs. NIA print guidance vs. MSCD familiarity view); passive difficulty (offline vs. online studies); negative framing improves understanding (Cochrane) vs. general advice to avoid negatives (applies to syntax, not framing).
