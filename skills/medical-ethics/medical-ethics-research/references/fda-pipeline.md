## 1. Headline status (as of 2026-11-09)

- **The FDA has approved no classic psychedelic (5-HT2A agonist) or entactogen for any indication.** [S: Becker's Hospital Review, Sept. 2026 hearing coverage; no approval found in the 2026-11-09 sweep.]
  - Esketamine (Spravato, J&J) is FDA-approved: for treatment-resistant depression (2019) and for depressive symptoms with acute suicidal ideation (2020) [M]. It is an NMDA antagonist. It is usually *not* classed as a classic psychedelic, but it is the closest regulatory precedent: REMS, certified sites, and 2-hour post-dose monitoring [M].
- **Three psychedelic new drug applications (NDAs) are reported to be under FDA review:**
  1. Compass COMP360 psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression (TRD). The company announced its NDA submission complete on 2026-11-09 [V: Compass press release via Psychiatric Times, 2026-11-09].
  2. Resilient Pharmaceuticals (formerly Lykos) MDMA for PTSD. Resubmission *reported* 2026-08-09 (the date of Psychedelic Alpha's report, not a confirmed filing date), without a new Phase 3 trial [S: Psychedelic Alpha; MAPS statement 2026-08-10 quotes Doblin that it was submitted without a new Phase 3 study]. No FDA acceptance, resubmission class, or PDUFA date found as of 2026-11-09.
  3. Usona Institute psilocybin for major depressive disorder (MDD). Reported filed about 2026-11-02 [U: Psychedelic Alpha; Usona declined comment; FDA has not confirmed].
- **Two programs hold Commissioner's National Priority Vouchers (CNPV) and have filed NDAs:** Compass and (reportedly) Usona. The voucher targets a 1–2-month review instead of the usual 10–12 months [V: FDA announcement coverage, 2026-04-24]. Compass guides to a U.S. launch in the first half of 2027. That launch is subject to FDA approval *and* DEA rescheduling, and federal rescheduling must be followed by state rescheduling [V: Compass Q3 2026 8-K; S: Seeking Alpha call summary].

### 2.1 Key content of the 2026 final psychedelic guidance

[S: King & Spalding 2026-08-05; CASRAI; Applied Clinical Trials; cogres.com]:
- **Same evidentiary standard as any drug.** The final guidance encourages "innovative approaches for control groups," which the 2023 draft did not say.
- **Functional unblinding:**
  - Recommends active comparators, such as lower doses of the psychedelic or other psychoactive drugs.
  - Recommends blinded central raters.
  - Recommends blinding-integrity and expectancy assessments.
- **Adverse events:** some expected psychoactive effects, such as euphoria, hallucinations, and perceptual and cognitive changes, should be recorded as adverse events. This is the direct lesson of the Lykos CRL.
- **REMS:** the 2023 draft treated a REMS as the exception. The final guidance tells sponsors to "consider the potential need" for a REMS and anticipates postmarketing safety assessments.
- **Public-health effects:** benefit-risk may include effects on nonmedical use, substance use disorder, and accidental exposure.
- **Dosing sessions:** require direct observation and a plan for managing acute distress. Driving and impairment should be assessed over time.
- **Pharmacology:** 5-HT2B functional activity must be assessed for serotonergic drugs, because of the risk of cardiac valvulopathy.
- **Abuse-potential testing:** a human abuse-potential study "may not be scientifically necessary" in some cases. Self-administration and conditioned-place-preference studies are usually not needed.

### 2a. The Commissioner's National Priority Voucher (CNPV) program: facts and controversy

Use this section for any question about the vouchers. These facts are tagged; the arguments are attributed. Present both sides (see psychedelic-ethics-issues.md §7).

**Design.**
- Announced June 2025 by Commissioner Makary. First awards 2025-10-16.
- Targets a 1–2-month review after a complete application, against the usual 10–12 months.
- Vouchers are non-transferable.
- **Not created by statute or regulation.** Statutory priority review vouchers, by contrast, are legislated.
- [V/S: FDA program page via BioSpace; Holland & Knight; Akin Gump]

**Scale and record.**
- Initially framed as a pilot of no more than five drugs, then expanded: 18 vouchers by January 2026 [S: AP/PBS], 22 publicly identified by October 2026 [S].
- FDA had listed 10 approvals as of 2026-08-26 [S: Life Science Daily News, checked against FDA pages, 2026-10-15].
- One CRL: Disc Medicine's bitopertin, February 2026 [S: BioSpace].
- A review-council structure was set by FDA Staff Manual Guide, effective 2026-01-15 [S].
- FDA held a public hearing on the program on 2026-06-12 [S].

**Compass and politics** [S: STAT, 2026-02-04, five anonymous sources; reported by BioSpace, Psychedelic Alpha, Reason]:
- FDA put COMP360 on its October 2025 list of 10 candidates.
- HHS and White House officials removed it hours before the first awards. It was the only candidate dropped.
- It then received a voucher on 2026-04-24, six days after EO 14401.
- atai co-founder Christian Angermayer called the "snub" coverage "a mountain out of a molehill" [S].

**Legal authority and sign-off** [S: AP/PBS 2026-01-18; BioSpace; Rep. Auchincloss's letters, including a 2026-02-03 follow-up]:
- Critics, including Rep. Auchincloss, say Congress never authorized the program and that FDA's legal office was not consulted.
- Defenders say the commissioner has broad discretion to organize reviews.
- Two officials reportedly declined to sign approvals under the program over legal concerns: former CDER director George Tidmarsh and principal deputy commissioner Sara Brenner.
- Reuters reported that some large drugmakers hesitated to join, citing liability exposure [S: via Mondaq].

**Leadership change** [V: NPR, STAT, Daily Signal, 2026-05-12]:
- Makary resigned 2026-05-12, after 13 months in office.
- Kyle Diamantas (deputy commissioner for food) became acting commissioner.
- Trump announced the nomination of **Heidi Overton**, MD, PhD (deputy director, White House Domestic Policy Council) as commissioner on 2026-08-19; Senate HELP confirmation required. Confirmation status not re-checked on 2026-11-09; search before stating it [V: AABB, Fierce Pharma, Axios, 2026-08-19].
- HHS says the voucher program continues [S: BioSpace, 2026-05-27].
- Brenner moved to HHS in April 2026, and Prasad left CBER at the end of April 2026 [S: Mondaq].

**Stakeholder positions** [S]:
- Doctors for America and others urged a pause in June 2026, citing transparency, selection criteria, and political influence.
- Former Office of New Drugs head Peter Stein called the timeline too short for thorough review.
- Supporters cite faster access and rolling review: FDA has had Compass's modules since April 2026.

## 4. Program detail

### 4.1 COMP360 psilocybin, TRD (Compass Pathways)
- **Designations:** Breakthrough Therapy, 2018 [V: SEC 424B5].
- **"More than 1,000 participants"** (Compass NDA release, 2026-11-09) [CLAIM]: the two Phase 3 trials dosed 258 + 581 = 839 [V; calc]. The total passes 1,000 only with the 233-participant Phase 2b included. Say so if a summary credits "two Phase 3 trials with more than 1,000 participants."
- **Phase 2b** (NEJM, 2022-11-03): 233 participants; single 25 mg vs 10 mg vs 1 mg [V for publication date; M for n].
- **COMP005** (Phase 3; U.S.; single 25 mg dose vs placebo; n=258 dosed, 171 vs 87):
  - Week-6 MADRS treatment difference −3.6 (95% CI −5.7 to −1.5; p<0.001), first reported June 2025 [V: Compass press release 2026-02-17].
  - Serious adverse events: 11 treatment-emergent SAEs in 8 participants (5%) [V].
- **COMP006** (Phase 3; two fixed doses 3 weeks apart; **three arms: 25 mg n=296, 10 mg n=142, 1 mg n=143; 581 dosed**; primary comparison 25 mg vs 1 mg) [V: Compass release 2026-02-17]:
  - Week-6 MADRS difference −3.8 (95% CI −5.8 to −1.8; p<0.001), reported 2026-02-17 [V].
  - **39% of the 25 mg arm achieved a ≥25% MADRS reduction at week 6** [V]. *Appraisal note:* ≥25% is a *lower bar* than the conventional ≥50% "response" definition. Flag any summary that calls it "response" without saying so.
  - Serious adverse events: 6 SAEs in 6 participants (2%, Part A); suicidal ideation <1% across studies; one suicidal-behavior event, in the 1 mg arm [V: Compass release; ConsultantLive].
  - Full Phase 3 data were not peer-reviewed as of February 2026 [S].
- **Durability claims:**
  - Durability "through week 26" is reported *among participants who had ≥25% reduction at week 6*. That is a responder-conditioned subgroup, not a randomized comparison.
  - The 52-week open-label COMP005 data are open-label [V: Q3 2026 8-K].
- **Comparator context:** Compass's chief patient officer said a 3-point MADRS difference is "generally considered clinically meaningful" in TRD. He compared the result with esketamine's roughly 4-point difference [S: HCPLive, company statement]. *This is a sponsor's framing. The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for MADRS is debated (see statistics-appraisal.md §8).*
- **Regulatory:**
  - Rolling NDA review granted April 2026; CNPV April 2026 [V].
  - NDA completed 2026-11-09 [V].
  - Management cites a potential 30–60-day review [S].
  - Commercial launch is subject to DEA rescheduling and to REMS design [S].
- **PTSD:** IND accepted January 2026; Phase 2b/3 COMP202 [V].

### 4.2 Psilocybin for MDD (Usona Institute)
- **Designations:** Breakthrough Therapy for MDD, 2019 [V: Usona site].
- **Phase 2 PSIL201:** published in JAMA; single dose; niacin active placebo [M for the details].
- **Phase 3 uAspire (PSIL301; NCT06308653):**
  - Arms per registry: 25 mg vs 5 mg (active comparator) vs inactive placebo, with facilitator psychosocial support, eyeshades, and a curated music playlist [V: registry via Veeva].
  - Design: 238 adults enrolled (actual) [V: registry]; 6-week double-blind period; 12-month follow-up.
  - Primary completion 2026-01-05 (actual); status "active, not recruiting"; record last verified by the sponsor 2026-07, last update posted 2026-07-23 [V: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, checked 2026-11-09]. (A search snippet showed "2026-10"; the API record governs.)
  - **No topline efficacy results were found publicly as of 2026-11-09.** Do not assert results.
- **Regulatory:** CNPV April 2026 [V/S]. NDA reportedly filed ~2026-11-02 [U].
- **Note:** a nonprofit sponsor with an access-focused mission. This bears on equity analysis, but it is not evidence of efficacy.

### 4.3 MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD (Resilient Pharmaceuticals; formerly MAPS PBC → Lykos Therapeutics)
- **Corporate history:**
  - MAPS PBC was founded in 2014 as MAPS's commercial spinout.
  - Renamed Lykos Therapeutics, January 2024.
  - Renamed Resilient Pharmaceuticals, about August–September 2025. Sources differ on the date, and one calls it "Resilient Therapeutics." MAPS uses "Resilient Pharmaceuticals" [S].
  - MAPS has no active role in Resilient's program [V: MAPS statement 2026-08-10].
- **Trials:** MAPP1 (NCT03537014) and MAPP2 (NCT04077437). Each included three MDMA sessions of 80–120 mg plus an optional supplemental dose, combined with therapy [V: registry].
- **Designations:** Breakthrough Therapy and a Special Protocol Assessment, both 2017 [S].
- **2024 Advisory Committee concerns** [S]:
  - Functional unblinding: nearly all MDMA participants guessed their assignment.
  - Expectancy.
  - Durability.
  - Abuse potential.
  - Reports of misconduct.
- **CRL (published 2025-09-04)** [S: HCPLive, BioSpace, Psychedelic Alpha, Reason]:
  - **Three main concerns:**
    1. Failure to capture and report "positive" or "favorable" adverse events relevant to abuse potential.
    2. Unreported safety events at two sites.
    3. Inadequate durability data and selection bias. Prior MDMA use among participants was one concern raised.
  - FDA asked for a new trial that captures all abuse-related adverse events "regardless of perceived emotional valence" and that uses standardized discharge-readiness criteria.
  - Doblin (MAPS founder) said the FDA "moved the goal posts." Reason's coverage presents the same view.
- **Resubmission:** reported 2026-08-09 (report date, not a confirmed filing date), *without* a new Phase 3 trial [S]. As of 2026-11-09, no public source was found confirming whether FDA accepted the resubmission for review, or its review class. Re-verify.
- **Ethics history** (see psychedelic-ethics-issues.md §3): the MAPS MP4 Phase 2 Canadian site (therapists Richard Yensen and Donna Dryer) and the participant Meaghan Buisson. That case led to the MAPS Code of Ethics (2019; revised 2021).

### 4.4 DT120 ODT (lysergide) — Definium Therapeutics (formerly MindMed; drug formerly MM120)
- **Phase 2b GAD** (JAMA, 2025-09-04; n=198; four doses vs placebo): dose-response shown; 100 µg selected [V: company release].
- **Breakthrough Therapy designation:** GAD, March 2024 [V].
- **Emerge** (Phase 3 MDD; n=149; 75 vs 74; single 100 µg; topline 2026-06-22) [V: company release, https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/232 ; Business Wire 2026-06-22]:
  - MADRS week 6 (primary): −13.3 vs −5.2; difference −8.1 (p<0.0001).
  - Week 12: difference −7.3. Week 1: difference −14.2.
  - Response (≥50%) at week 6: 35% vs 7%. Remission (MADRS ≤12): 24% vs 3%.
  - *Worked example:* the response counts reconstructed from rounded percentages (≈26/75 vs 5/74) give NNT ≈ 3.6 (95% CI ≈ 2.5–6.6). Run `scripts/risk_calc.py`, and always say that the counts are reconstructed.
- **Voyage** (Phase 3 GAD; n=214; 1:1; topline August 2026) [V: company release via Las Vegas Sun / company IR]:
  - HAM-A week 12: −11.6 vs −6.2; difference −5.4 (p<0.0001); d=0.81.
  - Response 43% vs 16%; remission 14% vs 4%.
- **Panorama** (Phase 3 GAD; 100 µg n=96, placebo n=97, plus a 50 µg control arm n=52 not powered for comparison; topline 2026-09-14) [V: SEC exhibit 99.1]:
  - HAM-A week 12: −9.8 vs −4.7; difference −5.1 (p<0.0001); d=0.64.
  - Response 32% vs 14%; remission 15% vs 4%.
  - **TEAE rates: 94.8% (100 µg), 96.1% (50 µg), 62.9% (placebo).** This number speaks directly to functional unblinding.
- **Design notes:**
  - Blinded sample-size re-estimation reduced Panorama's target to 200 [S: investor day].
  - The trials were powered around a 5-point difference [S].
  - Ascend (second MDD Phase 3; ~165 participants) is ongoing, with topline guided 2027 [V: Q2 2026 8-K exhibit].
  - Haven (PTSD Phase 3; ~200 participants; CAPS-5 at week 8) is planned for 2027 [V].
- **Breakthrough Therapy designations:** GAD (March 2024) and **MDD (announced 2026-09-08)** [V: HCPLive / company].
- **NDA timing:** pre-NDA meeting guided 4Q 2026; **NDA filing anticipated 1H 2027** [V: Panorama release, 2026-09-14]. This would be a fourth psychedelic NDA, and the first LSD NDA.

### 4.5 HLP003 (Helus Pharma; formerly Cybin's CYB003), adjunctive MDD
- **Molecule:** deuterated psilocin analog, designed for less pharmacokinetic variability and a shorter experience [S].
- **Breakthrough Therapy designation** [V].
- **Phase 2 claims** (company): 100% response and 71% remission at 12 months after two 16 mg doses, using MADRS ≤10. Remission is "100%" using ≤12.
  - *Appraisal flag:* company-selected remission thresholds; small n; an open-label durability phase.
- **APPROACH** (NCT06564818; n=223; two 16 mg doses 3 weeks apart vs placebo; 1:1):
  - Enrollment completed 2026-07-21; topline guided Q4 2026 [V: Helus release].
  - **Results not found as of 2026-11-09.** Re-verify.
  - The registry completion date was changed once [S: AppliedXL].
- **EMBRACE** (NCT06793397) enrolling; **EXTEND** long-term extension. NDA guided 2028 [V].
- **Design:** the protocol allows concomitant antidepressants (adjunctive design), partly in response to the Lykos review [S].

### 4.6 TSND-201 methylone, PTSD (Transcend; Otsuka)
- **IMPACT-1** (Phase 2; published JAMA Psychiatry, February 2026) [S: secondary database]:
  - Part B: n=52; four weekly doses; no psychotherapy.
  - CAPS-5 difference −9.64 vs placebo (p=0.011).
  - Response 57.1% vs 19.2%.
- **IMPACT-2** (NCT06215261; Phase 2; open-label Part A plus single-blind dose-ranging Part B): completed (n=62 per a registry mirror). A search snippet showed results first submitted 2026-09-08; the independent check could not confirm that date [U].
- **EMPOWER-1** (Phase 3; NCT07456696): randomized 1:1:1 to two doses vs placebo, weekly for 4 weeks; n≈300; estimated primary completion December 2027 [S: registry mirror].
- **Corporate and regulatory:** Otsuka acquisition announced 2026-03-27; closed 2026-06-12 ($700M upfront, up to $1.225B total) [V: Otsuka release]. Breakthrough Therapy designation July 2025. CNPV April 2026 [V].

## 5. State (non-FDA) access programs

These are *not* FDA approvals. They operate under state law while the substances remain federally Schedule I.
- **Oregon (Measure 109, 2020; Oregon Psilocybin Services, OAR chapter 333, division 333):**
  - Licensed service centers and facilitators.
  - Oregon frames services as **not a medical or clinical treatment** and requires no diagnosis or referral.
  - Detailed informed-consent and touch rules (see ethics-codes-to-borrow.md §4).
  - OHA circulated draft rule amendments to a Rules Advisory Committee in November 2026, with adoption expected early 2027 [V: OHA draft PDFs].
- **Colorado (Natural Medicine Health Act, Proposition 122, 2022):**
  - DORA licenses facilitators; the Department of Revenue licenses healing centers and other businesses [V: DORA site].
  - Facilitator training of at least 150 hours, including 25 hours of ethics and Colorado rules and 10 hours of relational boundaries and touch [S].
- **New Mexico:** Medical Psilocybin Act (2025) [M]. Verify the program status before citing.

## 6. Appraisal flags that recur across programs

Load statistics-appraisal.md and study-design-flaws.md for the method. These are the recurring patterns in this pipeline:
1. **Functional unblinding.**
   - TEAE rates of ~95% vs ~63% (Panorama) mean participants can infer their arm.
   - Low-dose comparators help only partly: 1 mg (COMP006), 5 mg (uAspire), 50 µg (Panorama).
2. **Topline-by-press-release.**
   - Least-squares means without CIs, and p<0.0001 without effect-size context.
   - Selected secondary endpoints; nonstandard "response" thresholds (≥25%).
3. **Responder-conditioned durability.** "Durable through week 26" among week-6 responders is not a randomized durability estimate.
4. **Expectancy and hype.** Sponsors, advocates, and the political environment (EO 14401; CNPV) all push in one direction. Speed of review is not evidence of efficacy.
5. **Adverse-event capture.** After the Lykos CRL, check whether "positive" psychoactive effects were captured as AEs (as the 2026 guidance expects).
6. **Psychotherapy as co-intervention.** Where therapy is part of the protocol, FDA approves the drug, not the therapy. The contribution of each component is not separable in a two-arm trial.
7. **Selection.** Prior psychedelic use; exclusion of comorbidity, suicidality, and family history of psychosis. Trial populations may differ from the people who will receive the drug.

## 7. How to re-verify

Before a review relies on any pipeline fact:
1. **ClinicalTrials.gov.** Use the NCT number. Check "Overall Status," "Last Update Posted," and "Results First Posted."
2. **FDA.** Search fda.gov press announcements and the CRL database (openFDA), the Federal Register, and the FDA meetings page.
3. **Company.** Check the investor-relations press release and its SEC 8-K exhibit 99.1. These are primary for what the company claims.
4. **Trade press, for discovery.** Fierce Biotech, BioSpace, Endpoints, STAT, RAPS, and Psychedelic Alpha.
5. **Recording.** Record in the ledger: the fact, the source URL, the date checked, and the tag ([V]/[S]/[M]/[U]). Update this file only by re-saving the skill. Day-to-day updates belong in the Drive knowledge base (see SKILL.md, "Knowledge-base growth").
