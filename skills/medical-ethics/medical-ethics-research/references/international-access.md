## 2. Australia

- **Legal basis.** From 2023-07-01, the Poisons Standard allows psychiatrists who are specifically authorised under the AP scheme to prescribe MDMA for PTSD and psilocybine for TRD. For every other use, both remain Schedule 9 [V: TGA stakeholder-meeting summary; MDMA and psilocybine hub].
- **Four access controls:** the prescriber must be an authorised psychiatrist; a human research ethics committee (HREC) must endorse the application; the TGA must approve it; and treatment must take place in a controlled health setting [V: TGA news page].
- **The products are unapproved.** Products supplied through this pathway are not registered on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods. No sponsor has applied to register a psilocybin or MDMA product, so patients pay privately [S: Medication Evidence; 1News].
- **Uptake** [V: TGA FOI documents, read through search-index excerpts; check the full PDF before quoting a figure]:
  - As of 2025-09-16: 30 MDMA APs and 26 psilocybine APs. Unique patients from July 2023 to the period ending 2025-06-30: 87 MDMA and 47 psilocybine (FOI 26-2177).
  - Later release (FOI 26-2560, released 2026-02-06; the PDF gives no as-of date and labels its documents ambiguously): 48 MDMA and 40 psilocybine prescribers; 123 MDMA and 65 psilocybine patients. A November 2025 TGA slide (FOI 26-3215) says about 40 prescribers and about 130 new patients. Use the newest figure and state its date limits.
  - New APs approved 2026-01-01 to 2026-06-30: 17 MDMA and 22 psilocybine (FOI 27-3505, data as of 2026-07-31).
  - Adverse events reported to the TGA: 0 for each drug from 2023-07-07 to 2025-12-31 (FOI 26-2560); 2 for MDMA and 0 for psilocybin from 2026-01-01 to 2026-09-02 (FOI 27-3566 [S: read through a search-index excerpt; the site blocks automated fetching]).
  - **Caveats the TGA itself states:** patient counts are self-reported in six-monthly AP reports; the TGA "does not hold prescription data"; one patient may receive several doses; and the TGA cannot guarantee the counts represent everyone treated. A zero adverse-event count is a count of *reports*, not proof of zero events.
- **May 2026 reforms** (consultation outcomes published late May 2026, dated 2026-05-26 on the TGA news page and reported 2026-05-28; 83 stakeholders invited, 29 responded) [V: TGA news page; S: Medical Republic, 2026-05-28]:
  1. **Competency:** shown through clinical-trial experience in psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy *or* supervision by an experienced AP. Personal psychedelic use is not a requirement.
  2. **Therapy dyad:** the AP psychiatrist plus at least one practitioner registered with a National Board whose scope of practice includes psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy. The AP decides whether that practitioner is qualified. (Company releases describe a wider pool, including counsellors and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health workers [U: Optimi release, 2026-05-29]. Check the TGA text before relying on it.)
  3. **Oversight:** the AP psychiatrist is responsible for **real-time** screening and consent; the TGA said virtual screening and consent "may not adequately meet safety expectations," accepting the cost to rural access. The only explicit **in-person** rule covers administration: the AP must be physically present while the medicine is given, but not for the whole dosing day [V: TGA outcomes page].
  4. **Site:** equipment and trained staff for monitoring and emergencies; secure Schedule 8 storage; within 15 minutes of a facility with an emergency department; hospital accreditation not required.
- **No take-home doses** under the AP pathway [S].
- **Registry:** the Australian Interventional Pharmacotherapy and Psychedelic Assisted Psychotherapy Research Registry (ANU) collects outcomes; its use is recommended, not required [S].

## 3. New Zealand

- **Psilocybin.** Medsafe approved one psychiatrist, Professor Cameron Lacey (Christchurch), to prescribe psilocybin for TRD. Announced by Associate Health Minister David Seymour on Wednesday 2025-06-18 [V: Ministry of Health release; S: NZ Herald for the name; S: 1News and RNZ for the date]. Conditions: a defined assessment process, including any history of medicine misuse, plus record-keeping and reporting to Medsafe. Psilocybin remains a Class A controlled drug, and no psilocybin product is approved [V].
- **MDMA.** Announced 2026-09-04: Medsafe approved two psychiatrists, Dr Gary Wynn (Wellington) and Dr Tom Paterson (Auckland), to prescribe pharmaceutical-grade MDMA for adults with PTSD that has not responded to conventional therapies [V: Ministry of Health release; S: Euronews for the names].
  - They had to show "substantial expertise" and a detailed delivery plan.
  - Treatment is given only in the clinic, with no take-home doses.
  - No specific MDMA product is approved.
  - About NZ$15,000 per round of treatment, about three rounds per course; no public funding [S].
- **Count:** "New Zealand currently has two clinicians approved to prescribe psilocybin and two approved to prescribe MDMA" [V: Ministry of Health, 2026-09].
- **Design difference from Australia:** New Zealand approves practitioners one at a time and did not reschedule either drug. Australia rescheduled both drugs for named indications and authorises psychiatrists through a standing scheme [S: Psychedelic Alpha; Mind Medicine Law].

## 4. Switzerland

- **Legal basis:** Narcotics Act (NarcA, SR 812.121) Art. 8(5). The Federal Office of Public Health (FOPH/BAG) may grant exceptional authorisations for "limited medical use" of banned narcotics, generally only for patients resident in Switzerland [V: FOPH page].
- **History:** the FOPH issued licences in 1988–1993 and again from 2014 [S].
- **Scale:** 723 authorisations in 2024 (348 psilocybin, 245 MDMA, 130 LSD), given by about 100 physicians; the counts include therapy extensions, so they are not patient counts [V: Liechti et al., *Neurosci Appl* 2025]. A cumulative figure of about 1,660 treatments by 2024 appears in Liechti's work; an older estimate of 2,000 to 3,000 treatments since 2014 (Holze et al.) is likely stale [S].
- **Payment:** generally outside compulsory health insurance [S].
- **Appraisal note:** Switzerland has the longest real-world record, but it reports case counts, not controlled outcomes.

## 5. Germany

- On 2025-07-31 the Central Institute of Mental Health (Mannheim) announced the first EU psilocybin compassionate-use program, confirmed by the federal drug regulator BfArM. Treatment is given in Mannheim and at OVID Clinic Berlin for adults with TRD, in justified exceptional cases [S].
- **Product:** Filament Health's PEX010, supplied free [S].
- **Eligibility** (reported): failure of at least two antidepressants and ineligibility for an ongoing trial [S].
- **Duration:** first listed by BfArM from 2025-07-11 to 2026-07-11. **Renewed:** BfArM's compassionate-use table lists the Mannheim program (Gründer, ZI) with a confirmed notification received 2026-06-18 and a term running to 2027-06-17 [V: BfArM table].
- Compassionate use in Germany is a notified cohort program under the Medicines Act framework, not a marketing authorisation [S].

## 6. Czech Republic

- Amendments adopted in 2025 took effect on 2026-01-01 and allow therapeutic psilocybin in specialised healthcare facilities [V: NUDZ statement]. Government Regulation 552/2025 Sb. defines the individually prepared medicinal product [V]. The criminal-liability exemption is described in legal commentary [S].
- **Not yet operating:** as of 2026-03-23, NUDZ said patients could not yet apply or join a waiting list, reimbursement rules were still being set, and launch was expected in the second half of 2026 [V: NUDZ].
- **Limit:** Government Regulation 552/2025 Sb. caps psilocybin at 75 mg per month, in at most three doses at least 7 days apart, with a 35 mg single-dose cap [V: fact-checker reading of the regulation].

## 7. Canada

- Since 2022-01-05 (SOR/2021-271), practitioners may ask Health Canada's Special Access Program for restricted drugs, including psilocybin and MDMA, for patients with serious or life-threatening conditions when other therapies have failed, are unsuitable, or are unavailable [V].
- **Earlier route:** personal exemptions from the Health Minister under s. 56 of the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act (used for end-of-life patients from 2020) [M].
- SAP is case by case and not a general access program. No public count of approvals was located.

## 8. European Union and the United Kingdom

- **EMA:** no psychedelic has a marketing authorisation. EMA officials have publicly encouraged sponsors to apply [S: Pink Sheet]. COMP360 holds the EU's PRIME designation [S].
- **UK:** COMP360 holds an ILAP designation from the UK regulator (MHRA) for TRD, a development-support route [S: Life Science Daily, citing Compass's 2026-11 release]. **No UK or EU marketing application has been confirmed** [S]. One site reported that Compass intended to file in the EU; the company release did not confirm it, so treat that report as [U].
- Psilocybin remains a Schedule 1 drug in the UK [M]; check before stating it.

## 9. Oregon (and Colorado) real-world data

**Oregon screening hard stops** [V: Cornell LII, OAR text effective 2025-01-01, read 2026-11-09]: the state's required Client Information Form asks, "Have you taken the prescription drug Lithium in the last 30 days?" and a client who answers yes "may not participate in an administration session" (OAR 333-333-5050(3)(a), (5)(a)); the facilitator has no discretion. A session may not exceed 50 mg of psilocybin analyte in total (OAR 333-333-5240(1)). Use these when someone asks whether a person on lithium can use an Oregon service center, and never present the 50 mg cap as a dose recommendation.

**Program status** [V: OHA budget documents, August and October 2026]:
- **Licensing counts, 2023-01-01 to 2026-07-23** (all-time issued vs. approved as of 2026-07-23): facilitators 641 vs. 379; service centers 39 vs. 20; manufacturers 19 vs. 7; laboratories 2 vs. 1.
- **Products sold:** over 50,000 psilocybin products for administration sessions by 2026-10-01. That is not a client count, because one client can buy secondary doses.
- **Staffing:** OPS was cut from 23 to 17 full-time positions.
- **General Fund support:** $3,139,672 in 2023–25, although Measure 109's backers said fees would fund the program [S for the promise].
- **Fees:** the 2026 proposal would have doubled the annual service-center and manufacturer fee (from $10,000 to $20,000) and the facilitator fee (from $2,000 to $4,000) [V: OHA rulemaking notice]. **OHA withdrew the increases on 2026-09-22** after advisory-board, committee, and public feedback. A new rulemaking unrelated to fees has a planned effective date of 2027-01-15 [V: OHA October 2026 overview].
- **Merger:** OPS merged with the Oregon Medical Marijuana Program into the Medical Cannabis & Psilocybin (MCAP) Section, effective 2026-09-01 [V].
- **Closures:** about a third of service centers had closed by January 2026 [S: New York Times via OPB].

**OPEN cohort** — Korthuis PT, … Wilson-Poe AR. *JAMA Netw Open* 2026;9(8):e2630608, published 2026-08-19, corrected September and October 2026 [V: JAMA Network page]:
- **Design:** prospective, uncontrolled, single-arm cohort (STROBE). Enrollment November 2024 to March 2026, follow-up through June 2026.
- **Sample:** 346 clients at 24 of 26 active licensed centers, supervised by 83 facilitators; 312 (90.2%) completed 3 months; mean dose 31.9 mg [V].
- **Safety:** 4 of 346 (1.2%) had serious behavioral reactions that required medical attention. All 4 were psychedelic-naive, had preexisting mental health conditions, and took moderate-to-high doses (25 to 50 mg) [V]. There were no serious medical reactions. The paper is internally inconsistent: the abstract and table give 4, the Results text describes 1, and facilitators recorded only 1 of the 4 (participants reported the rest), which itself shows the limits of facilitator-only reporting. Clients who rated the experience harmful: 1.6% at 1 week, 1.9% at 1 month, 2.3% (7 of 312) at 3 months.
- **Outcomes:** prevalence of moderate-to-severe symptoms, baseline vs. 3 months:

  | Outcome | Baseline | 3 months | Within-person RR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Depression | 42.2% | 16.5% | 0.40 (0.32–0.50) |
  | Anxiety | 45.1% | 13.2% | 0.29 (0.22–0.38) |
  | PTSD | 48.0% | 16.8% | 0.35 (0.27–0.45) |

- **Limitations stated by the authors:** an uncontrolled design that rules out causal inference; a sample of only about 5% of recipients, with possible selection bias; results may not apply to lower-income or marginalized groups; results apply to licensed centers only.
- **Funding:** OHSU accounts, SAMHSA, NIH/NIDA, and OCTRI. One author (Luoma) owns the Portland Institute for Psychedelic Science and has received fees from Atai Life Sciences.

**Other real-world studies:**
- *Mystery-shopper study:* Adams DR et al., *Health Affairs Scholar*, 2026-10-12 (doi 10.1093/haschl/qxag231). Callers reached 15 of 22 active centers. All screened for psychiatric history and contraindications; none allowed unsupervised or take-home use; services were expensive [S: WashU release].
- *Oregon and Colorado retrospective study:* 2,363 clients, medRxiv preprint, August 2026; **not peer reviewed**. Reported about a 50% drop in depression and anxiety scores at 2 weeks; five serious events of unclear relation to psilocybin; ambiguous suicidality findings [S]. Label it a CLAIM and say it is a preprint.
- *Inaugural-year analysis:* Frontiers in Psychiatry 2026 (doi 10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1777387). Statewide aggregate data: 5,935 clients served in 2025 and 258 denials; rare adverse events; **no benefit data** [V: Frontiers page].

## 10. Appraising real-world program data

Use these checks with statistics-appraisal.md and study-design-flaws.md:
1. **A within-person RR is not a treatment effect.** OPEN's RR 0.40 compares each person with their own baseline. It cannot separate the drug from regression to the mean, expectancy, natural recovery, or the people who chose to seek services. There is no control group.
2. **The denominator.** Ask who enrolled. OPEN covered about 5% of Oregon recipients, and the dropouts (34 of 346) may differ from completers.
3. **Counts that depend on reports.** The Australian adverse-event counts and patient counts are self-reported to the regulator. Oregon's adverse reactions are facilitator-reported during the session, and OPEN added participant reports. A low count that rests on reports from people with a stake in the result calls for caution.
4. **A different population.** Oregon screens clients and requires no diagnosis, and OPEN participants had relatively low baseline symptoms (the authors' description). The authors say outcomes "resemble" trials; they do not claim their participants were healthier. Still, do not carry Oregon's safety rates over to a TRD population without saying the populations differ.
5. **Rate per what?** Distinguish rates per client, per session, and per product sold. Oregon's 50,000-plus products are not 50,000 clients.
6. **Preprints and company releases.** Label them CLAIM and say what they are.

## 11. Ethics lessons from comparing models

| Question | Australia | New Zealand | Switzerland | Oregon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medical or non-medical model? | Medical; psychiatrist-led | Medical; named psychiatrists | Medical; physician per patient | Non-medical "wellness" model; no diagnosis |
| Gatekeeper | TGA + HREC + psychiatrist | Medsafe per practitioner | FOPH per patient | OHA licensing; facilitator screening |
| Consent | Real-time, by the AP psychiatrist (2026); virtual judged inadequate | Defined assessment process | Physician | Detailed state consent form (OAR 333-333-5040) |
| Staffing in the room | AP present for administration plus a registered second practitioner | Prescriber's clinic | Physician | One licensed facilitator may suffice |
| Who pays | Mostly private [S] | Private | Mostly private | Private (plus a 15% tax on products) |
| Main ethical tension | Access vs. gatekeeping; very few patients treated | Access depends on a handful of clinicians | Opaque outcomes reporting | Cost and closures; equity; safety in a non-medical setting |

Points to draw out:
- **Justice and access.** Every pathway is mainly self-pay. Few treated patients plus high cost means the benefits go to those who can pay (compare JAMA consensus point 17 on insurance parity; psychedelic-ethics-issues.md).
- **Gatekeeping trade-offs.** Australia's real-time screening and consent rule protects safety but limits rural access. The TGA said so explicitly and chose safety.
- **Staffing.** Australia now requires a second registered practitioner. Use it as a donor for consent and protocol reviews that allow a single facilitator (see ethics-codes-to-borrow.md and consent-review-checklist.md).
- **Personal-experience requirements.** Neither the TGA's competency test nor the JAMA consensus (point 14) requires personal psychedelic use.
- **"No approval anywhere."** An answer that says a country has "approved psilocybin" is wrong. Say "permits authorised prescribers to use an unapproved product."
