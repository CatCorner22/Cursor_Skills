# Glossary

**Purpose.** One term, one meaning, used the same way throughout every review. When a source uses a term differently (e.g., "response" for a 25% reduction), keep this glossary's meaning and note the source's usage explicitly.

**Format.** `Term (abbreviation)` — definition. → related terms. The file opens with a quick-reference table of the effect measures that reviews most often get wrong.

| Term | One-line definition |
|---|---|
| ARR | risk0 − risk1 for a bad outcome; absolute percentage-point reduction |
| RRR | ARR ÷ risk0 = 1 − RR |
| RBI | ABI ÷ risk0 = RR − 1, for a *good* outcome (response); the counterpart of RRR |
| NNT | 1 ÷ ARR (or 1 ÷ absolute benefit increase), over a stated time |
| NNH | 1 ÷ absolute risk increase of a harm |
| OR | (odds in group 1) ÷ (odds in group 0); ≈ RR only for rare events |
| HR | ratio of instantaneous event rates; assumes proportional hazards |
| MD / LS mean difference | between-arm difference in (model-adjusted) mean change |
| SMD (d, g) | MD ÷ pooled SD |
| CI | range of values reasonably compatible with the data, given the model |
| p-value | probability of data at least this extreme if the null model were true |

---

## A
- **Authorised Prescriber (AP) scheme** — Australia's TGA pathway that lets an individually approved psychiatrist prescribe an unapproved medicine; since 2023-07-01, MDMA for PTSD and psilocybine for TRD. Not a product approval (international-access.md §2).
- **Absolute benefit increase (ABI)** — risk1 − risk0 for a *good* outcome (e.g., response). → NNT.
- **Absolute risk increase (ARI)** — risk1 − risk0 for a *bad* outcome that the intervention increases. → NNH.
- **Absolute risk reduction (ARR)** — risk0 − risk1 for a bad outcome the intervention reduces. Expressed in percentage points.
- **Abuse liability / abuse potential** — the likelihood that a drug will be used nonmedically for its rewarding effects. Assessed through "drug liking," euphoria adverse events, and preclinical studies.
- **Active placebo** — a comparator with noticeable effects but no expected therapeutic effect (e.g., niacin, diphenhydramine). Used to reduce functional unblinding.
- **Adjunctive treatment** — added to existing treatment (e.g., HLP003 added to an antidepressant), as opposed to monotherapy.
- **Adverse event (AE) / treatment-emergent AE (TEAE) / serious AE (SAE)** — any untoward medical occurrence after exposure; TEAE: began or worsened after treatment; SAE: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, congenital anomaly, or another important medical event (FDA definition [M]).
- **Allegiance (investigator allegiance)** — researchers' belief in an intervention, which can bias delivery and assessment.
- **Allocation concealment** — preventing anyone from foreseeing the next assignment *before* enrollment. Distinct from blinding.
- **Analytical judgment (label)** — this skill's label for the reviewer's own reasoned conclusion. → Verified fact; Source claim.
- **Autonomy (respect for)** — the principle that persons may make informed, voluntary decisions about themselves. → Principlism.

## B
- **Baseline risk** — the risk of the outcome without the intervention (the control-group risk). It sets the absolute benefit.
- **Belmont Report (1979)** — U.S. statement of three research-ethics principles: respect for persons, beneficence, and justice.
- **Beneficence** — the duty to promote welfare and to balance benefits against risks.
- **Benefit-risk assessment** — the regulator's or reviewer's structured weighing of magnitudes and uncertainties of benefits and harms.
- **Blinding (masking)** — keeping participants, clinicians, raters, or analysts unaware of assignment. "Double-blind" is ambiguous; name who was blinded.
- **Blinding index (Bang; James)** — statistics that quantify how well blinding held, from arm-guess data.
- **Boundary crossing / boundary violation** — a departure from usual practice (a crossing, possibly benign) versus a harmful, exploitative departure (a violation).
- **Breakthrough Therapy designation (BTD)** — an FDA *process* designation for serious conditions when preliminary clinical evidence suggests substantial improvement. It is not approval and not proof of efficacy.

## C
- **CAPS-5** — Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5.
- **Capacity (decision-making capacity)** — the ability to communicate a choice, understand, appreciate, and reason, for a specific decision. A clinical judgment. → Competence (a legal determination).
- **Casuistry** — case-based ethical reasoning from paradigm cases.
- **Certainty of evidence** — GRADE rating: high, moderate, low, very low.
- **Clinical equipoise** — genuine uncertainty in the expert community about which arm is better. It justifies randomization.
- **CNPV (Commissioner's National Priority Voucher)** — 2025–2026 FDA pilot granting accelerated (1–2-month target) review to selected products.
- **Cohen's d / Hedges' g** — standardized mean differences; g corrects small-sample bias. → SMD.
- **Collider bias** — a spurious association created by conditioning on a variable that two other variables both cause.
- **Common Rule** — 45 CFR 46, the U.S. federal policy for the protection of human subjects.
- **Competence** — the legal determination of a person's authority to decide. Often used loosely to mean capacity.
- **Complete Response Letter (CRL)** — FDA letter stating that an application cannot be approved in its present form. FDA has published CRLs since September 2025 [V].
- **Confidence interval (CI)** — an interval produced by a procedure that captures the true value in a stated proportion of repetitions (e.g., 95%). Read it as the range of values reasonably compatible with the data, given the model.
- **Confounding** — distortion of an exposure-outcome association by a third factor related to both.
- **Confounding by indication** — confounding because the reason for treatment also predicts the outcome.
- **CONSORT 2025** — 30-item reporting checklist for randomized trials (April 2025) [V].
- **Contamination** — control participants receive the intervention.
- **Critical-to-quality (CtQ) factors** — in ICH E6(R3), the trial attributes essential to participant protection and reliable results.

## D
- **Declaration of Helsinki** — WMA research-ethics principles; current revision adopted October 2024 [V].
- **Default mode network (DMN)** — a brain network implicated in self-referential thought; its activity is altered by psychedelics.
- **Dichotomization** — converting a continuous outcome into categories (e.g., response yes/no). Loses information.
- **Double effect (doctrine of)** — a harmful side effect may be permissible if not intended and proportionate to the good.
- **Dual relationship (multiple relationship)** — a professional who holds a second role with a client (friend, business partner, teacher).
- **Durability** — persistence of benefit over time. Assess it with randomized follow-up, not only open-label or responder-conditioned data.

## E
- **E-value** — the minimum strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away an observed association.
- **Effect size** — the magnitude of an effect, in any metric (RD, RR, MD, SMD).
- **Entactogen / empathogen** — a drug class (e.g., MDMA) producing emotional openness and prosocial feelings.
- **Estimand** — the precise quantity a trial aims to estimate, including handling of intercurrent events (ICH E9(R1)).
- **Expanded access** — FDA-reviewed access to an investigational drug outside trials. → Right to Try.
- **Expectancy** — the participant's or rater's belief about the treatment's effect, which can change outcomes.

## F
- **Facilitator** — in state programs (Oregon, Colorado), the licensed person who supports a client through preparation, administration, and integration. Not necessarily a clinician.
- **Fragility index** — the minimum number of patients whose outcome must change (non-event to event in the arm with fewer events) to make a significant result non-significant by Fisher's exact test. The value depends on which outcome you count as the event (response or non-response), because that changes which arm has fewer events; report the outcome you used.
- **Functional unblinding** — participants or raters infer assignment from the drug's noticeable effects.

## G
- **GCP (Good Clinical Practice)** — the ICH E6 standard. Current version E6(R3), adopted January 2025 [V].
- **Goldwater Rule** — American Psychiatric Association annotation §7.3: no professional opinion on a public figure the psychiatrist has not examined.
- **GRADE** — framework for rating the certainty of evidence and the strength of recommendations.

## H
- **HAM-A / HAM-D** — Hamilton Anxiety / Depression Rating Scales.
- **Hazard ratio (HR)** — see the quick-reference table.
- **Heterogeneity (I², τ²)** — variation in true effects across studies. I² is a proportion; τ² is the between-study variance.
- **HPPD** — hallucinogen persisting perception disorder.

## I
- **ICMJE** — International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (authorship, conflicts, registration).
- **IND (Investigational New Drug application)** — FDA permission to test an unapproved drug in humans.
- **Informed consent** — a voluntary, capacitated decision after adequate disclosure and understanding, documented as required.
- **Integration (session)** — post-dosing session(s) to process the experience.
- **Intention-to-treat (ITT)** — analysis of all participants as randomized.
- **IRB / REC / IEC** — institutional review board / research ethics committee / independent ethics committee.

## J
- **Justice** — fair distribution of benefits and burdens, including fair selection of research participants and fair access.

## L
- **Limited medical use** — Switzerland's FOPH exceptional authorisation, under Narcotics Act Art. 8(5), to treat a named patient with a banned narcotic (LSD, MDMA, psilocybin). Case by case; not a marketing authorisation.
- **Least-squares (LS) mean** — a model-adjusted mean. Typical of press-release efficacy numbers.
- **Likelihood ratio (LR+ / LR−)** — how much a positive or negative test result changes the odds of disease.
- **LOCF** — last observation carried forward. An outdated missing-data method.

## M
- **MADRS** — Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (0–60).
- **MCID / MID** — minimal clinically important difference. Method-dependent and contested; never present one value as settled.
- **Microdosing** — repeated sub-perceptual doses. Weak controlled evidence; raises 5-HT2B theoretical concerns.
- **mITT** — modified intention-to-treat. Definitions vary; always state the exclusions.
- **MMRM** — mixed model for repeated measures. Assumes data are missing at random.
- **Multiplicity** — inflation of false positives from multiple comparisons.
- **Mystical-type experience** — subjective experience of unity, sacredness, ineffability, and similar features (measured by the MEQ30).

## N
- **NDA (New Drug Application)** — application to FDA for marketing approval.
- **Neuroplastogen / psychoplastogen** — a compound that promotes rapid structural neural plasticity.
- **Nocebo** — a harmful effect arising from negative expectations. Includes placebo-arm disappointment.
- **Nonmaleficence** — the duty not to inflict harm.
- **NNT / NNH** — see the quick-reference table. NNTB/NNTH are used in CIs that cross zero.

## O
- **Odds ratio (OR)** — see the quick-reference table.
- **Open-label extension (OLE)** — post-randomization phase in which participants know their treatment. Not a randomized comparison.
- **Outcome switching** — reporting a different primary outcome than the one registered.

## P
- **Per-protocol analysis** — analysis of participants who adhered to the protocol.
- **Percentage point** — the arithmetic difference between two percentages (7% → 35% is +28 points).
- **Placebo-adjusted difference** — the between-arm difference in change from baseline.
- **Post-trial access** — provision of a beneficial investigational treatment after the trial ends (Helsinki 2024).
- **Power** — probability of detecting a specified true effect (1 − β).
- **Prediction interval** — in meta-analysis, the range of true effects expected in a new setting.
- **Principlism** — the four-principle framework: autonomy, nonmaleficence, beneficence, justice.
- **Publication bias** — selective publication based on results.
- **p-value** — see the quick-reference table. Not the probability that the hypothesis is true.

## R
- **Relative benefit increase (RBI)** — (risk1 − risk0) ÷ risk0 for a *good* outcome such as response; equals RR − 1. Emerge: 35% vs 7% gives RBI +400% (RR 5) and ABI 28 percentage points. Counting non-response instead gives a relative reduction of only about 30%, so always say which outcome the relative figure counts.
- **Randomization** — allocation by chance.
- **Regression to the mean** — extreme measurements tend to be followed by less extreme ones.
- **REMS (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy)** — an FDA-required safety program (e.g., certified sites, monitoring).
- **Remission / response** — defined per scale and per trial. In this skill, "response" means ≥50% reduction from baseline unless stated otherwise; for MADRS, "remission" is commonly ≤10 or ≤12 (state which).
- **Rescheduling** — DEA (and state) change of a substance's Controlled Substances Act schedule. Required after FDA approval of a Schedule I drug.
- **Responder-conditioned** — an analysis restricted to participants who responded earlier. It loses randomization.
- **Right to Try (2018 Act)** — pathway to access certain investigational drugs without FDA review of each request.
- **Risk difference (RD)** — risk1 − risk0.
- **Risk ratio / relative risk (RR)** — risk1 ÷ risk0.
- **RoB 2 / ROBINS-I V2** — Cochrane risk-of-bias tools for randomized and non-randomized studies (ROBINS-I V2 launched November 2024) [V].

## S
- **Schedule I** — CSA category: high abuse potential, no currently accepted medical use, lack of accepted safety under supervision.
- **Selection bias** — systematic differences in who enters, or remains in, the compared groups.
- **Set and setting** — the participant's mindset and the physical/social environment. Part of the intervention.
- **SMD** — standardized mean difference.
- **Source claim (label)** — this skill's label for a statement attributed to a source and not independently verified. → Verified fact.
- **Special Protocol Assessment (SPA)** — FDA agreement on a protocol's design, before the trial.
- **Spin** — reporting that makes results look more favorable than the data justify.
- **SPIRIT 2025** — 34-item protocol checklist [V].
- **Surrogate endpoint** — a marker used in place of a patient-important outcome.

## T
- **Therapeutic misconception** — a participant's belief that research procedures are designed for their personal benefit.
- **Touch (supportive / therapeutic)** — physical contact by a facilitator or therapist. Regulated in Oregon to client-requested, prior-written-consent, limited forms [V].
- **Type I / Type II / Type M / Type S error** — false positive / false negative / exaggerated magnitude / wrong sign.

## U
- **Unblinding** → functional unblinding.

## V
- **Verified fact (label)** — this skill's label for a fact checked against a primary source, with the date checked.
- **Voluntariness** — freedom from coercion and undue influence. A threshold element of consent.

## W
- **Within-person relative risk** — the ratio of a condition's prevalence after an exposure to its prevalence before, in the same people (e.g., OPEN cohort depression RR 0.40). It has no control group, so it is not a treatment effect.
- **Winner's curse** — statistically significant estimates from underpowered studies overstate the true effect.
