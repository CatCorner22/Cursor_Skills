## 2. What the history teaches an ethics review

| Episode | Lesson | Where it applies now |
|---|---|---|
| MKUltra | Psychedelics have been used on people without consent and for non-therapeutic ends; the state was the wrongdoer. Trust is fragile, especially in communities with reason to distrust research | Consent; transparency; independent oversight; justice |
| Saskatchewan and early clinical work | Set and setting shape outcomes; preparation and support are part of the intervention | Trial design (therapy as co-intervention); ReSPCT reporting |
| Harvard Psilocybin Project | Investigators who are also advocates and users blur roles; recruitment of students raised coercion concerns | Conflicts of interest; therapist self-use; vulnerable recruits |
| Good Friday Experiment | Blinding fails when one arm has an unmistakable experience; the placebo arm knew | Functional unblinding (trial-design.md §6) |
| 1970 scheduling and MDMA in 1985–88 | Legal status can follow politics and enforcement more than evidence; research froze for decades | Fair presentation of drug-policy debates; base rates; no "suppressed miracle" narrative either |
| MP4 misconduct (2018–2022) | Touch, altered states, and closed rooms enable abuse; video existed but no one reviewed it in time | Touch rules; recording with independent review; staffing |
| Lykos CRL and retractions (2024) | Data integrity, adverse-event capture, and therapist conduct can sink a program even with positive topline results | AE capture of "positive" effects; trial governance |
| Oregon closures and fees (2023–2026) | A non-medical model can be safe in screened clients yet financially fragile and expensive for clients | Justice and access (international-access.md §9) |

## 3. Classes and shared pharmacology

| Class | Core mechanism | Members |
|---|---|---|
| Classic (serotonergic) psychedelics | 5-HT2A receptor agonism (partial or full), with other receptor activity | Psilocybin (prodrug of psilocin), LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT (also 5-HT1A), mescaline |
| Entactogens / empathogens | Release of serotonin (and noradrenaline, dopamine) via transporter reversal; oxytocin release | MDMA, methylone, MDA |
| Dissociatives | NMDA receptor antagonism | Ketamine, esketamine (FDA-approved as Spravato) |
| Atypical | Mixed: ibogaine acts on NMDA, opioid, and serotonin transporter targets and has a long-acting metabolite (noribogaine); salvinorin A is a kappa-opioid agonist | Ibogaine, salvinorin A |
| Non-hallucinogenic analogues (investigational) | Designed to keep neuroplastic effects without the subjective trip | Several preclinical and early-clinical compounds [M] |

**Shared features of classic psychedelics** [M]: low physiological toxicity relative to the dose that produces effects; rapid tolerance with repeated daily dosing; cross-tolerance within the class; low compulsive-use liability in animal self-administration models; transient increases in blood pressure and heart rate.

## 4. Substance monographs

Durations are typical ranges [M]. Effects vary with dose, setting, expectation, and the person.

**Psilocybin** (psilocin is the active form)
- *Source:* Psilocybe mushrooms; synthetic forms in trials (COMP360; Usona's synthetic psilocybin).
- *Course:* onset 20–60 minutes; peak 1–2 hours; total 4–6 hours.
- *Effects:* altered perception, emotion, and sense of self; mystical-type and "ego dissolution" experiences at higher doses; anxiety or challenging experiences are common.
- *Physiology:* raised blood pressure and heart rate; headache (often the next day); nausea.
- *Main risks:* acute anxiety and panic; dangerous behaviour if unsupervised; psychosis in predisposed people; a suicidality signal in some trials (COMP360 Phase 2b imbalance; Phase 3 data in fda-pipeline.md).
- *Status:* Schedule I in the US; under FDA review with a priority voucher; legal pathways in Oregon, Colorado, and several countries.

**LSD (lysergide)**
- *Course:* onset 30–90 minutes; total 8–12 hours or longer, which makes sessions long and costly.
- *Effects:* similar to psilocybin, with more stimulation.
- *Clinical:* Definium's MM120/DT120 programs in GAD and MDD (fda-pipeline.md).
- *Risks:* as psilocybin, over a longer exposure; **seizures reported with lithium** (§6).

**DMT and ayahuasca**
- *DMT* smoked or injected: onset in seconds to minutes; total 15–30 minutes; very intense. It is inactive orally unless combined with an MAO inhibitor.
- *Ayahuasca:* a brew of DMT-containing plants plus *Banisteriopsis caapi* (harmine and harmaline, which inhibit MAO-A). Onset 30–60 minutes; total 4–6 hours; vomiting is common and ritually expected.
- *Risks:* the MAO inhibition creates **dangerous interactions with serotonergic drugs** (§6). About 1.3% of 8,216 ayahuasca users (a subsample of a 10,836-person global survey) self-reported "fits or seizures" (Bouso et al., *PLOS Glob Public Health* 2022;2(11):e0000438) [V]. Self-report, not a clinical rate.
- *Clinical:* short-acting IV and inhaled DMT programs are in development [M].

**5-MeO-DMT**
- *Source:* synthetic, or from the venom of the *Incilius alvarius* toad.
- *Course:* very fast; total under about 30–60 minutes.
- *Effects:* intense; often a loss of all perceptual content.
- *Risks:* unsupervised and ceremonial use has been linked to serious harms [M]; the evidence base is very small.
- *Clinical:* GH Research's GH001 is in development; its Phase 3 has not started (fda-pipeline.md).
- *Ethics:* toad harvesting raises conservation and Indigenous-appropriation concerns.

**Mescaline**
- *Source:* peyote and San Pedro cacti.
- *Course:* slow onset; total 10–12 hours.
- *Risks:* nausea and vomiting are common.
- *Status:* peyote is protected for Native American Church religious use under federal law [M]. Overharvesting threatens peyote populations.

**MDMA**
- *Course:* onset 30–60 minutes; total 3–6 hours.
- *Effects:* euphoria, emotional openness, reduced fear response. It is less perceptually psychedelic than the classic psychedelics.
- *Physiology:* raised heart rate and blood pressure; jaw clenching; hyperthermia.
- *Main risks:*
  - hyponatremia from excess water intake;
  - hyperthermia in hot, crowded settings;
  - serotonin toxicity with interacting drugs;
  - a post-use mood dip;
  - neurotoxicity at high, repeated recreational doses (debated);
  - moderate abuse liability.
- *Clinical:* MAPS/Lykos MAPP1 and MAPP2 trials; complete response letter in 2024; resubmission reported in 2026 (fda-pipeline.md).
- *Interactions:* SSRIs and SNRIs block or blunt MDMA's effects (controlled Swiss studies) [S].

**Methylone**
- An entactogen related to MDMA. Transcend's program for PTSD holds an April 2026 priority voucher (fda-pipeline.md).

**Ketamine and esketamine**
- *Course:* acts within hours; dissociative effects for 1–2 hours.
- *Status:* esketamine (Spravato) is FDA-approved for TRD, with a REMS requiring 2 hours of observation; approved as monotherapy in 2025. Off-label IV ketamine clinics are widespread.
- *Risks:* dissociation, blood-pressure rise, abuse liability, bladder toxicity with chronic heavy use.
- *Ethics:* the off-label clinic market is a live example of access outrunning evidence and regulation.

**Ibogaine**
- *Course:* a very long experience (24–36 hours or more); the metabolite noribogaine persists for days.
- *Main risk:* cardiac. Ibogaine blocks hERG potassium channels, causing QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. **33 ibogaine-related deaths were publicly reported between 1990 and 2020**, most in settings without cardiac monitoring [V: Luz & Mash, *Expert Opin Drug Metab Toxicol* 2021;17(9):1019–1022, a short commentary; the count matches Corkery's 2018 tally]. A 2026 preprint (not peer-reviewed) counts 48 deaths for 1990–2026 [S: preprint]. Other harms include ataxia and severe vomiting.
- *Use:* opioid withdrawal and addiction. The 2026 FDA ibogaine RFI and EO 14401 encourage research (fda-pipeline.md).

**Salvinorin A** (*Salvia divinorum*)
- A kappa-opioid agonist; very short and often dysphoric. Not a therapeutic focus; listed for completeness [M].

## 5. Cross-cutting risks

| Risk | What is known | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Challenging experiences | Fear, paranoia, grief, and re-experienced trauma are common and can be therapeutically meaningful, yet some are harmful. Most resolve within the session; some do not | M |
| Prolonged psychosis or mania | Rare; concentrated in people with personal or family histories of psychosis or bipolar disorder, who are excluded from most trials | M |
| HPPD (persisting perception disorder) | Rare persistent visual changes; true prevalence unknown | M |
| Suicidality | Signals in some trials (COMP360 Phase 2b); depression itself carries risk; the C-SSRS is needed at fixed times | V (fda-pipeline.md) |
| Suggestibility and belief change | Heightened suggestibility; lasting changes in beliefs, including metaphysical ones. Raises consent and undue-influence concerns | S (psychedelic-ethics-issues.md §4) |
| Boundary violations | Altered states plus touch plus private rooms; documented misconduct (MP4) | V |
| Cardiovascular | Transient BP and HR rises for all classes; QT prolongation (ibogaine); valvulopathy concern with chronic 5-HT2B activation (hence the FDA requirement to test 5-HT2B activity) | V/M |
| Abuse liability | Low for the classic psychedelics; moderate for MDMA and ketamine | M |
| Unsupervised and mixed use | Most serious harms in the real-world record involve no supervision, other drugs, or dangerous settings | M |

## 6. Interactions that matter

| Combination | Effect | Evidence | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lithium + classic psychedelics** | Seizures: 47% of 62 online experience reports (the 39% medical-attention figure appears only in an abstract-summary site [S]); none of 34 lamotrigine reports involved seizures | Nayak, Gukasyan, Barrett, Erowid, Erowid & Griffiths, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2021;54(5):240–245. Self-reported data; a signal, not a rate. Oregon bars anyone who has taken lithium in the last 30 days from a session (OAR 333-333-5050(3)(a), (5)(a)) [V] | V |
| **Ayahuasca (MAO-A inhibition) + SSRIs, SNRIs, MDMA, and other serotonergic drugs** | Serotonin toxicity | Pharmacological and case evidence | M/S |
| **SSRIs + psilocybin** | Older reports of blunting; two small recent studies found the effects were not blocked | Mixed; small studies | S |
| **SSRIs or SNRIs + MDMA** | Blocks or blunts the effects; theoretical serotonin-toxicity risk | Controlled studies | S |
| **Antipsychotics + psychedelics** | Attenuation (risperidone more than haloperidol in one study); used as rescue medication | Small studies | S |
| **Ibogaine + QT-prolonging drugs, opioids, or stimulants** | Arrhythmia | Case series | V/M |
| **Tramadol, MAOIs, or St John's wort + MDMA** | Serotonin toxicity; seizures (tramadol) | Pharmacology | M |

Antidepressant tapering before a trial is itself a risk (withdrawal, relapse, suicidality) and selects who enters the trial (trial-design.md §7).

## 7. Who is at higher risk

- Personal or first-degree family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder.
- Uncontrolled hypertension, significant heart disease, or QT prolongation (absolute for ibogaine).
- Current lithium; MAO inhibitors; serotonergic drug combinations (§6).
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding (no data).
- Adolescents (developing brain; excluded from trials) and older adults (cardiovascular risk; few data).
- Active suicidality; severe personality disorders (unclear; usually excluded).
- People with trauma histories in settings without trauma-informed care.
- People with a history of being harmed by authority figures or practitioners, for whom touch, altered states, and power imbalance carry extra risk.
- Psychedelic-naive clients in non-medical settings: all four serious reactions in the OPEN Oregon cohort occurred in first-time users (international-access.md §9).
