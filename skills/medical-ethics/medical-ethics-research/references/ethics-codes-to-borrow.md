# Codes of Ethics to Borrow From

Psychedelic care creates problems that no single professional code was written for:
- consent given before an altered state;
- prolonged sessions with intense dependency;
- therapeutic touch with highly suggestible clients;
- unlicensed facilitators;
- financial and spiritual influence;
- Indigenous knowledge.

This file maps those gaps to existing codes that already solved an analogous problem. When the skill recommends a safeguard, it cites the source provision and states how the safeguard must be adapted.

**Citation discipline.**
- Paraphrase code provisions. Never quote long passages; codes are copyrighted.
- Cite the code, its edition, and the section or opinion number, verified against the current official text before a memo relies on it.
- Section numbers below are tagged [M] (training knowledge, not re-verified) or [V] (verified 2026-11-09).
- Codes get revised. ACA expects a new code in fall 2026, and APA is drafting one, so check the edition in force.

## 1. The gap map (start here)

| Psychedelic-care gap | Best donor codes | What the donor provision does (paraphrase) | Adaptation needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consent to touch given before dosing** | Colorado 4 CCR 755-1 §1.4 "supportive touch" (hands/feet/shoulders only; consent before the administration session in a physical touch contract; never sexual) and Rule 6.6 (touch only on the participant's request; "stop" word or gesture; revocable at any time) [V]; Oregon OAR 333-333-5040 and -5120 [V]; MAPS Code §5 Touch [V: section titles]; AMTA massage-therapy code (consent and right to stop) [M] | Oregon: no touch without the client's *prior written* consent, discussed before the session; touch types limited; touch must be client-requested. Massage codes: explicit consent to each area; withdrawal of consent at any time | Add an in-session "stop" signal that works while intoxicated; ban any expansion of consent during the session; consider video review |
| **Sexual boundaries** | AMA Code (opinion on romantic or sexual relationships with patients) [M]; American Psychiatric Association annotations (sex with current or former patients unethical) [M]; MAPS Code §6 [V: title]; ABA Model Rule 1.8(j) (no sexual relations with clients unless pre-existing) [M] | Absolute prohibition with current patients; strong presumption against with former patients, especially in psychotherapy | Extend to facilitators and sitters; set a defined post-treatment period, or make it permanent, given altered-state intimacy |
| **Diminished capacity during sessions** | ABA Model Rule 1.14 (client with diminished capacity: maintain a normal relationship as far as possible; take protective action when necessary) [M]; psychiatric advance directives [M]; anesthesia norms for sedated patients [M] | Preserve the client's autonomy as far as possible; protective steps only when needed, and least-restrictive | Pre-session "session plan" or advance directive covering touch, music, leaving the room, emergency medication, contacting family |
| **Chaperones / second presence** | AMA guidance on chaperones for intimate exams [M]; Oregon and Colorado rules on who may be present and on the client's right to request another person [V/S] | A chaperone is offered or required for sensitive exams; patient preference is respected | Two-facilitator or recording requirement for high-risk sessions; client consent to recording |
| **Unlicensed facilitators and scope of practice** | Oregon OAR 333-333-5130 (facilitators may not exercise another license's privileges while facilitating) [V]; Colorado facilitator licensing [V] | Clear scope; no diagnosis or treatment beyond the license | In medical (FDA) models: who is the "therapist," what credentials, under what supervision |
| **Dual relationships and influence** | ACA 2014 §A.6 (boundaries and professional relationships) [M]; APA 3.05 (multiple relationships) [M]; NASW 1.06 (conflicts of interest) [M]; MAPS Code (avoid dual relationships likely to impair judgment or exploit) [S: CBC] | Avoid relationships that risk exploitation or impair objectivity | Teacher/student/facilitator role overlap in training programs; retreat "community" relationships |
| **Financial conflicts and inducement** | AMA opinions on conflicts of interest and industry relationships [M]; Colorado limits on facilitator financial interests in healing centers [V]; ICMJE disclosure [M] | Disclose and manage conflicts; avoid fee-splitting and self-referral | Bundled packages and repeat-session upsell; donation requests after transformative sessions |
| **Hype and claims of cure** | Oregon "dishonest conduct" rule (no curative or therapeutic claims in a non-medical program) [S]; FDA promotional rules [M]; ACA/APA standards against false or deceptive statements [M] | No misrepresentation of efficacy | Require absolute-risk statements and uncertainty disclosure in recruitment and marketing |
| **Mandatory reporting and confidentiality limits** | Oregon informed-consent form (facilitators may be mandatory reporters) [V]; *Tarasoff* duty-to-protect statutes [M]; HIPAA [M] | Disclose limits of confidentiality in advance | Disclosures under a drug that loosens inhibition; recordings |
| **Group sessions** | Oregon group administration rules [V]; Colorado group limits (the draft cap was reduced from 100 to 64 participants) [S]; AGPA group-therapy guidelines [M] | Ratio limits; group consent | Consent to peer touch (Oregon allows clients to consent to supportive touch from other clients) [V] |
| **Integration and abandonment** | ACA/APA termination standards [M]; Colorado (no separate fee for integration) [S] | No abandonment; referral on termination | Post-session crisis plans; follow-up obligations when insights destabilize |
| **Research misconduct and AE capture** | ICH E6(R3) [V]; 21 CFR 312 [M]; COPE [M]; 2026 FDA final guidance [V] | Report all AEs; quality management; integrity | Capture "positive" psychoactive AEs and boundary events as AEs |
| **Spiritual and existential influence** | Association of Professional Chaplains code [M]; APA standards on respecting client values [M] | Do not proselytize; respect the client's own beliefs | Facilitator restraint in interpreting "mystical" content; no imposition of metaphysics |
| **Indigenous knowledge and benefit-sharing** | Nagoya Protocol [M]; UNDRIP [M]; CIOMS benefit-sharing [M]; community codes (e.g., Chacruna reciprocity initiatives) [M] | Prior informed consent and benefit-sharing for traditional knowledge | Voluntary reciprocity in a U.S. context where the United States is not a Nagoya party |

## 2. Medical and psychiatric codes

- **AMA Code of Medical Ethics** [M]. Opinions in chapters, e.g.:
  - Ch. 1: patient-physician relationship;
  - Ch. 2: consent, communication, decision-making;
  - Ch. 7: research and innovation;
  - Ch. 8: physicians and the health of the community;
  - Ch. 9: professional self-regulation, including sexual boundaries;
  - Ch. 11: financing and delivery of care.
  - Verify opinion numbers on the AMA site before citing.
- **American Psychiatric Association**, *Principles of Medical Ethics with Annotations Especially Applicable to Psychiatry* [M]. Exploitation; confidentiality; Goldwater Rule §7.3; the psychiatrist's responsibility for the conduct of supervisees.
- **WMA International Code of Medical Ethics** (revised 2022 [M]) and the Declaration of Geneva (2017 [M]). These are global physician duties.
- **ASAM (addiction medicine) public policy statements** [M]. Relevant to abuse-liability framing and to harm reduction.

## 3. Mental health professions codes

- **APA (psychological)**, *Ethical Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct* (2002; amended 2010, 2016) [M]. A draft new code went to public comment closing 2025-03-19 [V]. Standards to borrow:
  - 3.05 Multiple relationships;
  - 3.10 Informed consent;
  - 10.01 Informed consent to therapy, including treatments without established techniques;
  - 10.05–10.08 Sexual intimacies;
  - 8.02 Informed consent to research.
  - (Numbers [M].)
- **ACA Code of Ethics (2014)** [V: in force]. Revision expected fall 2026 [V].
  - Sections A (counseling relationship), B (confidentiality), C (professional responsibility), G (research), H (distance counseling).
  - The 2026 draft reportedly adds a forensic-practice section and a counselor well-being standard [S].
- **NASW Code of Ethics** (2021 amendments [M]). Social justice, cultural competence, self-care, conflicts of interest (1.06), sexual relationships (1.09), physical contact (1.10) [M].
  - *Note:* NASW 1.10 directly addresses appropriate physical contact. It is a useful donor for touch rules.
- **AAMFT Code of Ethics** (2015 [M]). Multiple relationships; responsibility to clients.
- **ANA Code of Ethics for Nurses (2025 revision; released 2025-01-29; now 10 provisions, including a new global-health provision)** [V]. Nurses often staff dosing sessions.

## 4. Psychedelic-specific codes and state rules

- **MAPS Code of Ethics for Psychedelic Psychotherapy**, version 4, 2021-01-07 (12 sections; a PDF reposted June 2022) [V]. Original 2019 [V].
  - Sections include: Safety; confidentiality and privacy; transparency; therapeutic alliance and trust; **§5 Touch; §6 Sexual Boundaries**; diversity; special considerations [V: section list from maps.org/safety].
  - Created 2018–2019 after the Canadian MP4 site violations; revised 2021.
  - Criticism: MAPS did not tighten who may act as a trial therapist [S: CBC].
- **Oregon Psilocybin Services (ORS 475A; OAR chapter 333, division 333)** [V]:
  - **333-333-5040 Informed consent:**
    - a state-provided form;
    - acknowledgments, including that facilitators may not use touch without prior written consent;
    - pregnancy and breastfeeding risks are unknown;
    - facilitators may be mandatory reporters;
    - services are not medical treatment.
    - Amended effective 2025-01-01; further draft amendments went to the Rules Advisory Committee in November 2026, with adoption expected early 2027 [V].
  - **333-333-5120 Facilitator conduct:**
    - no misrepresentations;
    - supportive touch only when *requested by the client* and with *prior written consent*;
    - touch limited to hugs or hands on the client's hands, feet, or shoulders;
    - in groups, clients may consent to supportive touch from other clients [V: current text via Cornell/LII-mirror]. An earlier 2022 draft limited touch to hand or shoulder [V: Harbin analysis] — a useful example of drafting evolution.
  - **333-333-5130 Scope of practice:** no exercise of another professional license while facilitating [V].
  - **333-333-4640 Video recordings:** prior written consent of everyone recorded; free viewing by the people recorded; secure storage; no sharing without written consent; 5-year retention; consent to recording may be withdrawn only before the session [V: Cornell LII, 2026-11-09].
  - **Facilitator training** must cover Oregon's Facilitator Code of Ethics; equity, privilege, bias, and power; the vulnerability of altered states; touch and consent [V: OHA curriculum worksheet].
- **Colorado Natural Medicine Program** (Prop 122, 2022; DORA for facilitators, DOR for businesses) [V]:
  - at least 150 hours of training, including **25 hours of "Ethics and Colorado Natural Medicine Rules and Regulations"** and **10 hours of "Relational Boundaries and Introduction to Physical Touch"** [V: 4 CCR 755-1, Rule 4.1(F)(1)(b)–(c)]. Rule 4.1(H)(4) allows credit for substantially equivalent prior coursework, so do not describe the ethics hours as "not waivable";
  - limits on facilitators' financial interests [V: C.R.S. 12-170-105];
  - participant may request another person be present [S];
  - **Rule 6.6, Use of Physical Touch**: touch only when the participant requests it, always optional, pre-agreed "stop" words or gestures, revocable at any time [V: Justia, current through 2025-06-10];
  - **recording**: Rule 6.5(H) (disclose any recording; release only with explicit permission naming use, recipients, and terms); Rule 6.18(G)(2) (outside a healing center, a video recording is required when no second facilitator is present) [V];
  - administration permitted at some locations other than healing centers [S].
- **Industry and community pledges** (e.g., the 2021 "North Star" psychedelic ethics pledge [M]). These are voluntary and unenforced; cite them only as aspirational.

## 5. Codes from adjacent fields

- **Massage and bodywork** (AMTA Code of Ethics; NCBTMB standards [M]): consent to touch areas, draping, the client's right to stop at any time, and no sexual contact. The closest analogue for *operational* touch rules.
- **Anesthesiology and procedural sedation** [M]: care of patients who cannot protect themselves; chaperone norms; documentation. A useful analogue for the dosing-session duty of care.
- **Law: ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct** [M]:
  - 1.0(e) defines informed consent;
  - 1.4 communication;
  - 1.7 conflicts;
  - 1.8(j) sexual relations with clients;
  - **1.14 client with diminished capacity**: a strong, well-developed analogue for intoxicated-client decision-making;
  - 1.6 confidentiality and its exceptions.
- **Chaplaincy / spiritual care** (APC/NACC common code [M]): no proselytizing; respect for diverse beliefs.
- **Hypnosis** (ASCH code [M]): heightened suggestibility; false-memory risk. An analogue for memory and belief-change risks under psychedelics.
- **Doulas and peer support** (e.g., peer-specialist codes [M]): non-clinical support roles, close to the "sitter" or "facilitator" role.

## 6. Research and publication codes

- ICH E6(R3) GCP [V]; 21 CFR 50/56/312 [M]; 45 CFR 46 [M]; Declaration of Helsinki 2024 [V]; CIOMS 2016 [M].
- ICMJE recommendations (authorship, conflicts, trial registration) [M]; COPE (retractions, misconduct) [M]; CONSORT/SPIRIT 2025 [V].
- **Lesson from the 2024 FDA review of MDMA:** AE definitions written for ordinary drugs missed abuse-relevant "positive" effects. The research code must be adapted to the drug class. The 2026 final guidance now says this [V].

## 7. Indigenous and cultural reciprocity frameworks

- **Nagoya Protocol** on Access and Benefit-Sharing (adopted 2010; in force 2014) [M]. The United States is not a party [M]. For U.S. sponsors, its principles are therefore voluntary.
- **UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples** (2007) [M]: Article 31 on traditional knowledge [M].
- **Practical asks** in the literature [M]:
  - benefit-sharing (funds, access, research capacity);
  - acknowledgment;
  - community consent for use of ceremonial knowledge;
  - conservation (e.g., peyote; iboga sourcing);
  - avoiding patents that enclose traditional practice.
- **Analytical caution.** Synthetic molecules may raise weaker *legal* claims but still raise *moral* claims of reciprocity. Present both views.
