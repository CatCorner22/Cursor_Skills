# Consent-Form and Protocol Review Checklist (Psychedelic Studies and Services)

Use this checklist when Blake asks for a review of a consent form, a protocol section, a facilitator agreement, or a policy.

**How to work through it:**
1. Run the federal elements first (§1). They are the legal floor for FDA-regulated research.
2. Run the psychedelic-specific items next (§2). That is where most drafts fail.
3. Draft replacement text (§4). When `one-way-optimal-language` is available, load it for the drafting step: it applies the Adams rules to consent text, and Blake built it for this.

## 1. Federal required elements

**21 CFR 50.25(a): basic elements** (every FDA-regulated study) [V: eCFR]:

| Element | Content | Common failure in psychedelic forms |
|---|---|---|
| (a)(1) | Research purpose, duration, procedures; identify what is experimental | Calls the session "treatment" or "therapy" without saying it is research |
| (a)(2) | Reasonably foreseeable risks and discomforts | Omits challenging experiences, belief and identity change, HPPD, suicidality monitoring, BP/HR rise, next-day headache |
| (a)(3) | Benefits | Overstates benefit; "may help" with no uncertainty |
| (a)(4) | Appropriate alternatives | Omits approved alternatives (e.g., esketamine, ECT, psychotherapy) |
| (a)(5) | **The extent of confidentiality, and that FDA may inspect records** | "Everything is confidential": false |
| (a)(6) | Compensation and treatment for injury (more than minimal risk) | Missing |
| (a)(7) | Contacts for questions, rights, and injury | No contact outside the study team |
| (a)(8) | **Participation is voluntary; refusing or withdrawing costs no benefits** | "You may *ask* to stop" turns a right into a request |

**50.25(b): additional elements, when appropriate:**
- (b)(1) unforeseeable risks, including to an embryo or fetus;
- (b)(2) when the investigator may end participation;
- (b)(3) added costs;
- (b)(4) consequences of withdrawal and orderly termination. *For psychedelics: what stopping means after the drug has been taken.*
- (b)(5) significant new findings;
- (b)(6) approximate number of participants.

**50.25(c):** the ClinicalTrials.gov statement for applicable clinical trials [V].

**45 CFR 46.116** (federally funded research; Common Rule 2018 revision) [M]:
- requires a concise **"key information"** section at the start of the form;
- requires the "reasonable person" standard for information.

## 2. Psychedelic-specific items

1. **Consent to touch.** All of these should hold:
   - Obtained **before** dosing, in writing, as a separate touch agreement.
   - Limited to listed body areas.
   - Touch only when the participant requests it.
   - Consent can be **withdrawn but not expanded** during the session.
   - A simple stop signal.
   - Sexual contact prohibited, in plain words.
   - Safety touch disclosed separately: steadying a fall, or an emergency.

   - How the two consents fit together: touch requires **both** the signed contract made before dosing **and** a request from the participant during the session. A request during the session can only narrow what the contract allows, never add to it. **Source: Colorado 4 CCR 755-1 Rule 6.6(H)(6)** (with 6.6(A) and 6.17(B) for the request requirement). MAPS Code §5 is looser: it contemplates renewed consent during the session. Where the two differ, recommend Colorado's stricter rule and say that it is stricter than MAPS.

   Donors: Colorado 4 CCR 755-1 Rule 6.6 (the operative touch rule) and §1.4 (the definition); Oregon OAR 333-333-5120(7); MAPS Code §5 and §6 (see §5 below).
2. **Staffing.**
   - Who is present, and their license status.
   - At least two monitors for high-dose sessions: Johnson, Richards & Griffiths, *J Psychopharmacol* 2008;22(6):603–620 [V].
   - Whether the participant may request a particular gender or a second person.
   - Medical cover and its response time.
3. **Recording.** All of these should be stated:
   - A separate consent to video and audio.
   - Who views recordings and why, including **independent review for boundary violations**. The lesson of the MAPS MP4 site: footage existed but went unreviewed.
   - Retention period and destruction.
   - Use in training only with separate consent.
   - Whether the participant can withdraw a recording.

   Donors: Oregon OAR 333-333-4640 (the recording rule; see §5 below); Colorado Rules 6.5(H), 6.16(D)(2), and 6.18(G)(2); MAPS Code §3.
4. **Capacity and altered state.**
   - Capacity assessed before dosing.
   - No new decisions or consents during the session.
   - A pre-agreed session plan or advance directive: music, eyeshades, leaving the room, rescue medication, contacting family.
5. **Belief and identity change.** Disclose that some participants report lasting changes in beliefs, values, or sense of self. Offer discussion prompts: Smith & Sisti, "Ethics and ego dissolution," *J Med Ethics* 2021;47:807–814 [V].
   - *Counterpoint to present:* Caporuscio, *J Med Ethics* 2026 (doi 10.1136/jme-2025-111248) [V] argues that lasting change requires sober, agential follow-through. That view lowers the pressure on pre-session consent.
6. **Difficult experiences.** What "help" means: the staff, the protocol, the rescue medication, whether the participant can leave, and the after-care contacts.
7. **Withdrawal.** The right to stop *participation* is unconditional. Explain plainly that the drug's effects cannot be stopped once taken, and what support continues.
8. **Prior psychedelic use and expectancy.** Neutral description of the uncertainty. Avoid recruitment hype, which risks therapeutic misconception.
9. **Post-trial access** (Helsinki 2024) [V]: what happens to responders after the trial.
10. **Mandatory reporting and duty to protect.** Applicable state law; who reports.

## 3. Confidentiality and recording: the legal limits

- **FDA inspection.** FDA may inspect records that identify participants. The form must say so (50.25(a)(5)) [V].
- **Certificates of Confidentiality** (42 U.S.C. 241(d), amended by the 21st Century Cures Act §2012) [V]:
  - Mandatory for federally funded research that collects identifiable, sensitive information; NIH has issued them automatically since 2017-10-01.
  - Non-federally funded studies may apply to FDA (guidance, November 2020; Docket FDA-2019-D-3592).
  - A Certificate bars compelled disclosure, even under subpoena, subject to statutory exceptions.
  - Psychedelic studies involve a Schedule I drug, so Certificate status matters. State it in the form.
- **Psychotherapist privilege.** *Jaffee v. Redmond*, 518 U.S. 1 (1996), recognized a federal psychotherapist-patient privilege covering licensed psychiatrists, psychologists, and licensed social workers in the course of psychotherapy [V: syllabus]. Do not imply that conversations with an **unlicensed facilitator** are privileged.
- **Mandatory reporting and duty to protect.** These vary by state (*Tarasoff* line) [M]. Name the state law, or leave a placeholder for counsel.
- **Record retention** (21 CFR 312.62(c)) [V: FDA warning letters quoting the rule]. Investigators keep records for 2 years after a marketing application is approved for the indication. If no application is filed, or if it is not approved, they keep them for 2 years after the investigation is discontinued and FDA is notified. ICH GCP and sponsor contracts may require longer.
- **Promotion** (21 CFR 312.7(a)) [V: CFR text via GPO and FDLI]:
  - A sponsor or investigator may not represent in a promotional context that an investigational drug is safe or effective for the use under study.
  - The rule expressly does not restrict the full exchange of scientific information, including in scientific or lay media.
  - It applies to recruitment materials and press releases. Whether a particular release crosses the line is a question for FDA regulatory counsel.

## 4. Drafting replacement text

1. **Load `one-way-optimal-language`** if it is listed. Follow its category lexicon:

   | Category | Form |
   |---|---|
   | Duty | *must* |
   | Prohibition | *must not* (patient-facing: *Do not…*) |
   | Permission | *may* |
   | Possibility | *might*, with a frequency |
   | Acknowledgment | *I understand that…*, one fact per line |

   Every clause in active voice.
2. **Insert `[X]` placeholders** for any value the study owns: rescue medication, phone numbers, retention period, state law. List them as open questions. Never invent a clinical value.
3. **Add a source note after each borrowed paragraph:** "Adapted from: [code/rule, section] — [what was changed and why]."
4. **Separate what the form can fix from what the protocol must fix.** Staffing ratios, video review, and Certificate status are protocol decisions. List them separately for the IRB.
5. **Explain the grammar rule behind each wording change.** Blake wants the mechanics.
